### Study 1: Why paying sales managers on the team total backfires — and "star bias" is the reason
**Work ID:** W7224013661 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mechanism stated as established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The mechanism was reallocation, not extra effort: managers shifted coaching and resources from stars toward laggards"
- **Abstract says:** "A scenario-based experiment with sales managers suggests that these effects may stem from the reallocation of resources from high to low performers but provides no evidence of an increase in managers' overall resource investment."
- **Problem:** "The mechanism was" and "managers shifted" assert as fact what the abstract offers as a suggestive scenario-experiment inference, and "not extra effort" converts an absence of evidence into a positive null claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "the evidence points to reallocation rather than extra effort: the scenario experiment suggests managers may shift coaching and resources from stars toward laggards, with no evidence of greater overall investment.""

### Study 4: Learning from foreign partners doesn't speed up product launches — unless your systems can actually talk to theirs
**Work ID:** W7223260194 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Exploratory label extended too far** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Exploratory analysis found an inverted-U pattern: beyond a point, more interoperability investment delivered diminishing returns, and collaborative conflict-resolution capability amplified the productive pathways while weakening the direct one."
- **Abstract says:** "Particularly, exploratory additional findings indicate that digital interoperability exhibits an inverted U-shaped relationship with NPD speed... Collaborative conflict resolution strengthens the foreign knowledge spillover–digital interoperability and digital interoperability–NPD speed relationships but dampens the direct foreign knowledge spillover–NPD speed relationship."
- **Problem:** Only the inverted-U result is described as exploratory in the abstract; the conflict-resolution moderation is part of the tested moderated mediation model, but the digest's single sentence puts both under "exploratory analysis."
- **Suggested fix:** "Split the sentence so the exploratory label applies only to the inverted-U finding."

### Study 5: How you say it matters as much as what you say: 5,183 real sales calls say so

### Study 6: B2B influencers are not mini-celebrities, and contracts that treat them that way backfire
**Work ID:** W7221433026 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Governance mechanisms and readings specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two distinct "role logics" that explain why the same governance mechanism (briefs, approval rules, exclusivity terms) is read as supportive by some influencers and controlling by others"
- **Abstract says:** "We identify two distinct role logics that explain why similar governance mechanisms are interpreted differently and are associated with different collaboration dynamics."
- **Problem:** The specific mechanisms listed and the supportive/controlling contrast are the digest's gloss, not stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the role logics explain why similar governance mechanisms are interpreted differently, without naming specific clauses or interpretations."

**Issue B. "Destroy" overstates "constrained"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Authenticity emerged as an outcome that governance arrangements can either enable or destroy."
- **Abstract says:** "with authenticity emerging as an outcome that can be enabled or constrained by governance arrangements"
- **Problem:** "Destroy" is stronger than the abstract's "constrained."
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "enable or constrain.""

### Study 7: The sales ethics problem isn't lying — it's what gets mentioned last
**Work ID:** W7224954966 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Not only" turned into "less than"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The authors argue the ethical risk lies less in outright deception than in the ordering and withholding of accurate information."
- **Abstract says:** "it contributes to sales ethics by showing that the ethical problem of disclosure does not only lie in explicit deception but also in how information is sequenced or withheld"
- **Problem:** The abstract adds sequencing and withholding to deception; it does not rank sequencing as the larger risk, as the digest (and the headline) imply.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "the ethical risk lies not only in outright deception but also in the ordering and withholding of accurate information.""

**Issue B. Six tensions compressed into four** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "shaped by six competing tensions — between what customers want, what sales managers want, what other internal functions want, and what suppliers upstream allow"
- **Abstract says:** "six tension configurations... two customer-facing interorganizational tensions, two intraorganizational tensions (one vertical and one horizontal), one upstream interorganizational tension and one epistemic tension"
- **Problem:** The gloss lists only four sources and omits the epistemic tension, so the "six" does not map onto the categories given.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either name all five types including the epistemic tension, or drop the enumeration and say "six tension configurations spanning customer-facing, internal and upstream demands plus an epistemic tension.""

### Study 8: The most valuable AI skill in sales may be knowing when to switch it off
**Work ID:** W7221006513 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported claim about trusted salespeople** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the salespeople most trusted by their own managers are the ones who put the AI down and listen"
- **Abstract says:** "the findings highlight the importance of active listening and salesperson authenticity... participants identified knowing when not to use AI as the most critical AI competency"
- **Problem:** The study asked what competencies should be taught; it did not measure which salespeople managers trust most, so this presents an invented empirical relationship.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "sales managers and alumni in this study put active listening, authenticity and knowing when not to use AI at the top of the competency list.""

### Study 9: Older salespeople who feel included stay longer—but feeling good doesn't automatically sell more
**Work ID:** W7222645606 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. PAGO recast as external pressure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while being pushed to look good against targets does nothing for them"
- **Abstract says:** "Its indirect effect on performance becomes significant when LGO is high, whereas PAGO does not strengthen this relationship."
- **Problem:** Performance-approach goal orientation is a salesperson's own motivational orientation, not managerial pressure; the abstract also only says it fails to strengthen the well-being–performance path, not that it "does nothing."
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "while a focus on proving performance against targets did not strengthen the link between well-being and sales results.""
