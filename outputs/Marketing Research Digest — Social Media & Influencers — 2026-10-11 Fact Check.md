### Study 1: On TikTok, a tight-knit audience beats a broad one — and posting more can erode the advantage
**Work ID:** W7221178082 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Engagement definitions not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Bonding capital predicted more contributing engagement (likes, comments, shares) and more creating engagement (followers making their own content in response), while bridging capital predicted less of both."
- **Abstract says:** "Based on these data, the authors measured bonding, bridging, contributing, creating and post frequency and examined the relationships among them. … Bonding social capital is positively related to contributing and creating engagement, whereas bridging social capital is negatively related to both."
- **Problem:** The direction of the relationships is reported correctly, but the parenthetical definitions of what "contributing" and "creating" engagement comprise are not stated in the abstract and are the digest's inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop the parenthetical definitions or mark them as the digest's interpretation (e.g. "engagement the authors label 'contributing' and 'creating'")."

### Study 2: Draw more stages on the progress bar and people replace the product sooner
**Work ID:** W7221366370 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline states behavior, abstract measures intentions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Draw more stages on the progress bar and people replace the product sooner"
- **Abstract says:** "representing a product's lifespan with more (vs. fewer) visually distinct stages increases replacement intentions even when the product's actual usage is the same"
- **Problem:** The headline asserts an actual change in replacement behavior, while the research documents a shift in stated replacement intentions. The body text and caveats get this right, so the gap is confined to the headline.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to, e.g., "Draw more stages on the progress bar and people say they'd replace the product sooner.""

### Study 6: On review pages, "felt great in my hand" beats "good battery life"
**Work ID:** W7221191923 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unmeasured claim about what shoppers say they want** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Shoppers say they want specs, but they reward reviews that tell them what owning the thing feels like"
- **Abstract says:** "Experience-based tags show a stronger association with review helpfulness than attribute-based tags, though this association exhibits diminishing returns as tags accumulate and is stronger for higher- than lower-involvement products."
- **Problem:** The study analyzed archival helpfulness votes; it did not ask shoppers what kind of information they say they want, so the first half of the sentence is unsupported. "Reward" also implies a causal/behavioral reading of an association with helpfulness votes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as, e.g., "Reviews that describe what owning the thing feels like were more likely to be voted helpful than spec-focused ones.""

**Issue B. Helpfulness votes framed as trust** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "shapes which reviews shoppers trust"
- **Abstract says:** "tag's informational orientation, not merely presence or number, shapes review helpfulness"
- **Problem:** The outcome is review helpfulness (votes), which is a proxy for perceived usefulness, not a measure of trust.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "shapes which reviews get voted helpful.""

### Study 9: Chatbots that type word by word win more trust than ones that dump whole lines
**Work ID:** W7219615860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Acceptance recast as trust/belief** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chatbots that type word by word win more trust than ones that dump whole lines"
- **Abstract says:** "This study investigates how the presentation format of generative AI responses—high-dynamism formats (word-by-word) versus low-dynamism formats (line-by-line)—affects users' willingness to accept its output. … users exhibit greater receptivity when generative AI responses are presented in high-dynamism formats."
- **Problem:** The dependent variable is willingness to accept the output, not trust or belief; the consumer angle repeats this as "makes you more likely to believe what you're reading."
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "win more acceptance" / "makes you more likely to go along with what you're reading.""

### Study 10: Tell people how the chicken was raised and the identical meat tastes better
**Work ID:** W7218807019 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Country not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Single country/category; within-subject design means participants compared two samples side by side"
- **Abstract says:** "In a within-subject repeated-measures design, consumers ( n = 657) evaluated two identical chicken meat samples presented with either information about the production system or basic origin information only."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not identify the country or confirm a single-country sample, so the caveat asserts a detail that is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "country of data collection not reported in the abstract" instead of asserting a single country."

**Issue B. Specific information content inferred** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the same chicken genuinely tasted better to people who'd just read about outdoor access and minimal waste"
- **Abstract says:** "characteristics of the novel production system were generally rated as important, with minimal waste and outdoor access, specifically, receiving the highest importance ratings"
- **Problem:** Minimal waste and outdoor access are the system characteristics participants rated most important; the abstract does not state that these were the specific contents of the information shown before tasting.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "people who'd just read about the production system — whose most valued features were outdoor access and minimal waste.""

### Study 11: "Delayed a week" annoys customers more than "delayed seven days"
**Work ID:** W7215115595 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mechanism not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The authors attribute the difference to how unit framing shapes the perceived magnitude of the delay."
- **Abstract says:** "The authors propose that this happens because unit framing can influence company evaluations."
- **Problem:** The abstract offers only a circular process statement and never mentions perceived magnitude of the delay as the mediating mechanism, so the digest supplies an explanation the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the authors attribute the difference to unit framing's effect on company evaluations, and note that the abstract does not specify the underlying process in detail."

**Issue B. Boundary conditions unmentioned** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Three experiments examined how the unit used to describe a service delay changes consumer reactions."
- **Abstract says:** "The authors conduct three experiments testing the proposed hypotheses, process and boundary conditions."
- **Problem:** The abstract states that boundary conditions were tested; the digest presents the effect without noting that limits exist (the abstract does not name them).
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause such as "the authors also test unspecified boundary conditions" or flag in Caveats that the abstract does not disclose when the effect holds."

### Study 14: Why "ugly-cute" sells: the Labubu effect, tested
**Work ID:** W7220433704 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Effect size overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pairing ugliness with cuteness largely cancels that penalty"
- **Abstract says:** "the combination of ugly-cute configurations can attenuate the purchase-intention disadvantage associated with ugliness-related aesthetic deviation"
- **Problem:** "Attenuate" means reduce; "largely cancels" implies the penalty is nearly eliminated, which the abstract does not claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "reduces that penalty" or "takes the edge off that penalty.""

### Study 15: In luxury stores, "cool" sells — but "high status" backfires
**Work ID:** W7218728341 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderation path simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Price-conscious shoppers also got less out of store creativity."
- **Abstract says:** "we found that price consciousness weakens the path from store creativity to the studied outcomes via the extraordinary dimension"
- **Problem:** The moderation applies specifically to the creativity-to-outcomes path running through the extraordinary coolness dimension, not to store creativity in general.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "price consciousness weakened the creativity effect specifically where it ran through the 'extraordinary' dimension of coolness.""

**Issue B. Outcome relabelled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "convert service, display and store creativity into repeat-visit intentions and positive word of mouth"
- **Abstract says:** "linking service quality of employees, store display, and store creativity with customers' behavioral intention and positive word-of-mouth (WOM)"
- **Problem:** The abstract measures "behavioral intention," which the digest narrows to "repeat-visit intentions" without support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "behavioural intention" as the abstract does, or say "intentions toward the store.""

### Study 16: When you're in pain, you follow the crowd
**Work ID:** W7212201010 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Strength of claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Participants experiencing pain were consistently more likely to pick the majority-endorsed option"
- **Abstract says:** "we find evidence that physical pain heightens conformity to majority-endorsed options, even when the majority option is less desirable"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports "evidence" for the pattern; "consistently" implies a uniform result across all four studies, which the abstract does not state.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Across the studies, participants experiencing pain were more likely to pick the majority-endorsed option.""

**Issue B. Advice beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Big or unfamiliar decisions are better postponed until the headache passes."
- **Abstract says:** "Across a pilot field study, one laboratory experiment, and two preregistered online studies, we find evidence that physical pain heightens conformity to majority-endorsed options… The discussion outlines possible mechanisms and leaves their empirical adjudication to future research."
- **Problem:** The studies examined reliance on social proof in choice tasks; they did not test whether delaying decisions produces better outcomes, and the mechanism is explicitly unresolved.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to: "It may be worth noticing that when you're hurting, popularity cues carry more weight than usual.""

### Study 17: "Organic" plus "ancient grain" raised expectations — then the crackers disappointed
**Work ID:** W7215857329 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Dropped segment qualifier** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Combining the organic label with an ancient-wheat claim lifted expectations for liking, healthiness, sustainability, naturalness and novelty above the organic label alone"
- **Abstract says:** "Combined organic and ancient wheat cues generated higher expectations for liking, healthiness, sustainability, naturalness, and novelty than the organic label alone across consumer prioritizing health (TH) and both sustainability and health (attitudes) information."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the expectation lift within specific attitudinal/priority segments, not across all 118 participants; the digest presents it as a general effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add: "among the health-oriented segment (text-highlighting) and the high health-and-sustainability-interest cluster (explicit scales).""

**Issue B. Headline overstates outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** ""Organic" plus "ancient grain" raised expectations — then the crackers disappointed"
- **Abstract says:** "However, no assimilation toward expectations was found for liking, suggesting a misalignment between expectations and product experience."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an absence of assimilation (liking did not rise toward expectations); it does not report that liking fell or that consumers were disappointed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "— but the taste didn't follow" or "— and liking didn't rise to match.""

**Issue C. Country not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In an Italian sensory study, 118 consumers evaluated organic crackers"
- **Abstract says:** "Following a within-subjects design, one hundred and eighteen consumers evaluated the crackers under blind (only tasting), expected (only information…), and informed conditions (tasting+information)."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state where the study was run; the Italian location is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "Italian" or note that the country is inferred from the wheat varieties and not stated in the abstract."

### Study 18: Shoppers won't talk to Alexa about big purchases — until they try it once
**Work ID:** W7220476960 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Platform not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Shoppers won't talk to Alexa about big purchases — until they try it once"
- **Abstract says:** "Voice commerce (VC) is gaining momentum as a new channel and consumer touchpoint, with popular e-retailers adding it as an additional interface option on their websites."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to voice commerce on retailer interfaces generally and never names Alexa or any specific assistant.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "Alexa" with "a voice assistant" or "voice shopping.""

**Issue B. Moderator claim embellished** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Technophilic orientation and regulatory focus moderated how quickly people warmed to the channel."
- **Abstract says:** "Also, individual factors such as regulatory focus and technophilic orientation moderate the behaviour."
- **Problem:** The abstract says these factors moderate behaviour; it says nothing about the speed of adoption or how quickly attitudes formed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "moderated how consumers responded to the channel.""

**Issue C. Inference beyond the diary data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests the barrier is lack of experience rather than a hard ceiling on what people will buy by voice"
- **Abstract says:** "This research explores consumers' buying journey of high-involvement products using VC… Findings reveal that consumer behaviour in VC for high-involvement purchases is a self-reinforcement loop where consumer attitude and trust, arising from personal experiences, modify intentions and behaviour in VC."
- **Problem:** An exploratory 23-person diary study with no control group cannot establish that inexperience, rather than other factors, is the barrier to high-involvement voice purchasing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "this small exploratory study points to experience as one possible route to greater comfort with voice for considered purchases.""
