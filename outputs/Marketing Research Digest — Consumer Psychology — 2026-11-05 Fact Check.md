### Study 1: One "swap and save" nudge can rewire a shopper's habit for 10 trips
**Work ID:** W4406862001 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Duration overstated as permanent** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "a single, cheap interface element can permanently break habitual brand loyalty in a repeat-purchase category"
- **Abstract says:** "shoppers who switched to an alternative product after receiving a prompt were 23.7 percentage points more likely to select that alternative product in subsequent visits, with this effect lasting for up to 10 shopping trips"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an effect bounded at roughly 10 shopping trips; "permanently" contradicts both the abstract and the digest's own caveat about a short horizon.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "permanently break" with "disrupt for a run of about 10 subsequent trips"."

**Issue B. "Months" not supported** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a single prompt is enough to reset the default for months"
- **Abstract says:** "with this effect lasting for up to 10 shopping trips"
- **Problem:** The abstract measures persistence in shopping trips, not calendar time; converting to "months" adds an unsupported time frame.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "for roughly ten subsequent shopping trips" instead of "for months"."

### Study 2: Japanese shoppers trust "made here" far more than they trust awards
**Work ID:** W7225201218 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderation stated too absolutely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it was associated with choice more positively only among consumers who rated the linked place as attractive"
- **Abstract says:** "Award recognition did not function as a uniformly positive signal; its conditional association with choice was more positive at higher levels of perceived Soma City attractiveness."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a continuous conditional association (more positive at higher attractiveness), not a threshold effect present "only" in one group.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "was more positively associated with choice the more attractive consumers rated the linked place"."

**Issue B. "Entirely" overstates dependence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "can depend entirely on whether the consumer has a positive image of the place behind the product"
- **Abstract says:** "its conditional association with choice was more positive at higher levels of perceived Soma City attractiveness"
- **Problem:** "Entirely" implies the award signal has no standalone association, which the abstract does not establish.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "appears to depend partly on whether the consumer has a positive image of the place"."

### Study 6: Clustering a few bad reviews together makes them feel like the majority
**Work ID:** W7224935301 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported magnitude wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "made consumers believe that viewpoint was far more common than it was ... scattering the same reviews among the majority weakened the effect substantially"
- **Abstract says:** "grouping minority reviews into a contiguous run increases their perceived prevalence and in turn shifts product evaluations accordingly, whereas scattered minority reviews have weaker effects"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no effect sizes; "far more common" and "weakened substantially" imply quantified magnitudes that are not reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "more common than it was" and "produced weaker effects" without the intensifiers."

### Study 7: Typing beats talking: text AI recommendations get adopted more than voice

### Study 8: Eye tracking shows bad brand news wins the attention battle — and specific crises leave specific scars
**Work ID:** W7223841933 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped moderator** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Anger and sadness did not mediate the damage, and the hit to the specific legitimacy belief was larger and more persistent than the hit to overall brand evaluation."
- **Abstract says:** "Anger and sadness did not mediate these relationships, while social identification strengthened both pathways."
- **Problem:** The abstract names social identification as a moderator that strengthened both emotional pathways; the digest omits it, losing a condition that matters for who is most affected.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that consumers who identified more strongly with the brand showed stronger emotional pathways."

**Issue B. Sample composition asserted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "these are controlled experiments with student-scale samples"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 uses within-participant eye tracking (n = 117). Study 2 combines eye tracking and survey measures in a randomised experiment (n = 285). Study 3 uses a three-wave longitudinal experiment (n = 279 at T3)."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe participants as students or state their composition; "student-scale" implies a sample type that cannot be verified.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "moderate sample sizes of undisclosed composition"."

**Issue C. "Permanently" overstates persistence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "you may still think the brand is fine overall while permanently doubting that it treats its staff decently"
- **Abstract says:** "Legitimacy threats also produced larger, more persistent changes in the affected legitimacy belief than in overall brand evaluation."
- **Problem:** "More persistent" across three waves is not the same as permanent; the digest's own caveat notes the limited longitudinal horizon.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "permanently" with "more durably" or "still doubting weeks later"."

### Study 10: Lonely shoppers reach for busier packaging
**Work ID:** W7221515577 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderation described as disappearing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The effect was strongest among people low in a dispositional sense of security and faded among those who already felt secure."
- **Abstract says:** "the positive effect of loneliness on the desire for symbolic security is stronger among consumers with lower levels of security trait and weaker among those with higher levels of security trait"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a weaker effect at high security trait, not an absent one; "faded" implies elimination.

### Study 11: The more unequal people think their society is, the more they buy self-improvement
**Work ID:** W7224997542 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies actual purchasing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The more unequal people think their society is, the more they buy self-improvement"
- **Abstract says:** "high (vs. low) perceived economic inequality significantly increases consumers' preferences for self‐improvement (vs. non‐self‐improvement) products ... extends to more direct consumption‐related outcomes, including purchase intention and recommendation likelihood"
- **Problem:** The measured outcomes are stated preferences, purchase intention and recommendation likelihood, not observed buying; the headline reads as behavior. The body and caveats are careful, so this is a headline-only slip.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "...the more they say they want self-improvement products" or "...the stronger their preference for self-improvement purchases"."

### Study 16: People who check receipts, not memory, weather financial shocks better
**Work ID:** W7225263524 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb for an associational finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "using more sources — particularly financial records and receipts rather than memory — produced fewer imprecise and incomplete expense records"
- **Abstract says:** "Employing more sources is associated with higher tracking data quality (fewer imprecise and incomplete records), and consulting financial records and receipts outperforms memory."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an association from cross-sectional survey and diary data; "produced" states causation. The caveat covers the tracking-quality-to-resilience link but not this one.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "produced" to "was associated with"."

**Issue B. Headline chains two associations into one causal claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "People who check receipts, not memory, weather financial shocks better"
- **Abstract says:** "consulting financial records and receipts outperforms memory. We further find that greater precision in recorded amounts is associated with stronger household financial resilience"
- **Problem:** Receipt use is linked to record precision, and precision is separately linked to resilience; the abstract does not test a direct receipts-to-resilience effect, and neither link is causal.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Precise expense records go with better resilience — and precision comes from receipts, not recall"."

### Study 18: Shoppers let their guard down with AI influencers — but only on low-stakes buys
**Work ID:** W7224077478 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline generalizes a conditional effect to shoppers at large** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Shoppers let their guard down with AI influencers — but only on low-stakes buys"
- **Abstract says:** "a dual pathway model in which appearance anxiety drives consumption through a fear of missing out (FOMO)-mediated compensatory route ... and a direct disinhibitory route unique to AI contexts"
- **Problem:** The disinhibition route is modelled as a pathway from appearance anxiety, not a general effect on all shoppers; the headline reads as universal. The body text does specify appearance anxiety.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the precondition, e.g. "Appearance-anxious shoppers let their guard down with AI influencers — but only on low-stakes buys"."
