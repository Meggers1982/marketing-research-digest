### Study 3: Why companies cannot count the emissions hiding in their supply base
**Work ID:** W7224156801 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Share of footprint overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most of a consumer brand's carbon footprint sits with its suppliers"
- **Abstract says:** "However, a substantial portion of emissions occurs beyond these boundaries, and firms continue to struggle to identify, measure, and manage emissions embedded in their supply bases."
- **Problem:** The abstract says a "substantial portion" of emissions lies beyond Scope 1 and 2; the digest asserts a majority, and repeats it as "the far bigger share" in the consumer angle. The abstract does not quantify the share.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "A substantial portion of a consumer brand's carbon footprint sits with its suppliers" and soften "far bigger share" to "the portion buried in its suppliers.""

### Study 5: Green business models scale just as well as the ordinary kind — but only via the same boring levers
**Work ID:** W7224854055 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Failure configurations over-specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "half-committing (green claims without matching public-sector alignment) sits in the failure configurations"
- **Abstract says:** "Third, successfully scaled business models exhibit congruence between environmental ambitions and public alignment."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports congruence among successfully scaled models; it does not describe the content of the two failure configurations, so attributing "green claims without matching public-sector alignment" to them is an inference presented as a finding.

### Study 6: PR agencies are quietly using AI — but in Arabic, humans are still doing the hard part
**Work ID:** W7221812755 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Generalization beyond Arabic** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests the efficiency case for AI in communications weakens sharply outside English"
- **Abstract says:** "Adoption is early-stage and selective ... inhibited by skills gaps, limited Arabic-language tooling and unclear governance ... ethical risks are compounded in Arab settings by cultural–linguistic fit and uneven institutional support."
- **Problem:** The evidence concerns Arabic-language tooling in the Arab region; "outside English" and "weakens sharply" extend the claim to all non-English languages with a magnitude the abstract never states.

### Study 11: Who really built the new golf league's brand? Content creators the league doesn't control
**Work ID:** W7225053690 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Creators framed as displacing the league's own communications** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "They find that external new media content creators, not the league's own communications, did much of the work of legitimating and framing the league's novelty, appeal and competitive credibility."
- **Abstract says:** "The study's findings highlight the role played by external actors in legitimating and framing the league's novelty, appeal, and competition."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a co-creation finding — external actors played a (discrete) role alongside the league and other stakeholders. It does not say external creators did more of this work than the league's own channels, and no comparative weighting is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "External new media content creators played a distinct role in legitimating and framing the league's novelty, appeal and competition, alongside the league's own communications.""

**Issue B. Single case generalized in "Why it matters"** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "For any brand launching something genuinely new, the narrative that sticks is substantially authored by third-party creators, which makes creator relations a core part of launch strategy rather than an add-on to paid media."
- **Abstract says:** "Through a netnographic analysis of league and stakeholder social media content during the league's creation and staging of its first season ... the study investigates actor performativity in brand co-creation."
- **Problem:** An interpretive single-case netnography of one new golf league cannot support a claim about "any brand launching something genuinely new"; the caveats acknowledge this but the framing does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Hedge to: "In this case, much of the legitimating narrative came from third-party creators — a prompt to consider creator relations in launches of unfamiliar categories.""

### Study 12: A century of NFL marketing, read through Black-owned magazines
**Work ID:** W7225126842 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported claim about mainstream press lag** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The historical pattern here suggests campaigns that align activist imagery with commercial interests tend to be recognized as such by the constituencies involved, long before the mainstream press catches on."
- **Abstract says:** "The findings elucidate the branding strategy of NFL entertainment as documented in black-oriented periodicals, which saw black resistance repurposed for white ends by leveraging the political and epistemological meanings of racial struggle to maintain control over historically dynamic processes."
- **Problem:** The abstract documents how Black-oriented periodicals depicted the NFL's strategy, but makes no comparison with mainstream press coverage or its timing; the digest invents a comparative claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "long before the mainstream press catches on" or rephrase to note that Black-oriented periodicals documented these dynamics at the time."

**Issue B. Term attribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The authors argue the league repeatedly deployed what they term "racelighting""
- **Abstract says:** "the authors extend "racelighting" (RL) as a conceptual framework"
- **Problem:** The abstract says the authors extend an existing concept rather than coin it, so "what they term" slightly misattributes the term.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "what the authors, extending an existing concept, call 'racelighting'"."

### Study 13: The "terroir paradox": why famous local food can still be invisible in the nearest big city
**Work ID:** W7223808860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intensified wording on market presence and cooperatives** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Ödemiş products are barely represented in İzmir restaurants and retail. The barriers were not product quality but rising input costs, logistics gaps, distrust of cooperatives"
- **Abstract says:** "Despite strong gastronomic capacity, including GI-registered potatoes and olive oil and a strong dairy sector, Ödemiş remains underrepresented in İzmir. Key barriers include rising input costs, logistics deficits and negative assessments of cooperative effectiveness."
- **Problem:** "Barely represented" is stronger than "underrepresented", "restaurants and retail" is more specific than "İzmir's urban gastronomy market", and "distrust of cooperatives" recasts negative assessments of cooperative effectiveness as distrust.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's wording: "underrepresented in İzmir's urban gastronomy market" and "negative views of how effective cooperatives are"."

**Issue B. Perspective of informants not noted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "interviewing 17 purposively selected stakeholders (producers, cooperatives, traders and institutional actors)"
- **Abstract says:** "This study examines how Ödemiş's rural food production connects with İzmir's urban gastronomy market in western Türkiye, primarily from the perspective of rural and locally embedded food-system actors."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not break the 17 informants into those four categories and explicitly states the vantage point is primarily rural/locally embedded actors — meaning urban buyers' views are largely absent, a relevant limitation when barriers are reported as fact.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "17 purposively selected stakeholders, primarily rural and locally embedded food-system actors" and note in caveats that urban-market actors' perspectives are largely absent."

**Issue C. Single case generalized in consumer angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it explains why the celebrated specialty from the region an hour away is so hard to find in your own city: the bottleneck is usually trucks, middlemen and broken local institutions, not farming"
- **Abstract says:** "The framework extends production-centric understandings of terroir and offers propositions for comparative testing."
- **Problem:** The authors present propositions for comparative testing from one district–city pair; "usually" implies an established general pattern.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "usually" with "in this case" or "can be"."

### Study 14: Students say they care about sustainable eating — but convenience and company decide where they eat
**Work ID:** W7223028679 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Perceived value presented as eating behavior** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Students say they care about sustainable eating — but convenience and company decide where they eat"
- **Abstract says:** "Results indicate that contextual opportunities – such as accessibility and socialization – are the primary drivers of students' perceived values of university canteens, while individual pro-environmental motivation and dietary orientation play a limited role"
- **Problem:** The modeled outcome is perceived value of canteen services, not where students choose to eat; the headline and the consumer angle ("Where you eat is largely decided by what is close, quick and shared with friends") assert a behavioral outcome the study did not measure, contradicting the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "what students value in a university canteen is driven more by access and sociability than by eco-concern" and adjust the consumer angle accordingly."

**Issue B. Nature of the attitude–behavior gap** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It is a concrete illustration of the gap between green values and everyday food choices"
- **Abstract says:** "suggesting a potential gap between pro-environmental intentions and actual perception of the institutional food environment"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a potential gap between intentions and how students perceive the food environment, not between values and observed food choices.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "a potential gap between pro-environmental intentions and how students perceive the institutional food environment"."

**Issue C. Added driver not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Contextual "opportunity" factors — accessibility, convenience and the chance to socialize — were the dominant drivers of perceived value."
- **Abstract says:** "contextual opportunities – such as accessibility and socialization – are the primary drivers"
- **Problem:** "Convenience" is added to the list of named contextual factors; the abstract names only accessibility and socialization.
- **Suggested fix:** "List only accessibility and socialization, or mark convenience as the digest's own gloss."
