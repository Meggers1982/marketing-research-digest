### Study 1: Employees posting on LinkedIn feels like marketing. A Norwegian study suggests it barely moves the needle.
**Work ID:** W7213979081 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. 'Actual' LinkedIn activity** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "used to build an index of actual LinkedIn activity"
- **Abstract says:** "The interview findings allowed for the development of a LinkedIn activity index."
- **Problem:** 'Actual' implies objectively observed posting behavior; the abstract only says an activity index was developed from the interviews and measured within the survey, so it is likely self-reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'actual' — e.g. 'used to build a LinkedIn activity index'."

### Study 4: Customer references can backfire: the hidden downside of name-dropping your best client
**Work ID:** W7212796904 | **Verdict:** ✅ Accurate

**Issue A. Illustrative example not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "for example anchoring prospects to a previous delivery in ways that constrain how a new solution is understood"
- **Abstract says:** "the findings identify six boundary spanning roles of customer referencing: Certification, Anchor, and Bridge (boundary objects)… how the different customer referencing roles carry both functional and dysfunctional attributes in boundary spanning"
- **Problem:** The specific dysfunction described for the Anchor role is a plausible inference but is not stated in the abstract — unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mark as an illustration, e.g. 'for example, an "Anchor" reference could plausibly tie prospects to a previous delivery (the abstract does not detail specific dysfunctions)'."

### Study 6: Does corporate do-gooding actually win business customers? Matched supplier-buyer data says yes — through trust
**Work ID:** W7203828784 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates outcome and causality** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Does corporate do-gooding actually win business customers? Matched supplier-buyer data says yes — through trust"
- **Abstract says:** "Enhanced buyer trust subsequently contributes to stronger relationship performance, demonstrated through greater long-term orientation and relationship extendedness."
- **Problem:** The measured outcome is relationship performance (long-term orientation and relationship extendedness) among existing buyer–supplier dyads, not winning new business customers; and a survey-based SEM model does not establish that CSR 'wins' anything.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe, e.g. 'Supplier CSR is linked to stronger buyer trust — and to buyers' stated intent to stay and expand the relationship'."

### Study 7: Keeping retailers happy only pays off if they're digitally equipped, Vietnamese liquor-channel study finds
**Work ID:** W7203649054 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator framed as a necessary condition** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Keeping retailers happy only pays off if they're digitally equipped, Vietnamese liquor-channel study finds"
- **Abstract says:** "DE positively moderates the RS–RCP link, such that higher DE strengthens the effect of satisfaction on retailer outcomes."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that digital enablement strengthens the satisfaction–performance link, not that the link disappears without it; 'only pays off if' tells readers satisfaction has no payoff in low-digital outlets, which the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Keeping retailers happy pays off more when they're digitally equipped' or 'digital enablement amplifies the payoff'."
