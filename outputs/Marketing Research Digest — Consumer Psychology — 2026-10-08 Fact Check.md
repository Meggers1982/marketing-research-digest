### Study 6: Shoppers trust the logo and the claim for different reasons — and self-reported "green" segments get it wrong
**Work ID:** W7215077900 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Consumer angle reframes the certification shift** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a stronger certification can shift which reason feels more persuasive"
- **Abstract says:** "superior certification shifts dual-motivated consumers' default health preference toward environmental framing, narrowing it to near-parity"
- **Problem:** The abstract's effect is specific to dual-motivated consumers and to a shift toward environmental framing; the digest's generalized phrasing loses the segment boundary.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that among dual-motivated shoppers, stronger certification shifts the default health preference toward environmental framing."

**Issue B. Omitted null result on platform trust** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "analyzed with conditional logit, latent class analysis and trust-stratified models"
- **Abstract says:** "Platform trust does not significantly moderate certification premiums."
- **Problem:** The digest mentions trust-stratified models but never reports the null finding that platform trust did not moderate certification premiums — a practically relevant result for retailers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a sentence noting that platform trust did not significantly moderate certification premiums."

### Study 7: Advertising the joy of missing out: opting-out appeals raised purchase intent via perceived brand authenticity
**Work ID:** W7214744519 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator described as overall effect strength** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The effect was strongest among consumers high in social comparison orientation"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 3 shows social comparison orientation strengthens the effect of JOMO-oriented advertising on brand authenticity, thereby carrying its influence on purchase intention through brand authenticity and the serial pathway."
- **Problem:** The abstract locates the moderation specifically on the ad-to-brand-authenticity path, not on the overall effect; the digest generalizes it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the moderation operated on the path from JOMO advertising to perceived brand authenticity."

**Issue B. Claim about participant source** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants were social media users in a single study program"
- **Abstract says:** "Three between-subjects experiments (n = 625) with social media users"
- **Problem:** The abstract says only that participants were social media users; "a single study program" is not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "in a single study program" or mark it as not reported in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only."

### Study 8: Voice calls are now the least valuable part of your phone plan — and how much least depends on where you live
**Work ID:** W7212065721 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline claims voice is least valuable** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Voice calls are now the least valuable part of your phone plan"
- **Abstract says:** "the value share of voice communication consistently declined from 3G to 5G ... users in the USA and Germany emphasize traditional voice and text services"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a declining value share for voice, not that voice ranks last among the six attributes; in the US and Germany voice is still emphasized, which contradicts the headline's universality.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Voice is a shrinking share of what people value in a phone plan — and how fast it shrinks depends on where you live.""

**Issue B. Attribute naming** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "six categories of mobile service — voice, social networking, information access, mobile finance, mobile shopping and digital content"
- **Abstract says:** "voice communication, social networking, information access, financial services, mobile shopping and digital content"
- **Problem:** Minor paraphrase of "financial services" as "mobile finance"; harmless but slightly narrows the attribute.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's label "financial services.""

### Study 9: Prettier isn't better: in AR shopping, vividness adds nothing, but interactivity and novelty move willingness to pay
**Work ID:** W7219780211 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline links interactivity/novelty directly to WTP** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "vividness adds nothing, but interactivity and novelty move willingness to pay"
- **Abstract says:** "Interactivity and novelty were the most consistent drivers of need satisfaction ... Autonomy predicted willingness to pay"
- **Problem:** Interactivity and novelty drove need satisfaction; only autonomy was linked to willingness to pay. The headline compresses a mediated chain into a direct effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "interactivity and novelty drive the psychological needs that feed price premiums.""

**Issue B. Product uncertainty moderator partly dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "check whether high product uncertainty blunts the autonomy route in your category"
- **Abstract says:** "product uncertainty weakens the autonomy and relatedness pathways but not the competence pathway"
- **Problem:** The digest names only the autonomy pathway; the abstract reports uncertainty also weakens relatedness, and notably not competence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Mention that product uncertainty weakens both autonomy and relatedness pathways but not competence."

**Issue C. Quality finding omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "modeling how four AR attributes — interactivity, vividness, novelty and quality — relate to satisfaction of three psychological needs"
- **Abstract says:** "quality was associated with autonomy alone, and only marginally so"
- **Problem:** The digest lists quality as an attribute but never reports its weak result, leaving readers to infer it behaved like interactivity and novelty.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that quality related only marginally to autonomy."

### Study 11: The standard model of healthy eating was built on mostly female samples — it mostly, but not entirely, holds for men
**Work ID:** W7214384564 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Relative importance of predictors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "What is in your kitchen and what you see other people eating appear to shape your diet more than your beliefs about nutrition do"
- **Abstract says:** "capability and opportunity were associated with behaviour indirectly through motivation, which was the strongest predictor of diet quality"
- **Problem:** The abstract identifies motivation (which includes intentions, self-efficacy, affective attitudes and perceived behavioural control) as the strongest predictor, with opportunity indicators such as the home food environment and descriptive norms operating indirectly through it. The digest's ranking of environment and norms above beliefs is not what the model reported, even though instrumental attitudes did drop out at the indicator level. The same tilt appears in 'where habit, home environment and what peers are seen eating did the explanatory work'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to: 'motivation remained the strongest predictor, but the specific levers that held up for men were habit, the home food environment and descriptive norms rather than instrumental, information-based attitudes.'"

### Study 12: Why people go out at night: safety and transport move the money, and FOMO amplifies everything
**Work ID:** W7203639635 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline singles out two of four factors** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "safety and transport move the money"
- **Abstract says:** "The results confirm four key decision-making factors: safety, transport, quality and social, which have a significant impact on NTE usage and expenditure."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports all four factors as significant and gives no ranking, so elevating safety and transport over venue quality and social draw is not supported by the abstract. The same emphasis recurs in 'lighting, policing and transport schedules may do more for takings than another drinks promotion'.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change the headline to reference all four confirmed factors, e.g. 'safety, transport, venue quality and the social draw all move the money'."

**Issue B. FoMO described as raising spend** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "And if you're prone to fear of missing out, all of those considerations get louder — and your night-out budget tends to be bigger."
- **Abstract says:** "these relationships are stronger for individuals who report higher levels of FoMO"
- **Problem:** FoMO is reported only as a moderator that strengthens the links between decision factors and usage/expenditure; the abstract does not report a direct main effect of FoMO on how much people spend.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that for high-FoMO respondents the link between these considerations and spending was stronger, rather than that their budgets are bigger."

### Study 13: In free-to-play games, beauty sells and performance doesn't
**Work ID:** W7212753203 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Price utility relabelled as price fairness** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "price fairness was the only functional dimension that mattered"
- **Abstract says:** "Functional dimensions of quality and competency showed no significant effects, while price utility emerged as the only functional predictor."
- **Problem:** The measured construct is price utility; perceived fairness appears only in the authors' practical recommendations, not as the tested variable.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'price utility (perceived value for money)' when describing the finding and reserve 'fairness' for the authors' recommendation."

**Issue B. Gaming-intensity moderator omitted from the study description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tested which types of perceived value drive attitudes toward virtual characters and intention to buy them"
- **Abstract says:** "Gaming intensity was included as a moderating variable... Gaming intensity moderated the effects of price utility and self-image representation among high-intensity players."
- **Problem:** A moderator the abstract names and reports as significant is absent from the study summary; it surfaces only obliquely in a story angle.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting that gaming intensity moderated the price-utility and self-image effects among high-intensity players."

**Issue C. 'Whales' and 'sells' overstate the outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "transparent, fair pricing still matters even to the most engaged whales"
- **Abstract says:** "theory-testing survey design... Structural equation modeling (SEM) was used to test hypothesized relationships among consumption value dimensions, attitude, and purchase intention"
- **Problem:** The outcome is stated purchase intention from a cross-sectional survey, not revenue, and the abstract describes 'high-intensity players', not high-spending whales.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace 'whales' with 'high-intensity players' and frame the headline around purchase intention rather than sales."

### Study 16: "Pick what you want" and "reject what you don't" are not the same shopping task — your brain treats them differently
**Work ID:** W7128495320 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Neuro framing in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "your brain treats them differently"
- **Abstract says:** ""Take" decisions required longer processing times in both image viewing and evaluation stages than "Leave" decisions, whereas the "Wait" decision elicited the longest processing times"
- **Problem:** The reported measures are behavioural processing times in a sequential task, not brain data; the headline implies neuroimaging or physiological measurement that the abstract does not describe.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'they take different amounts of mental effort' or 'decision times differ'."

**Issue B. Absolute wording on sequence effects and variability** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "produced far more variability in deferral behavior — with none of the steady adaptation across the shopping sequence seen in positive-selection tasks"
- **Abstract says:** "higher between-participant variability for "Wait" decisions compared to Positive-selection tasks... such temporal modulation was largely absent under Negative-selection"
- **Problem:** 'Far more' and 'none of' harden the abstract's 'higher' and 'largely absent' into absolutes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'higher between-participant variability' and 'with the across-sequence adaptation largely absent'."

### Study 17: A free, street-level map of how England and Wales shops
**Work ID:** W7203765190 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Street-level' overstates resolution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A free, street-level map of how England and Wales shops"
- **Abstract says:** "Spatial microsimulation is used to create synthetic populations at the Lower Super Output Area level"
- **Problem:** LSOAs are small-area neighbourhood units of roughly 1,500 residents, not streets; the abstract consistently calls this a 'small-area' classification.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'a free, neighbourhood-level map' or 'small-area map'."

**Issue B. Characterisation of commercial products** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Commercial geodemographic segmentations are expensive black boxes"
- **Abstract says:** "Existing frameworks overlook localised variations and fail to integrate consumer surveys."
- **Problem:** The abstract's stated gap is localised variation and the absence of consumer-survey integration; it makes no claim about cost or opacity of commercial products.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the gap as the abstract states it, e.g. 'existing frameworks miss local variation and do not integrate consumer surveys'."

### Study 18: When worldly taste backfires: Mexican consumers mock the globe-trotting elite online
**Work ID:** W7214717190 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Brand spillover claim not measured** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it can read as class display and attract organized online ridicule that drags the brand along with the consumer"
- **Abstract says:** "the intensity of symbolic boundary contestation (mocking and criticizing)... claims to cosmopolitan status are publicly evaluated and challenged in emerging-market contexts marked by historical inequalities"
- **Problem:** The abstract documents contestation of individuals' cosmopolitan performances; nothing in it measures or reports consequences for brands, and the digest's own caveat says brand outcomes were not measured.
- **Suggested fix:** "Hedge to 'which may expose brands associated with that imagery to the same ridicule, though brand effects were not measured'."

**Issue B. 'Stripped away' phrasing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Cosmopolitan consumption emerged as both a status resource and a liability that can be stripped away in public"
- **Abstract says:** "cosmopolitanism serves as both a resource for distinction and a contested identity within digitally mediated fields"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a contested identity, not one that is demonstrably stripped away; the digest's phrasing implies a stronger, documented outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'both a resource for distinction and an identity that is publicly contested'."
