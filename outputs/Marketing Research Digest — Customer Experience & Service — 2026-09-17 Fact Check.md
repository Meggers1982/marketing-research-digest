### Study 1: Customers at a fork in the road are the ones worth marketing to
**Work ID:** W7211878886 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

### Study 4: Customers who crack a joke get better service
**Work ID:** W7212527755 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested comparison with complaining** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "being playful rather than transactional with a service worker appears to buy you more discretionary effort than complaining does"
- **Abstract says:** "Results across the two studies show that customer humor is positively related to employees' PCSP... through CEX"
- **Problem:** The studies compared levels of customer humor, not humor versus complaining; the abstract reports no complaining condition, so the comparative claim has no evidential basis.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the comparison: "customers who bring a bit of humor to an interaction were linked to more proactive effort from staff.""

**Issue B. Both studies located in China** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two studies in China examined what happens when the customer, not the employee, brings the humor."
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 employed an online scenario-based experiment (N = 146). Study 2 used a three-wave, multi-source survey of frontline hospitality employees in China (N = 340)."
- **Problem:** Only Study 2 is identified as Chinese in the abstract; the location of the Study 1 online sample is not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "two studies, the second conducted with frontline hospitality employees in China.""

**Issue C. Source of humor ratings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "customer humor as reported by employees"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 2 used a three-wave, multi-source survey of frontline hospitality employees in China (N = 340)."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes the survey as multi-source without specifying who rated customer humor, so attributing it to employee report is unverifiable and may contradict the multi-source design.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Study 2 is a multi-wave, multi-source survey; the abstract does not specify who rated customer humor.""

### Study 6: Your AI complaint agent only works if it's allowed to issue the refund
**Work ID:** W7203673228 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported claim about current retailer practice** — Severity: Minor

- **Abstract says:** "Retailers increasingly deploy generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) to handle complaints, yet evidence remains limited on when these agents restore purchasing rather than merely improve immediate evaluations."

### Study 10: Salespeople who want to look good deliver warmer service — and weaker technical quality
**Work ID:** W7204989107 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline drops boundary condition and uses causal verb** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Salespeople who want to look good deliver warmer service — and weaker technical quality"
- **Abstract says:** "PPGO is positively associated with both technical and functional quality but may exhibit a notable trade-off in service contexts, enhancing functional quality while weakening technical quality."
- **Problem:** The trade-off is reported as contingent on service (vs goods) contexts and as an association from cross-sectional dyadic data; "deliver" implies a general causal effect. The body restores the boundary, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "In service industries, salespeople focused on proving performance are rated warmer — but weaker on technical quality.""

**Issue B. Overall PPGO effect omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Employees driven to prove their performance scored higher on functional quality (how the service feels) but, in service industries specifically, showed a trade-off with weaker technical quality"
- **Abstract says:** "PPGO is positively associated with both technical and functional quality but may exhibit a notable trade-off in service contexts"
- **Problem:** The digest omits that performance-prove orientation was positively associated with technical quality overall, which makes the trade-off look like a general deficit rather than a context-specific reversal.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that performance-prove orientation was positively associated with both quality dimensions overall, with the trade-off appearing in service contexts."

### Study 11: When robots fail badly, giving them a human face backfires
**Work ID:** W7203488123 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Setting stated more narrowly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across three experiments in hospitality service settings, researchers tested how anthropomorphic design of AI service robots"
- **Abstract says:** "Drawing on three experimental studies in service contexts, this research examines how service robot anthropomorphism influences customers' continued usage intentions (CUI) after service failures."
- **Problem:** The abstract says the experiments were run in "service contexts" generally; hospitality is only mentioned as the background trend motivating the work, not as the tested setting.

**Issue B. Unverified operationalization of anthropomorphism** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "anthropomorphic design of AI service robots (human-like faces, names, voices)"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract refers only to "anthropomorphic design" and "anthropomorphic cues" without specifying faces, names or voices."
- **Problem:** The specific cues listed cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the parenthetical or mark it as illustrative: "anthropomorphic design (human-like cues)"."

### Study 13: In VR retail displays, showing the product in use beats letting shoppers imagine it
**Work ID:** W7169838255 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated consistency of effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Contextual VR displays consistently produced stronger experiential responses across jeans, perfume, local-brand and foreign-brand conditions"
- **Abstract says:** "the findings show that contextual VR displays generally produce stronger experiential responses than imaginative displays across jeans, perfume, local‐brand, and foreign‐brand conditions"
- **Problem:** "Consistently" implies the effect held in every condition; the abstract says "generally", leaving room for exceptions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace "consistently" with "generally"."

**Issue B. Mediation presented as unconditional** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with evaluation and intensification sequentially mediating the effect on experience extension"
- **Abstract says:** "The results further show that display type is associated with experience extension directly in some contexts and indirectly through experience evaluation and experience intensification."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a mixed pattern (direct in some contexts, indirect in others); the digest reports the sequential mediation as a uniform result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "with the effect on experience extension running directly in some contexts and indirectly through evaluation and intensification in others"."

### Study 21: Who gets blamed when a gig worker misbehaves? It depends on the customer's politics
**Work ID:** W7202074508 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mechanism specificity dropped for the messaging effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Critically, a message clearly emphasizing gig workers' independent contractor status reduced liberals' tendency to blame the platform and raised their intention to keep using the service."
- **Abstract says:** "providing a clear message emphasizing gig workers' independent status reduces liberals' tendency to blame organizations, given their weaker beliefs in personal responsibility, thereby increasing intentions to continue using the service"
- **Problem:** The abstract conditions the message effect on liberals' weaker beliefs in personal responsibility; the digest presents it as an unconditional effect and also adds "contractor" to "independent status," a slight embellishment.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the message worked through (was conditioned on) liberals' weaker beliefs in personal responsibility, and use the abstract's phrase "independent status.""

**Issue B. Study design details not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Experimental studies relying on stated intentions and hypothetical transgressions rather than real incidents"
- **Abstract says:** "Across three studies, consumers with a liberal (vs. conservative) political ideology exhibit differential attribution patterns"
- **Problem:** The abstract says only "three studies" and does not specify that they were experiments with hypothetical transgressions. Unverifiable from abstract only — though the caveat is appropriately cautious.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "three studies (design details not specified in the abstract); outcomes appear to be stated intentions.""

**Issue C. US-specific inference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "political ideology findings are likely US-specific"
- **Abstract says:** "(no country or sample information given)"
- **Problem:** The abstract names no country, so the US framing is an inference rather than a stated fact.
- **Suggested fix:** "Phrase as "country not reported in the abstract; liberal/conservative framing suggests a US context, which would limit generalization.""
