### Study 4: A less human chatbot may be exactly what stigmatized customers want
**Work ID:** W7222997881 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Barrier framing overstated as exclusive** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reframes the real barrier to many services as behavioral friction rather than price or availability"
- **Abstract says:** "This paradox suggests that the primary barrier to utilization is not only structural supply failure but also behavioral friction at the mental health service entry threshold."
- **Problem:** The abstract frames behavioral friction as an additional barrier alongside structural supply failure, not a replacement for it; "rather than price or availability" makes it an either/or.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "reframes a major barrier as behavioural friction in addition to price and availability"."

**Issue B. "Opposite of" vs "qualifies" existing assumption** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the opposite of the prevailing assumption in service technology research"
- **Abstract says:** "we theorize an inversion of intimacy, the proposition that lower ASP facilitates sensitive disclosure in stigma-laden contexts, which qualifies a central assumption in service technology research"
- **Problem:** The authors say the proposition qualifies a central assumption in a specific context; the digest escalates this to a flat reversal of the field's view.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "which qualifies a central assumption in service technology research"."

### Study 5: Consumers Will Hand the Wheel to AI — But Only If They Can Grab It Back
**Work ID:** W7223130166 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interaction described as an acceptability threshold** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When the technology felt threatening, high autonomy was only acceptable — perceived as lower risk — if users kept meaningful control"
- **Abstract says:** "Under high perceived technological threat, AI autonomy and user control interact to shape perceived product risk, with greater autonomy associated with lower risk when user control is high, which in turn influences consumer responses."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an interaction on perceived risk, not that autonomy was unacceptable without control; "only acceptable if" implies a tested threshold the abstract does not state.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "greater autonomy was associated with lower perceived risk only when users retained high control"."

### Study 6: Livestream Shopping Doesn't Travel: What Works on Taobao Falls Flat in the West
**Work ID:** W7222464186 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts performance outcomes and names an unmentioned platform** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Livestream Shopping Doesn't Travel: What Works on Taobao Falls Flat in the West"
- **Abstract says:** "This qualitative inquiry employs screencast videography of 33 LSS sessions from 12 platforms to explore how they enact affordances in real time... arguing that cross-market transferability of LSS strategies depends on these configurations within different platform ecosystems."
- **Problem:** The study coded platform affordances, not sales or engagement outcomes, so "works"/"falls flat" implies performance evidence that does not exist; Taobao is not named in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe to something like "Livestream shopping formats may not transfer: China and Western platforms enable different engagement mechanics"."

**Issue B. Consumer experience claim beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Why live shopping feels addictive in China and awkward in the US isn't about consumer taste alone"
- **Abstract says:** "The findings reveal systemic and episodic-relational logics associated with distinct viewing experiences."
- **Problem:** No consumer responses were measured; characterizing live shopping as "addictive" in one market and "awkward" in another goes beyond the researchers' interpretive coding of platform sessions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the study describes distinct viewing experiences enabled by different platform logics, without asserting how consumers in each market feel."

### Study 7: Disclosure Warnings on AI Therapy Bots May Boost Use Rather Than Protect Users
**Work ID:** W7223088624 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

- **As written:** "perceived adequacy of disclosures strengthened rather than tempered the path to use"
- **Abstract says:** "perceived disclosure adequacy moderates the relationship between ability and use intentions"

### Study 8: AI Patents Don't Create Value — Marketing Execution Does
**Work ID:** W7222898245 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for observational panel associations** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "AI Patents Don't Create Value — Marketing Execution Does"
- **Abstract says:** "AI robotics capability (AI-RC) does not exhibit an automatic direct association with CVR and BV; rather, its value potential is realized when firms can effectively execute, coordinate and deliver AI-enabled offerings through MEC"
- **Problem:** The abstract consistently uses associational language; the headline states causation, and "puts hard panel evidence behind" adds weight the design cannot carry, even though the caveats acknowledge the limit.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "AI patents alone aren't linked to customer or brand value — marketing execution is the pathway"."

### Study 9: The More Capable People Feel with AI, the More Willing They Are to Misuse It

### Study 12: Chatbots keep customers in the ecosystem — but only if they make people feel sure of their decision
**Work ID:** W7221013951 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for survey data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chatbots keep customers in the ecosystem — but only if they make people feel sure of their decision"
- **Abstract says:** "chatbot service quality is positively associated with customers' share of time with the chatbot and omnichannel ecosystem lock-in in both countries. These relationships are mediated by decision confidence"
- **Problem:** The headline asserts a causal, conditional effect, while the study reports associations from cross-sectional survey data; the digest's own caveat says causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Chatbot quality tracks with ecosystem lock-in — and decision confidence looks like the link"."

**Issue B. Caveat implies an unnamed industry partner** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sample sizes near the lower bound for multi-group comparison; industry partner not named"
- **Abstract says:** "we analyze survey data from the United States ( N = 345) and Saudi Arabia ( N = 302)"
- **Problem:** The abstract mentions no industry partner or sponsor; stating that one is "not named" implies commercial involvement that the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Delete the "industry partner not named" clause or replace with "no funding or partner information in the abstract"."

### Study 14: An AI web crawler found five times more nicotine pouch brands than Nielsen or national surveys
**Work ID:** W7223205040 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline multiple overstated for Nielsen** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "An AI web crawler found five times more nicotine pouch brands than Nielsen or national surveys"
- **Abstract says:** "ONPWebScout alone captured 86 brands (40.95%), compared with 16 (7.62%) from NYTS and 25 (11.90%) from Nielsen."
- **Problem:** 86 vs. 25 is roughly 3.4 times, not five times; the five-fold multiple applies only to the youth survey comparison, so the headline overstates the gap against scanner data.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "found three times more brands than Nielsen and five times more than the national youth survey"."

**Issue B. Cost claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "An LLM-plus-search-API crawler is cheap enough to run as a standing early-warning system"
- **Abstract says:** "we developed an automated tool 'ONPWebScout', leveraging large language models and the Google Search Application Programming Interface to track emerging brands"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no cost or resource data for the tool, so the affordability claim is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "an automated LLM-plus-search-API approach, which the authors describe as feasible for ongoing monitoring"."

### Study 15: Labeling a souvenir 'AI-generated' hurt sales intent with Americans — but Chinese shoppers didn't blink
**Work ID:** W7223005232 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation nuance dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Perceived effort and perceived authenticity both mediated the drop."
- **Abstract says:** "Perceived effort effects were significant for both comparisons with the unlabeled condition across three examined products, whereas the human–AI co‐created versus unlabeled authenticity effect was nonsignificant in Study 1 but significant in Study 2. Nationality moderated some, but not all, indirect effects."
- **Problem:** The authenticity pathway was inconsistent across studies and nationality moderated only some indirect effects; presenting both mediators as uniformly operating overstates the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that perceived effort mediated consistently while the authenticity pathway held only in Study 2, and that nationality moderated some but not all indirect effects."

### Study 16: When AI sets the price, $19.99 backfires and $20 works better
**Work ID:** W7222544706 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Human-seller baseline asserted as finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the classic just-below tactic that works for human sellers backfired when attributed to an algorithm"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 provides directional evidence that consumers respond less favorably to AI‐set prices and supports a preference for round over just‐below prices when pricing is attributed to AI. Study 2 supports the less favorable response to AI pricing and provides directional evidence for the predicted price‐format effect."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a preference for round over just-below prices under AI attribution but does not report that just-below prices worked better in the human-seller condition, so the contrast is inferred rather than evidenced.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that participants preferred round over just-below prices when the price was attributed to AI, without asserting the opposite result for human sellers."

### Study 18: Who's actually buying matters: older shoppers and their adult children respond to different product images
**Work ID:** W7221777092 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Nationality not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A 2x3 between-subjects experiment with 330 Chinese participants"
- **Abstract says:** "A 2 × 3 between‐subjects experiment involved 330 participants: 165 older self‐buyers aged 60–75 and 165 adult‐child proxy buyers aged 40–59."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no country for the sample, so identifying participants as Chinese (and the "single country" caveat) cannot be verified from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "Chinese" or note that the sample's country is not specified in the abstract."

**Issue B. Descriptive pattern reported as a result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "older self-buyers responded best to plain home-context imagery, while adult children buying on their behalf responded best to images showing an older user"
- **Abstract says:** "Purchase intention showed a significant segment × visual format interaction. Descriptively, mean purchase intention was highest under home‐context presentation among older self‐buyers and under the older‐user use scene in a home context among adult‐child proxy buyers."
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly labels the direction of the segment-specific pattern as descriptive; presenting it as the finding implies tested simple effects.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the interaction was significant but the direction of the segment-specific advantage is reported descriptively by the authors."
