### Study 1: What 1,144 real support calls reveal: unhappy customers say it outright, happy ones only sound it
**Work ID:** W7224826657 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported claim about call distribution** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "reserve voice-based models for the ambiguous middle, where most calls actually sit"
- **Abstract says:** "The authors analyze 1,144 real-world service calls, each coded as dissatisfied, neutral, or satisfied"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no breakdown of how many calls fell into each satisfaction category, so the claim that most calls are neutral/satisfied cannot be verified.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "where most calls actually sit" or replace with "for neutral and satisfied calls, where vocal cues add predictive value"."

**Issue B. Predictive modeling framed as live scoring** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this shows satisfaction can be scored live"
- **Abstract says:** "Technology-mediated service interactions, such as phone calls, generate rich behavioral signals that firms currently struggle to monitor in real time... The authors analyze 1,144 real-world service calls... using text-only, audio-only, and multimodal deep learning models."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes retrospective modeling of recorded, pre-coded calls; real-time monitoring is the motivation, not a demonstrated capability.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "this suggests satisfaction signals present in calls could in principle be scored during the interaction"."

### Study 2: When an AI handles a B2B complaint, buyers judge the warmth, not just the bot
**Work ID:** W7221491089 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Ranking not established by abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "whether the agent appears warm, acknowledges emotion, and is seen as fair matters more than what it is"
- **Abstract says:** "Industrial customers' responses to AI-enabled recovery are not determined solely by agent type."
- **Problem:** The abstract states that agent type is not the sole determinant; it does not report a comparison showing relational cues outweigh agent type.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "agent type is not the sole determinant — warmth, emotional acknowledgment and perceived fairness also shape responses"."

### Study 4: In virtual stores, flash beats substance — but only for the first few minutes
**Work ID:** W7225124455 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderation overstated as a null effect at short exposure** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "informativeness, co-presence and trialability only gained traction with longer exposure"
- **Abstract says:** "(1) six scene features, i.e. Visual appeal, Navigability, Informativeness, Novelty, Embodied co-presence and Trialability, significantly enhance purchase intention, pleasure and loyalty; ... (3) the immersive time positively moderates the impact of Informativeness, Embodied Co-presence and Trialability on the initial inspired-by stage."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports all six features had significant effects, with immersive time strengthening three of them on the inspired-by stage only. "Only gained traction" implies those three features did nothing at short exposure, which the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "longer immersion strengthened the effect of informativeness, co-presence and trialability on the first (inspired-by) stage"."

**Issue B. Invented time threshold** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "what grabs you in a virtual store in the first thirty seconds — the look, the newness"
- **Abstract says:** "the immersive time positively moderates the impact of Informativeness, Embodied Co-presence and Trialability on the initial inspired-by stage"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no duration thresholds; "thirty seconds" (and the headline's "first few minutes") attaches false precision to a moderator reported only as longer vs shorter immersive time.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with a non-numeric phrase such as "what grabs you on first contact"."

### Study 5: Trust isn't the universal driver of digital banking loyalty that banks assume
**Work ID:** W7225168433 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intention described as use** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Performance expectancy — simply believing the app gets the job done — was the strongest predictor of continued use"
- **Abstract says:** "Performance expectancy was the strongest direct predictor of sustained mobile banking acceptance intention (SAI)"
- **Problem:** The measured outcome is sustained acceptance intention, not observed continued usage; the body text drops the distinction the caveats later make.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "the strongest predictor of intention to keep using the app"."

**Issue B. Over-generalization beyond two markets** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most people keep using a banking app because it works, not because they have warm feelings about the institution."
- **Abstract says:** "This study examines how functional beliefs, platform quality and trust sustain mobile banking acceptance across Malaysia and Indonesia. ... Survey data from 940 existing users"
- **Problem:** The sample is existing mobile banking users in two Southeast Asian countries; "most people" (and "the stable foundation everywhere" in Why it matters) extends the result to consumers generally.
- **Suggested fix:** "Qualify as "among the Malaysian and Indonesian users surveyed, continued use tracked usefulness more than trust"."

### Study 6: Why "green" transit customers care more about how services connect than about the environment
**Work ID:** W7225757846 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested ranking of connectivity over environmental motivation** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It suggests sustainability-marketing for services succeeds less through environmental appeals and more through seamless integration into customers' everyday logistics"
- **Abstract says:** "the integration of sustainable mobility services within customers' ecosystems is driven by the perceived intra- and interconnectivity of the provider's mobility services, the perceived value those services contribute to customers' sustainability, customers' multimodal integration behaviour, and the perceived centrality of the provider"
- **Problem:** Perceived sustainability value is itself one of the significant drivers; the abstract never pits environmental appeals against connectivity or ranks them, so the headline, Why it matters and the "convenience, not conviction" line assert a contrast the study did not test.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "connectivity, perceived sustainability value, multimodal behaviour and provider centrality all predicted use" and drop the claim that environmental appeals matter less."

**Issue B. Dropped half of the connectivity construct** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the perceived interconnectivity between the provider's services"
- **Abstract says:** "the perceived intra- and interconnectivity of the provider's mobility services"
- **Problem:** The abstract names intra- and interconnectivity; the digest reports only interconnectivity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "perceived intra- and interconnectivity of the provider's services"."

### Study 7: A VR headset after the trip beats a phone screen at turning visitors into recommenders
**Work ID:** W7224128805 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb for an associational result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Post-visit VR content produced significantly stronger recommendation intentions than the smartphone version"
- **Abstract says:** "Results show that VR-mediated post-visit communication is associated with a stronger intention to recommend the destination than smartphone-based media"
- **Problem:** The authors describe the result as an association despite the between-subject design; "produced" asserts more than the abstract claims.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was associated with significantly stronger recommendation intentions"."

### Study 10: Send your executives to sit in the waiting room: ethnography inside a medical clinic as a CX diagnostic
**Work ID:** W7213312638 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested comparison with survey metrics** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this shows how immersive observation can catch friction that metrics never register"
- **Abstract says:** "this research demonstrates how design ethnography can diagnose best practices and opportunities for CXM improvement"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports no head-to-head comparison with NPS, CSAT or other metric-based programs, so the claim that observation catches what metrics miss is not evidenced.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "shows how immersive observation can surface friction points that a dashboard may not capture"."

**Issue B. Headline implies a managerial practice the study did not test** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Send your executives to sit in the waiting room"
- **Abstract says:** "Design ethnography is used at a family medicine clinic... Academic and practitioner researchers are offered a five-step guide for using design ethnography to (re)design services"
- **Problem:** The fieldwork was conducted by researchers and the guide is addressed to researchers/practitioners; the abstract does not evaluate executives immersing themselves in the service.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe the headline around researcher-led observational immersion rather than sending executives."

### Study 11: Green credentials buy forgiveness, not a second booking
**Work ID:** W7225114666 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Survey measure described as actual behavior** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "But that goodwill did not extend to actually using the service again."
- **Abstract says:** "it does not extend the same leeway in terms of service re-patronage" — measured "through surveys distributed to 804 digitally engaged sharing economy consumers using Partial-Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling"
- **Problem:** The word "actually" implies observed repeat usage, but re-patronage here is a self-reported survey construct. The digest's own caveat says "self-reported intentions rather than observed rebooking," so the body contradicts the caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "But that goodwill did not carry over to self-reported intentions to use the service again.""

**Issue B. Temporal framing dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Sustainability positioning appears to earn moral credit that shows up in advocacy metrics while leaving repeat purchase untouched"
- **Abstract says:** "sustainability signaling, though morally rewarded in the long-term through citizenship connoting judgment assimilation may not necessarily transform into a short-term financial reward through re-patronage"
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the result as a long-term versus short-term "temporal dichotomy"; the digest presents it as a flat, permanent gap between advocacy and repurchase.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the authors' framing: the citizenship payoff is described as long-term while re-patronage is the short-term financial reward that does not follow."

**Issue C. Citizenship behaviors specified beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "customers rewarded it with citizenship behaviors — defending it, recommending it, giving feedback"
- **Abstract says:** "consumers demonstrate desirable citizenship towards the firm when it is perceived to be a high green brand equity firm that delivers fair service recovery"
- **Problem:** The three named behaviors are a standard textbook definition of customer citizenship but are not itemized in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "citizenship behaviors (advocacy, helping and feedback are typical components)" or drop the itemization."

### Study 12: Cafés sell aspiration, but customers bond with brands that match who they already are
**Work ID:** W7223831545 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Significance of specific congruence paths not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Lifestyle congruence and brand identification mattered; "ideal self-congruence" — the brand matching who customers wish they were — did not register a significant effect."
- **Abstract says:** "obtained findings from 351 café consumers in China clarified the collective influences of operational excellence and identity alignment on satisfaction. The insignificant influence of ideal self-congruence specifies matching brand identity through actual self-congruence."
- **Problem:** The abstract only reports the insignificant ideal self-congruence path and a general "collective influence" of identity alignment; it does not separately confirm that lifestyle congruence and brand identification were each significant. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "identity alignment overall contributed to satisfaction, while ideal self-congruence did not register a significant effect.""

**Issue B. Direction of moderation asserted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Social media marketing activity strengthened the link between satisfaction and loyalty."
- **Abstract says:** "with SMMA moderates the relationship between satisfaction and brand loyalty"
- **Problem:** The abstract states only that SMMA moderates the relationship; it does not explicitly give the direction, although the managerial recommendation implies a positive one.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Social media marketing activity moderated the link between satisfaction and loyalty (the authors recommend stronger social engagement to lift loyalty).""

**Issue C. Satisfaction drivers framed as loyalty drivers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the data points toward actual-self fit and everyday operational quality as the loyalty drivers"
- **Abstract says:** "the collective influences of operational excellence and identity alignment on satisfaction. ... Satisfaction then influences brand loyalty through brand trust and brand love"
- **Problem:** In the model these stimuli predict satisfaction, which reaches loyalty only indirectly through brand trust and brand love; calling them "the loyalty drivers" skips the mediation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "drivers of satisfaction, which in turn feeds loyalty through brand trust and brand love.""
