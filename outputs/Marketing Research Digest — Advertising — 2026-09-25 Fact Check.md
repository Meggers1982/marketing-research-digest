### Study 1: Coke's AI Christmas ad drew 7,800 comments — and the anger wasn't mostly about the visuals
**Work ID:** W7204147563 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Ranking of backlash drivers not in abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The authors argue the deepest hostility came not from the ads looking bad, but from what using GenAI signaled about the brand's values."
- **Abstract says:** "The findings demonstrate that backlash against GenAI ads emerges not only from perceived inferiority to human work, but also from what the use of GenAI signals to consumers about a brand's values and intentions."
- **Problem:** The abstract presents the two sources as additive ("not only… but also"); the digest recasts them as competing and asserts that the symbolic source is the deeper one. No relative intensity is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "The authors report that backlash stemmed not only from the ads being seen as inferior to human work, but also from what using GenAI signalled about the brand's values.""

**Issue B. Headline and takeaway overstate the aesthetic/moral split** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It suggests that fixing the visual quality of AI-generated ads will not neutralize consumer backlash, because much of the objection is moral and symbolic rather than aesthetic."
- **Abstract says:** "content-related judgments focused on visual realism, execution quality, and contextual fit; psychological reactions centered on authenticity, creativity, and human connection; and socio-ethical attributions involving corporate motives, labor displacement, and broader ethical concerns"
- **Problem:** A qualitative thematic framework identifies three co-occurring dimensions; it does not weigh them or test whether improved visual quality would reduce backlash. The headline ("the anger wasn't mostly about the visuals") rests on the same unsupported weighting.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe to: "It suggests visual quality is only one of three dimensions of backlash — authenticity and ethics concerns appear alongside execution judgments — so better rendering alone may not address all objections.""

### Study 7: "Buy now," "Just do it": meta-analysis finds bossy ad copy backfires — most of all for charities
**Work ID:** W7214238772 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator applied to the wrong outcome** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "with the worst penalty in exactly the category that uses it most"
- **Abstract says:** "advertising domain significantly moderated the effect on state reactance, with nonprofit contexts eliciting stronger reactance responses than for-profit contexts"
- **Problem:** The nonprofit moderation is reported for state reactance only, not for the persuasion outcome. "Worst penalty" implies a larger persuasion loss for nonprofits, which the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "with nonprofit ads provoking stronger reactance than for-profit ads" rather than implying a larger persuasion penalty."

**Issue B. "Near-universal" overstates a meta-analytic average** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The research shows it's near-universal, and that pushy donation appeals trigger it more than pushy product ads."
- **Abstract says:** "Overall, assertive language significantly increased perceived freedom threat (d = 0.72) and state reactance (d = 0.39)"
- **Problem:** A significant pooled average effect is not evidence that the reaction is near-universal across individuals; the abstract reports mean effects and notes inconsistent findings across contexts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "The research shows it's a consistent average effect across 142 samples…""

### Study 19: Naming your rival works in America — and backfires in Europe
**Work ID:** W7171628015 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates European result** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Naming your rival works in America — and backfires in Europe"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 reveals a superiority of ICA over DCA with cross-national differences (US consumers embracing more CA compared to French and British consumers). ... Study 2 results show that DCA outperforms NCA"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that US consumers were relatively more receptive, not that direct comparative advertising backfired in Europe — and in France and the UK direct comparative ads actually outperformed non-comparative ones.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "US consumers are more receptive to comparative ads than French or British consumers — but indirect comparison beat direct comparison overall"."

**Issue B. Study 2 headline finding omitted** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Study 2 (n=800, France and UK) pitted direct comparative against non-comparative ads. Indirect comparison generally beat direct comparison"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 2 results show that DCA outperforms NCA, and imagery processing yields a more positive response to DCA and NCA."
- **Problem:** The digest describes Study 2's design but never reports its result — that direct comparative advertising outperformed non-comparative advertising in France and the UK — which cuts against the digest's framing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that Study 2 found direct comparative ads outperformed non-comparative ads, with imagery processing producing more positive responses."

**Issue C. Non-student sample stated as fact** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two between-subjects experiments with general-population consumers (not students)"
- **Abstract says:** "Calls for additional inquiry into comparative advertising (CA) using samples of populations beyond college students ... (n = 758 consumers)"
- **Problem:** The abstract motivates non-student sampling but only describes participants as "consumers"; the explicit "(not students)" claim is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "two between-subjects experiments with consumer samples"."

### Study 21: During Black Friday-style sales blitzes, ad overload turns eager shoppers hostile
**Work ID:** W7202369464 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Frequency substituted for intrusiveness** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "It suggests the peak-season strategy of maximizing ad frequency can suppress purchase intent precisely when shoppers are most fatigued, and that thin, information-poor creative is the worst offender."
- **Abstract says:** "Study 2 demonstrates a significant three-way interaction effect on emotional reactance, involving perceived intrusiveness (experimentally manipulated as ORSE social media advertising intrusiveness), social media cognitive fatigue, and ORSE participation motivation. Additionally, the ‘low information’ intrusive stimulus had a significant conditional effect on emotional reactance, that reduces both ORSE and product-level purchase intentions"
- **Problem:** The experiments manipulated perceived intrusiveness and information level, not advertising frequency. Attributing the purchase-intention drop to 'maximizing ad frequency' — and then advising marketers to cap frequency — recommends action on a variable the study did not test.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'ads perceived as intrusive — especially thin, information-poor creative — can suppress purchase intent among fatigued shoppers', and change the recommendation from 'capping frequency' to 'reducing perceived intrusiveness and raising information density'."

**Issue B. Headline direction not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "During Black Friday-style sales blitzes, ad overload turns eager shoppers hostile"
- **Abstract says:** "a significant three-way interaction effect on emotional reactance, involving perceived intrusiveness ..., social media cognitive fatigue, and ORSE participation motivation"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an interaction without stating the direction for high-motivation shoppers, and never names Black Friday; the headline asserts both.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to something like 'During online sales events, intrusive ads can trigger reactance — depending on fatigue and how keen the shopper is'."

**Issue C. Pilot study count** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two experimental studies (each preceded by pilots)"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 2 employs a between-group experimental design, comprising two pilot studies, which informed a subsequent main experiment ... Study 3 broadens the contribution of this research with a third pilot study and a main experiment"
- **Problem:** Study 2 had two pilots and Study 3 one; 'each preceded by pilots' is loose but not wrong.
- **Suggested fix:** "State 'Study 2 with two pilots and Study 3 with a further pilot'."
