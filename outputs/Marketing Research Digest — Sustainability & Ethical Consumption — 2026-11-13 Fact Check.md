### Study 1: Talking about your social-impact programs pays off — especially if you have a CMO
**Work ID:** W7225260260 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped moderator (relative firm size)** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The effect was stronger in firms that had a chief marketing officer on the executive team, and it held for service and non-service firms alike."
- **Abstract says:** "the moderating roles of chief marketing officer (CMO) presence, relative firm size, and firm type... Relative firm size matters, with firms with greater relative asset size benefiting more strongly when a CMO is present, and smaller firms showing broader gains across CTSIs disclosure dimensions."
- **Problem:** One of the three moderators the abstract reports — relative firm size, which interacts with CMO presence — is omitted entirely, so readers get a simpler 'CMO helps' story than the study found.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting that firm size also conditioned the effect: larger-asset firms gained most when a CMO was present, while smaller firms gained across more disclosure types."

**Issue B. Causal phrasing in 'Why it matters'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it suggests the marketing function is what converts those programs into market-visible signals"
- **Abstract says:** "Dynamic panel analyses show that CTSIs disclosures and their components positively impact firms' financial outcomes (Total q , return on equity), with stronger effects when a CMO is present."
- **Problem:** 'is what converts' states a mechanism as established; the evidence is a moderation in observational panel data. The story angle later concedes this is correlational, so the two passages sit awkwardly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'it is consistent with the marketing function helping convert those programs into market-visible signals.'"

### Study 2: "Show people you're the kind of person who does this": social-signaling ad beat the sustainability pitch by 40%
**Work ID:** W7225570405 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Comparison condition misdescribed** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "social-signaling ad beat the sustainability pitch by 40%"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 2 provides field-based evidence using two Facebook advertisements for a sustainable luxury hotel and click-through rate (CTR) as the behavioral outcome... the social-expressiveness advertisement generated a CTR more than 40% higher than the non-social-expressiveness advertisement."
- **Problem:** The control ad is described in the abstract only as 'non-social-expressiveness', not as a sustainability or environmental-impact pitch. Both ads were for a sustainable luxury hotel. The headline frames the test as identity framing versus green framing, which is not what the abstract reports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'social-signaling ad drew 40% more clicks than the non-signaling version' and drop the implication that the comparison ad was a sustainability appeal."

**Issue B. Story angle repeats the misframing** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "run paired creative for your sustainable premium offer — one framed around environmental impact, one around what choosing it says about the customer — and compare click-through"
- **Abstract says:** "two Facebook advertisements for a sustainable luxury hotel... the social-expressiveness advertisement generated a CTR more than 40% higher than the non-social-expressiveness advertisement."
- **Problem:** The recommended test is presented as a replication of what the study did, but the study did not pit environmental-impact framing against identity framing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Present the environmental-versus-identity test as an extension the study did not run, or restate it as social-expressive versus non-social-expressive creative."

**Issue C. Attenuation described as reversal** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "consumers with high psychological ownership of the service showed weaker, not stronger, effects — the predicted boost reversed"
- **Abstract says:** "the negative interaction terms indicate attenuation rather than the hypothesized strengthening... high psychological ownership can reduce the marginal importance of other drivers"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes attenuation of the relationships, not a reversal of the effect; 'the predicted boost reversed' can read as the effect flipping sign.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the hypothesized strengthening did not appear — high psychological ownership weakened rather than amplified the links."

### Study 3: Programs built to help refugees start businesses can quietly recreate the harm they target
**Work ID:** W7225785168 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Deterministic phrasing for qualitative findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "design choices about control and access systematically determine whether beneficiaries are empowered or trapped"
- **Abstract says:** "sustaining each position makes a distinct CV pattern likely: a recurrent form of constrained agency or unmet need"
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the link probabilistically ('makes likely', 'recurrent'); 'systematically determine' upgrades an interpretive pattern to a deterministic rule.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as 'design choices about control and access make particular vulnerability patterns more likely'."

### Study 4: Slack in the supply chain hurts green performance—unless it buys resilience
**Work ID:** W7223382174 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported net conclusion about environmental complexity** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and that in volatile, complex operating environments the case for slack gets stronger, not weaker"
- **Abstract says:** "environmental complexity negatively moderates the direct effect and positively moderates the two indirect effects"
- **Problem:** Complexity simultaneously deepens the direct penalty and strengthens the resilience pathway; the abstract reports no net effect, so claiming the overall case for slack gets stronger goes beyond the evidence (and sits against the digest's own body text).
- **Suggested fix:** "State that complexity sharpens both sides — a bigger direct penalty and a bigger resilience payoff — so the net result depends on whether slack is converted into resilience."

### Study 5: Why green industry coalitions die before they start: a front-row look at a cruise shipbuilding network
**Work ID:** W7223667129 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Blocked' / 'die' overstates 'hinder' and 'delay'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Accumulated tensions blocked the micro-processes needed to get collective market shaping off the ground"
- **Abstract says:** "accumulated tensions hinder the microprocesses required for initiating collective market shaping... delaying progress towards collective market shaping"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes hindrance and delay, not outright blockage or failure; the headline's 'die before they start' pushes a single in-progress case toward a verdict the abstract does not give (the digest's own caveat notes no outcome data).
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'hindered' or 'stalled' in the body and soften the headline to 'why green industry coalitions stall before they start'."

### Study 6: Entertaining sustainability content didn't move anyone: a UK–Malaysia comparison of green fashion marketing
**Work ID:** W7223696417 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. eWOM recast as influencer content** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It challenges the default sustainability playbook of entertaining, trend-led, influencer-amplified content"
- **Abstract says:** "social media marketing activities (entertainment, electronic word-of-mouth, trendiness, interactivity and personalisation)"
- **Problem:** The null path was for electronic word-of-mouth, which is consumer-to-consumer sharing, not influencer marketing; the substitution implies the study tested influencer activity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace 'influencer-amplified' with 'word-of-mouth-driven' or 'peer-sharing-led'."

### Study 7: Sweetness beats sustainability: what a taste-and-choose coffee experiment found in Jakarta and Copenhagen
**Work ID:** W7225778945 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Tiebreaker rather than a driver' overstates the ranking result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests sustainability labels function as a tiebreaker rather than a driver"
- **Abstract says:** "Sustainability labels generated positive utilities in both countries, with a similar preference for Fairtrade and Organic certification... the sweetness level was the most influential attribute across both countries"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports labels as positively valued but less influential than sweetness; recasting them as a 'tiebreaker' assigns them a role the choice model did not test.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say labels added value but mattered less than sweetness in driving choice."

**Issue B. Cities not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "what a taste-and-choose coffee experiment found in Jakarta and Copenhagen"
- **Abstract says:** "among Indonesia-based ( n = 197) and Denmark-based ( n = 109) respondents"
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies country of residence, not the cities; Jakarta and Copenhagen are unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'Indonesia and Denmark' in the headline unless the full text confirms the recruitment cities."

### Study 9: Gen Z shoppers who say they care about sustainability are also loyal to ultra-fast fashion
**Work ID:** W7225451567 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediator and direction stated more precisely than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Consumer sustainability consciousness was positively associated with brand loyalty, both directly and through brand equity"
- **Abstract says:** "consumer sustainability consciousness is associated with brand loyalty directly and under mediation, whereas social media peer communication is associated with brand loyalty only under mediation by brand equity"
- **Problem:** The abstract names brand equity as the mediator only for the peer-communication path and does not specify the sign of the sustainability-consciousness association; the digest supplies both details.

### Study 11: When a multinational goes green, its overseas suppliers' emissions fall too
**Work ID:** W7225780096 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unverifiable measurement detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a multinational's own environmental innovation (green patents, products and process investment)"
- **Abstract says:** "higher levels of MNC environmental innovation are associated with lower carbon emissions among foreign suppliers"
- **Problem:** The abstract never specifies how environmental innovation was operationalized; the parenthetical list of green patents, products and process investment cannot be confirmed. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the parenthetical or attribute it explicitly to the full paper rather than presenting it as established detail."

**Issue B. Causal headline for panel association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When a multinational goes green, its overseas suppliers' emissions fall too"
- **Abstract says:** "higher levels of MNC environmental innovation are associated with lower carbon emissions among foreign suppliers"
- **Problem:** The headline states a cause-and-effect sequence while the abstract reports an association in observational dyadic panel data. The body and caveats correct this, but the headline alone implies proven causation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as e.g. 'Multinationals that innovate on green tech have lower-emitting overseas suppliers'."

**Issue C. Mechanism emphasis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the study suggests capability transfer, not just compliance pressure, is doing the work"
- **Abstract says:** "MNCs' environmental innovation reduces foreign suppliers' carbon emissions by strengthening MNCs' incentives to govern suppliers and enhancing their capabilities to empower suppliers"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives equal theoretical weight to governance incentives and capability empowerment; the digest tilts the reading toward capability transfer. The abstract also does not report a test isolating which mechanism dominates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the authors argue both stronger governance incentives and greater capability to empower suppliers are at work."

### Study 12: "Only a few days left" works on frugal donors — and can backfire on everyone else
**Work ID:** W7225100539 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline claims a backfire not in the abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** ""Only a few days left" works on frugal donors — and can backfire on everyone else"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 demonstrates that scarcity appeals increase donation resilience among frugal individuals but not among nonfrugal individuals."
- **Problem:** A null effect among non-frugal donors is reported, not a negative one. 'Backfire on everyone else' tells readers scarcity framing actively reduces giving in that group, which the abstract does not support (and the digest's own body says it 'did nothing for non-frugal donors').
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to something like 'works on frugal donors — and does nothing for everyone else'."

**Issue B. Unverifiable manipulation detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "scarcity-framed charity appeals (limited time, limited matching funds)"
- **Abstract says:** "Scarcity-framed donation appeals are common, but their ability to sustain donor engagement over time remains unclear."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe the specific scarcity cues used in the stimuli. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove the parenthetical examples or mark them as illustrative."

### Study 13: Make people feel small: awe nudged shoppers toward buying less
**Work ID:** W7225693773 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies behavior** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "awe nudged shoppers toward buying less"
- **Abstract says:** "individuals experiencing awe demonstrating a higher willingness to engage in minimalist consumption compared to those in non-awe conditions"
- **Problem:** The outcome was stated willingness in scenario-based experiments with recruited Chinese participants, not purchases by shoppers. The body and caveats make this clear, but the headline does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'awe raised willingness to buy less' or 'awe moved participants toward minimalist intentions'."

**Issue B. Untested comparison with guilt messaging** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "awe-inducing imagery, rather than guilt or frugality messaging, may shift consumers toward buying less and better"
- **Abstract says:** "The results indicate that awe exerted a significant facilitating effect on minimalist consumption ... compared to those in non-awe conditions"
- **Problem:** The comparison condition was non-awe, not guilt or frugality appeals, so the abstract gives no basis for ranking awe above those alternatives.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that awe outperformed a neutral control, and that comparison with guilt or frugality framing remains untested."

**Issue C. Moderator described imprecisely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with the effect moderated by participants' power distance beliefs"
- **Abstract says:** "power distance belief moderates the predictive effect of self-transcendence and authentic-self pursuit on minimalist consumption"
- **Problem:** Power distance belief moderates the mediating path (from self-transcendence and authentic-self pursuit to minimalist consumption), not the overall awe effect as the phrasing suggests.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that power distance belief moderates the link from self-transcendence and authentic-self pursuit to minimalist consumption."
