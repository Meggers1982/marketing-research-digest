### Study 2: Grocery subscriptions: shoppers want the mental break more than the time savings
**Work ID:** W7221025098 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Drivers and barriers were estimated in separate models** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "researchers tested a value-versus-risk framework for automatic grocery replenishment subscriptions, pitting four benefits (time efficiency, supply reliability, cognitive relief, cost efficiency) against four matching worries (time concerns, supply mismatch, lock-in, financial concerns)"
- **Abstract says:** "Two structural models, a ‘drivers model’ and a ‘barriers model’, were estimated using quota-representative surveys in Germany (N = 493) and the UK (N = 339), complemented by multigroup analysis."
- **Problem:** "Pitting ... against" implies drivers and barriers were pitted head-to-head in one model; the abstract describes two separate structural models whose variance explained is compared.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the team estimated separate 'drivers' and 'barriers' models and compared how much variance each explained."

### Study 4: When every product looks the same, promotion and CRM are what investors pay for
**Work ID:** W7215085152 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Attenuation described as disappearance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The advantage disappeared for firms under heavy financial distress, where investors appear to discount the future payoff of relationship and communication investments."
- **Abstract says:** "the stronger value-enhancing effect of intangible differentiation in high-similarity industries is attenuated when firms face high financial distress"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the effect is weakened (attenuated), not eliminated; 'disappeared' implies a null result that the abstract does not state.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'The advantage was weakened for firms under heavy financial distress'."

### Study 5: "Leave a 5-star review and get a discount" damages trust more than simply paying for honest feedback
**Work ID:** W7221330457 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported trend claim in consumer angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Shoppers increasingly read the incentive behind the review, not just the review."
- **Abstract says:** "These findings demonstrate that consumers respond not only to the presence of review incentives but also to how they are structured and communicated."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a single between-subjects experiment with no time-series or trend data, so 'increasingly' is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'increasingly' — e.g. 'In this experiment, people reacted to how the incentive was framed, not just to its presence.'"

### Study 6: Consistent prices across channels build lock-in; consistent promotions do nothing
**Work ID:** W7221526089 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Null result read as consumer preference** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Most shoppers seem relaxed about app-only coupons and store-only specials, but notice when the shelf price and the online price don't match. The former reads as a deal; the latter reads as a trick."
- **Abstract says:** "promotion consistency is related to neither [synergy realisation nor lock-in]"
- **Problem:** A non-significant association between promotion consistency and the two outcomes is not evidence that shoppers are 'relaxed' about inconsistent promotions or that they read them as a deal; this is interpretation beyond the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that promotion consistency showed no measurable link to synergy or lock-in in this sample, without inferring how shoppers feel about channel-specific deals."

### Study 7: Half of smokers don't flinch at price hikes — but plain packs reach them
**Work ID:** W7215078694 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline understates the non-price-sensitive share** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Half of smokers don't flinch at price hikes — but plain packs reach them"
- **Abstract says:** "Class II (60%) was not significantly price-sensitive, preferred branded packaging and branded sticks"
- **Problem:** The non-price-sensitive class is 60% of smokers, not half; the headline also implies plain packs 'reach' that group, whereas it was Class I (40%) that preferred plain packaging while Class II preferred branded packs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as e.g. '60% of smokers in this study weren't price-sensitive — and the 40% who were preferred plain packs'."

**Issue B. Headline reverses who responds to plain packs** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "but plain packs reach them"
- **Abstract says:** "Class I (40% membership) was price-sensitive ... and preferred plain over branded packaging and sticks with health warnings over branded sticks. Class II (60%) was not significantly price-sensitive, preferred branded packaging and branded sticks"
- **Problem:** The abstract shows the price-insensitive majority preferred branded packaging; it does not show plain packs 'reach' that group. The combined-policy simulation reduces uptake overall, not specifically within Class II.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the two classes respond to opposite levers and that combining price and packaging policies reduced predicted uptake overall by 7.7 points."

### Study 8: Explaining how the tracker works makes drivers more likely to accept it, not less
**Work ID:** W7214964045 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Equal group split not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Half received a clear explanation of how telematics technology gathers and uses driving data before evaluating hypothetical policies; half did not."
- **Abstract says:** "1,301 European motor insurance policyholders were randomly assigned either to a control group or to a treatment group that received information explaining the functioning of telematics technology"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the allocation was 50/50; the exact split is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say participants were randomly assigned to a treatment group that received the explanation or to a control group that did not."

### Study 9: Nearly 4 in 10 cigarettes smoked in Bolivia's big cities are illicit — but taxes aren't the reason
**Work ID:** W7215090900 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mischaracterises the prior 2002 estimate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is the first independent, pack-level estimate for Bolivia since 2002"
- **Abstract says:** "The most recent estimate of illicit trade in Bolivia dates to 2002 and relied on industry-reported sales data. We provide the first independent, direct estimate of illicit cigarette penetration in the country."
- **Problem:** 'First independent ... since 2002' implies the 2002 estimate was also independent; the abstract says it relied on industry-reported sales data and that this is the first independent estimate full stop.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'the first independent, pack-level estimate for Bolivia — the only prior figure, from 2002, came from industry-reported sales data'."

### Study 10: Europe's supermarkets are selling nearly identical plant-based lineups — and pricing them inconsistently
**Work ID:** W7219001667 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Almost no fish analogues' overstates 'underrepresented'** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The authors flag thin brand variety, almost no fish analogues, limited recipe and flavor diversity, and inconsistent pricing and category labeling across retailers."
- **Abstract says:** "investing in underrepresented segments such as fish analogues, and increasing recipe-level variety in terms of flavour profiles, culinary traditions and plant protein sources"

- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'underrepresented fish analogues'."

### Study 11: Making it cheaper isn't enough: how constant tradeoffs trap low-income shoppers
**Work ID:** W7204257214 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Two concepts merged into one** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "which the authors theorize as "consumption entrapment": a self-reinforcing cycle in which repeated inadequate purchases erode the reciprocity, trust and mutual support networks people depend on"
- **Abstract says:** "The study develops the concepts of consumption entrapment and collapsing social capital, demonstrating how repeated consumption inadequacies weaken reciprocity, trust and relational support"
- **Problem:** The abstract presents two separate theoretical contributions — consumption entrapment (a cumulative, self-reinforcing condition) and collapsing social capital. The digest folds the erosion of trust and reciprocity into the definition of entrapment.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword as "which the authors theorize as 'consumption entrapment' alongside a parallel concept of 'collapsing social capital' — repeated inadequate purchases weaken the reciprocity, trust and relational support people depend on.""

### Study 12: AI shoppers say they'll pay more than real shoppers do — and the gap wrecks price optimization
**Work ID:** W7214644774 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates direction of error** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "AI shoppers say they'll pay more than real shoppers do — and the gap wrecks price optimization"
- **Abstract says:** "For two more hedonic products, synthetic respondents overstate WTP ... For two more utilitarian products, results are more heterogeneous, ranging from near mean-level correspondence to substantial WTP underestimation."
- **Problem:** Overstatement was found for only two of four products; for the other two the synthetic estimates were accurate or too low. The headline implies a uniform upward bias, which the digest body itself contradicts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe, e.g. "AI respondents miss real willingness to pay — sometimes high, sometimes low — and the gap undermines price optimization.""

**Issue B. Added emphasis not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the synthetic respondents systematically overstated willingness to pay"
- **Abstract says:** "synthetic respondents overstate WTP and imply overly optimistic demand and profit outcomes"
- **Problem:** "Systematically" adds a claim of consistency the abstract does not make.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "systematically" and simply say the synthetic respondents overstated willingness to pay."

### Study 13: For luxury NFTs, logos get clicks but only visual craft earns a price premium
**Work ID:** W7213234135 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Behavioral language for a stated intention** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "logos get clicks but only visual craft earns a price premium"
- **Abstract says:** "all three visual features significantly increase willingness to buy, whereas only visual quality significantly impacts willingness to pay a price premium"
- **Problem:** "Clicks" suggests observed online behavior; the study measured self-reported willingness to buy in an experiment.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "logos lift buying intent" rather than "logos get clicks.""

**Issue B. Three-way interaction omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Brand prominence also moderated typicality: the preference for category-typical NFTs faded when branding was subtle."
- **Abstract says:** "This interaction is further moderated by visual quality, suggesting a joint effect among the visual features."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that the brand prominence x typicality interaction is itself conditioned by visual quality; the digest stops at the two-way interaction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting the interaction was further moderated by visual quality, indicating the three design cues work jointly."

### Study 14: Opting out of data sharing may cost you: a model says privacy-protecting shoppers can end up paying more
**Work ID:** W4410534098 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Consumer advice not supported by the model** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Comparison shopping across sellers, not just privacy settings, is what protects your wallet."
- **Abstract says:** "data-sharing consumers who prefer third-party sellers may also experience negative spillovers, paying higher prices due to their inclusion in mixed segments ... they may inadvertently lead to higher prices for both anonymous and certain data-sharing consumers, reducing overall consumer welfare"
- **Problem:** The model shows consumers who favor third-party sellers can also be priced up, so it does not establish that shopping across sellers protects buyers. This is prescriptive advice the analysis does not test.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with a statement that the model finds no simple consumer defense — even shoppers who prefer third-party sellers can be swept into higher-priced segments."

**Issue B. Consumer welfare conclusion omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tighter data rules may hand platforms a new segmentation lever that raises prices for the very consumers the rules were meant to protect"
- **Abstract says:** "they may inadvertently lead to higher prices for both anonymous and certain data-sharing consumers, reducing overall consumer welfare"
- **Problem:** The abstract's headline implication — a reduction in overall consumer welfare — is not stated; the digest limits the harm to anonymous users.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the model implies reduced overall consumer welfare, affecting both anonymous and some data-sharing consumers."

### Study 15: When AI learns from users, the free tier can start eating the business
**Work ID:** W7213561739 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Capability-gap finding restated vaguely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the model implies there's a capability gap you have to actively manage between free and paid"
- **Abstract says:** "Narrowing the base capability gap between versions can also increase profitability by strengthening adaptive learning despite the increased cannibalization."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives a specific directional result — narrowing the gap can raise profits — while the digest's wording is directionless and could be read as advising a wider gap, the opposite of the stated finding.
- **Suggested fix:** "State the result directly: narrowing the capability gap between free and paid can raise profits because it strengthens adaptive learning, even though it increases cannibalization."
