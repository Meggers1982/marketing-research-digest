### Study 1: What 141,000 Consumer Complaints Reveal About Why Disputes Escalate in Poorer Neighborhoods
**Work ID:** W7204606942 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Comparison not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It suggests that in financial services, disputes are driven less by the failure itself than by whether people can comprehend and act on the remedy"
- **Abstract says:** "we examine how seven salience dimensions relate to dispute escalation. The results suggest that cognitive-control and behavioral-control salience have the strongest positive associations with disputing."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports relative strength among seven control-salience dimensions, not a contest between the service failure itself and control cues; the digest implies the failure's role was measured and found weaker.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "among the control-related cues examined, comprehension and ability to act were the strongest correlates of escalation," dropping the implied comparison with the failure itself."

### Study 5: Devout Customers Reported More Willingness to Be Rude to Staff — Unless Karma Was on Their Minds
**Work ID:** W7223658951 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Implied cross-study comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "That pathway was weaker among people with strong belief in karma, and weaker still when karma-style action–consequence thinking was made salient experimentally."
- **Abstract says:** "In Study 2, this indirect association was weaker among customers reporting stronger belief in karma. In Study 3, it was weaker when karma-related considerations were made situationally salient."
- **Problem:** "Weaker still" suggests the experimental manipulation attenuated the pathway more than measured karma belief did; the abstract reports two separate attenuations in different studies with no comparison between them.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "and also weaker when karma-style action–consequence thinking was made salient in a separate experiment.""

### Study 6: Not all workplace stress is bad: the kind that makes staff steal — and the kind that stops them
**Work ID:** W7221898122 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator direction unverifiable** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "peer-led development only buffered challenge stressors"
- **Abstract says:** "Self-led development moderates both stressor types, while peer-led development only affects challenge stressors."
- **Problem:** The abstract states only that peer-led development moderates the challenge-stressor path, without specifying whether it strengthens or dampens it; "buffered" asserts a dampening direction and sits oddly with challenge stressors having a beneficial effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "peer-led development moderated only the challenge-stressor path (direction not specified in the abstract).""

### Study 7: Customers find humanoid service robots creepy — except when the service is embarrassing
**Work ID:** W7220660112 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Boundary-condition detail beyond abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "there, the human employee's own creepiness rating rose with the context while the robot's did not, narrowing or reversing the robot's disadvantage"
- **Abstract says:** "we identify embarrassment associated with the service context as an important boundary condition. Further, we find that perceived creepiness of human (but not humanoid robot) frontline employees is contingent on service context."
- **Problem:** The abstract says human creepiness is contingent on context but does not state that it rose in embarrassing contexts, nor that the robot's disadvantage narrowed or reversed.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that human (but not robot) creepiness varied with service context and that embarrassment was an important boundary condition, flagging the specific pattern as not detailed in the abstract."

### Study 8: Gluten-free shoppers aren't asking for more choice — they want bread that tastes good and is safe
**Work ID:** W7222470326 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates the variety finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Gluten-free shoppers aren't asking for more choice — they want bread that tastes good and is safe"
- **Abstract says:** "product quality has the strongest association with consumer satisfaction, followed by service quality and product variety. ... Consumers report dissatisfaction with the limited availability of key product categories such as bread, pastry and ready-to-eat meals."
- **Problem:** Variety was still a positive correlate of satisfaction, just the weakest of three, and respondents explicitly complained about limited availability — so "aren't asking for more choice" overstates the result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "quality and safety matter more than breadth of range — but the bread and ready-meal shelves still fall short.""

**Issue B. Added gloss on "safety"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Safety (freedom from cross-contamination) and taste were the most valued product attributes"
- **Abstract says:** "Safety and taste emerge as the most valued product attributes."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not define safety as freedom from cross-contamination; the parenthetical is the digest's interpretation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the parenthetical or mark it as an interpretation."

### Study 9: How close the disaster feels decides whether tourists boycott a destination
**Work ID:** W7221524554 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation overread** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the damage operates through felt unfairness rather than the failure itself"
- **Abstract says:** "perceived relative deprivation and trust in destinations sequentially mediated the impact of the scopes of service failure crises on tourism boycott"
- **Problem:** Serial mediation means the failure's effect travels through deprivation and trust; it does not show the failure itself is not the source of the damage.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the effect of crisis scope travels through perceived relative deprivation and then eroded trust."

**Issue B. Theoretical framing incomplete** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two experiments grounded in construal level theory"
- **Abstract says:** "Grounded in self-bias theory and construal level theory"
- **Problem:** One of the two stated theoretical foundations is omitted.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add self-bias theory alongside construal level theory."

### Study 11: Corner shops can win the speed game without any of the quick-commerce infrastructure
**Work ID:** W7215042272 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported specification of digital service quality** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "basic digital service quality (chat ordering and similar lightweight tools)"
- **Abstract says:** "showing how social proximity, lightweight digital interaction and delivery responsiveness can create competitive advantage in informal retail environments"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers only to "lightweight digital interaction"; it never specifies chat/messaging ordering as the operationalization of digital service quality.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "lightweight digital interaction" or mark chat ordering as an illustrative example not specified in the abstract."

**Issue B. Perceived vs. actual speed comparison** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and the feeling of speed, more than literal delivery minutes, is what drives where we buy"
- **Abstract says:** "It distinguishes perceived immediacy from infrastructural scale and positions perceived nanostore competitiveness as a consumer-level evaluative mechanism."
- **Problem:** The study measured perceptions only; it did not compare perceived immediacy against actual delivery times, so a "more than literal delivery minutes" ranking is not established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "perceived immediacy, rather than logistics scale, is what the study links to purchase intention.""

### Study 12: When your hashtag gets hijacked, the angriest posts aren't the ones that spread
**Work ID:** W7206212528 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hedge dropped on diffusion finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the most negative messages did not spread further once a conversation was already negatively skewed"
- **Abstract says:** "the findings suggest that negative messages do not necessarily achieve greater diffusion within negatively skewed bashtag conversations"
- **Problem:** The abstract says negativity does not *necessarily* produce greater diffusion; the digest converts a hedged null-ish result into a flat statement that the harshest posts did not spread.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the hedge: "the most negative messages did not necessarily spread further...""

**Issue B. "Single market context" caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Two brands and two campaigns in a single market context"
- **Abstract says:** "This study analyzed 14,511 posts related to McDonald's and Walkers Crisps"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify a market, and the two brands are associated with different markets (US and UK), so describing the evidence as coming from a single market is unverifiable from the abstract and probably inaccurate.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "two brands and two campaigns only; market context not specified in the abstract.""

### Study 13: What a million words of app-store griping reveals about fintech users' well-being

### Study 17: Faith-aligned features won't save a glitchy app: evidence from Islamic mobile banking reviews
**Work ID:** W7216405737 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Complaint clustering presented as a finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "complaints clustered around system updates, verification processes, core banking functionality and stability — basic reliability, not religious compliance"
- **Abstract says:** "Islamic banks should prioritize improvements in system updates, verification processes, financial and banking functionalities, and overall system stability. Deficiencies in these fundamental service attributes generate negative emotional responses"
- **Problem:** The abstract presents these four areas as priority improvement recommendations tied to negative emotional responses, not as a measured distribution showing complaints concentrated there. The implied contrast with religious compliance is also the digest's addition — the abstract reports adherence to Islamic attributes as a significant positive contributor to satisfaction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "the authors direct managers to system updates, verification, core banking functionality and stability, where deficiencies triggered negative emotional responses" and drop the implied either/or with Islamic attributes."
