### Study 1: Thinner packaging saves material — and ends up in the trash instead of the recycling bin
**Work ID:** W7214074641 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated size of the corrective effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Prompting people to reconsider the value of the low-density package reversed much of the effect."
- **Abstract says:** "However, prompting consumers to reconsider the value of low-density packaging can curtail this unintended wastefulness."
- **Problem:** "Curtail" indicates the intervention reduces the effect; "reversed much of the effect" implies a quantified, near-complete reversal that the abstract does not report.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Prompting people to reconsider the value of the low-density package reduced this unintended wastefulness.""

### Study 2: Shoppers want recycled packaging — but will pay extra only for the plastic seal that proves it's not a fake
**Work ID:** W7172534656 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline generalizes a narrow sample** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Shoppers want recycled packaging — but will pay extra only for the plastic seal that proves it's not a fake"
- **Abstract says:** "A choice-based conjoint analysis was conducted to study consumer preferences for multi-attribute products with 133 young Thai consumers."
- **Problem:** The headline implies shoppers generally, whereas the result comes from a hypothetical conjoint task with 133 young consumers in one emerging market. The body and caveats do note this.
- **Suggested fix:** "Qualify the headline, e.g. "Young Thai shoppers want recycled packaging — but will pay extra only for the plastic seal.""

### Study 5: Visible sustainability can make luxury look less fashionable — unless the brand frames it as a design choice
**Work ID:** W7215083585 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline implies a directional effect the abstract does not report** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Visible sustainability can make luxury look less fashionable — unless the brand frames it as a design choice"
- **Abstract says:** "the findings indicate that sustainability‐related design cues do not exert a uniform influence on product evaluation. Instead, their effects depend on consumers' interpretations of those cues and the broader positioning context"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a non-uniform, interpretation-dependent effect rather than a baseline drop in perceived fashionability; the headline suggests a default negative effect that is "fixed" by framing. The body text does hedge correctly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe, e.g. "Visible sustainability cues cut both ways in luxury — it depends on whether shoppers read them as a design choice.""

### Study 8: "Experts recommend it" beat "you said you cared" — in both Ecuador and Norway
**Work ID:** W7214115221 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Almost entirely" overstates "largely attributable"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but the effect was driven almost entirely by the authority message"
- **Abstract says:** "instead, the results were largely attributable to the authority condition alone, which was considerably more effective than both other conditions across countries"
- **Problem:** "Almost entirely" implies a stronger attribution than the abstract's "largely attributable."
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "the effect was largely attributable to the authority message.""

**Issue B. "Real choice" not specified as consequential** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "before making a real choice between a more and a less sustainable option"
- **Abstract says:** "After replying to some survey items, participants made a choice between a more (vs. less) sustainable option."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a behavioral field experiment with a choice task but does not state that the choice was incentivized or consequential, so "real" is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "before making a choice between a more and a less sustainable option (a behavioral field experiment).""

### Study 10: How sponsoring Olympians buys a mining magnate glowing press
**Work ID:** W7214088398 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Claim that coverage omitted her core business** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "linking her to likeable athletes and to patriotism and national glory rather than to her core business"
- **Abstract says:** "this coverage overwhelmingly framed Rinehart as a benevolent benefactor of Olympic sport, associating her with charismatic athletes and more abstract constructs such as patriotism and national glory"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports what the coverage emphasized but does not state that her mining business was absent from the framing; the consumer angle repeats this as something the analysis "shows."
- **Suggested fix:** "Say coverage emphasized benefaction, athletes and national glory, and drop or hedge the claim about her core business dropping out."

**Issue B. "Mechanism" language for an interpretive case study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "It documents the mechanism by which sports sponsorship converts money into favorable framing in national media"
- **Abstract says:** "It identifies the earned media publicity generated from these sponsorships through qualitative content analysis of Australian newspapers... develops a critical public relations model of sportswashing (CPRMS)"
- **Problem:** A single-case qualitative media analysis proposes a conceptual model; "documents the mechanism" suggests a demonstrated causal process, although the caveats do note it is interpretive.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "It proposes a model of how sports philanthropy can translate into favorable framing in national media.""

### Study 12: Students will eat the climate-friendly lunch — just don't call it a vegetarian day
**Work ID:** W7214091952 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Reformulation framed as replacing vegetarian days** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "For anyone selling or serving lower-carbon food, quietly reformulating beats labeling a day as meat-free"
- **Abstract says:** "They also indicate that lower-carbon recipe reformulation may complement vegetarian-only days as a practical strategy for supporting acceptance and intake of climate-friendly school meals."
- **Problem:** The abstract positions reformulation as a complement to vegetarian-only days, not a superior substitute. The digest's "beats" framing (and the "stealth reformulation" angle, which implies concealment the study did not test) could lead operators to drop meat-free days on the strength of a finding about relative acceptability only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to: "recipe reformulation was more acceptable to students than vegetarian-only days, and the authors suggest it may work alongside — not instead of — meat-free days." Drop "quietly"/"stealth" framing or label it as the digest's own speculation."

**Issue B. Correlates described as drivers** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "girls and frequent vegetarian-meal choosers drive much of the positive attitude, limiting generalizability"
- **Abstract says:** "Quantitative analysis showed that more positive attitudes toward climate-friendly school meals were associated with perceiving climate considerations as important in food choice, more frequent selection of vegetarian meals at school, and being female."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports associations from multiple regression, not that these groups "drive" attitudes; the digest also omits the third correlate (importance of climate in food choice).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say attitudes were more positive among girls, frequent vegetarian-meal choosers, and those who rate climate as important in food choice."

### Study 13: Inside five agencies rewiring creative work around generative AI
**Work ID:** W7214980150 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Unsupported performance claim about mode-switching** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Teams that performed best switched fluidly between modes within a single job rather than committing to one."
- **Abstract says:** "Across both paths, micro-ambidexterity enables practitioners and teams to flexibly switch between and recombine collaboration modes within a single production process."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes mode-switching (micro-ambidexterity) as an observed capability, not as something distinguishing higher-performing teams; no performance data are reported. This also contradicts the digest's own caveat that there is "no outcome data on creative effectiveness, cost or client results."
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "The authors describe 'micro-ambidexterity' — teams flexibly switching between and recombining modes within a single production process — without comparing performance outcomes.""

### Study 14: Young shoppers don't blame themselves for unsustainable shopping — they blame the system

- **As written:** "a coordination problem spanning retailers, suppliers, landlords and government"
- **Abstract says:** "Participants describe sustainable retail as a coordination problem across stakeholders and system elements rather than as isolated practices or an individual decision problem."

### Study 15: The invisible labor of shopping for healthcare across a border
**Work ID:** W7204439549 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues
