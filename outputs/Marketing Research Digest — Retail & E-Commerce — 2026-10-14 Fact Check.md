### Study 1: Tell shoppers they're building a collection, not just buying a thing
**Work ID:** W7221306245 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Outcome restated as willingness to buy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "can increase willingness to buy and make the process itself a source of value"
- **Abstract says:** "improves consumers’ acquisition experience and increases product desirability"
- **Problem:** The measured outcomes are acquisition experience and product desirability; 'willingness to buy' is a different, purchase-proximal construct not reported in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the framing 'raised how desirable the product seemed and made the search itself more enjoyable' rather than 'willingness to buy'."

**Issue B. Participant pool not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "All studies are experiments, largely with online participants"
- **Abstract says:** "seven preregistered studies plus one supplemental study (N = 3,306)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no information about where participants were recruited; 'largely with online participants' is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the claim or hedge: 'participant sources are not specified in the abstract.'"

### Study 2: Launching a green product line lifted the parent brand — but only for firms with clean reputations
**Work ID:** W7221350449 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator turned into a necessary condition** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Launching a green product line lifted the parent brand — but only for firms with clean reputations"
- **Abstract says:** "A complementary analysis of subsequent SNPIs across 15 leading brands in 12 retail chains reveals that this effect is moderated by parent firms' ethical reputation and retail chains' sustainable assortment share."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a moderation, not a condition under which the effect disappears; the main event study shows a positive effect without that qualifier. The direction of the moderation is also not stated in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'and the size of the lift depended on the firm's ethical reputation and the retailer's sustainable assortment'; note that direction is not specified in the abstract."

**Issue B. Directional claim about weak-reputation firms** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "audit your own ethical track record first, since the evidence suggests firms with weak reputations get little signaling benefit"
- **Abstract says:** "this effect is moderated by parent firms' ethical reputation and retail chains' sustainable assortment share"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not quantify or sign the moderation, so 'little signaling benefit' for weak-reputation firms is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to 'the payoff appears to depend on existing ethical reputation, though the abstract does not report the size or direction of that difference.'"

### Study 3: In live shopping, the host's mood moves sales — and the sidekick may be costing you money
**Work ID:** W7220454801 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal shorthand for associational findings** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the host's emotional performance is the main lever on revenue"
- **Abstract says:** "streamer emotions are closely associated with viewer interaction and purchase decisions"
- **Problem:** The study reports associations and impulse responses from observational panel data; 'main lever on revenue' and the headline's 'moves sales' imply demonstrated causation, which the abstract is careful not to claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'the host's emotional energy is the variable most strongly associated with sales' and soften the headline to 'tracks sales'."

### Study 4: When the shock hit, paid search carried e-commerce revenue — and Facebook ads brought visitors who didn't buy
**Work ID:** W7220946924 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. 'Strongest' not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Direct traffic and repeat visitors were consistently the strongest revenue correlates in both periods"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings include that direct access and repeat visitors are consistently associated with revenue."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports consistency of association, not a ranking of channels by strength.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'were consistently associated with revenue in both periods'."

**Issue B. Spend substituted for channel** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "lagged models showed paid search spending today predicted later organic search and direct visits"
- **Abstract says:** "Lagged models show paid search is consistently associated with later organic search and direct access."
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to the paid search channel, not to spending levels, and frames the result as a lagged association rather than prediction of spend effects.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write 'paid search activity was consistently associated with later organic search and direct arrivals'."

**Work ID:** W7220470690 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Design details beyond the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "comparing review behavior before and after the feature appeared"
- **Abstract says:** "the introduction of AIGS actually leads to an increase in the volume of consumer reviews ... This mechanism is supported by an additional controlled laboratory experiment"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe the empirical design (whether it is a simple before/after comparison, a difference-in-differences, or otherwise), so characterizing it as a before/after design is unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'studied the rollout of AI-generated summaries on an e-commerce platform' without specifying the comparison design."

**Issue B. Unverifiable time-horizon caveat** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "short post-introduction horizon"
- **Abstract says:** "(no observation window is reported)"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no information about the length of the post-introduction period, so this caveat cannot be checked.
- **Suggested fix:** "Remove, or note that the observation window is not reported in the abstract."

### Study 8: On B2B platforms, a strong online reputation and a strong trade-show presence cancel each other out
**Work ID:** W7220830415 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Offline events narrowed to trade shows** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a strong trade-show presence"
- **Abstract says:** "offline event participation"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to offline event participation generally; trade shows are one possible form and are never named.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use 'offline event participation' in the headline."

**Issue B. 'Cancel each other out' overstates the interaction** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "cancel each other out"
- **Abstract says:** "their interaction is negative: the conditional association of each mechanism weakens as the other strengthens"
- **Problem:** A negative interaction means diminishing incremental returns, not mutual cancellation; the body text describes it correctly but the headline exaggerates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Headline as 'partly substitute for each other' or 'blunt each other's payoff'."

### Study 9: Fun-seeking shoppers forgive influencers who stray off-topic; practical shoppers don't
**Work ID:** W7221454638 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Warmth comparison shifted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "When the product was far outside the influencer's lane, hedonic consumers responded better to warm influencers than to competent-seeming ones."
- **Abstract says:** "when low-relevance products are recommended, hedonic (vs. functional) consumers are inclined to purchase products from warm (vs. competent) influencers"
- **Problem:** The abstract frames this as a contrast between hedonic and functional consumers in their preference for warm versus competent influencers; the digest reports only the within-hedonic comparison, dropping the functional-consumer half of the contrast.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the warm-over-competent preference distinguishes hedonic consumers from functional ones."

### Study 10: Three in four Vietnamese grocery shoppers will pay for home delivery — but they're exquisitely sensitive to the fee
**Work ID:** W7204970234 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Regional sample generalized to a country** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Three in four Vietnamese grocery shoppers will pay for home delivery"
- **Abstract says:** "The data was collected from a discrete choice experiment on 438 shoppers in the Mekong Delta, Vietnam. ... Around 75% of consumers have a positive WTP for home delivery."

- **Suggested fix:** "Headline as 'Three in four shoppers in Vietnam's Mekong Delta say they'd pay for home delivery'."

### Study 13: Why India's lowest-income shoppers stay loyal to one store — and it isn't trust
**Work ID:** W7214970397 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline reframes constrained choice as loyalty and opportunism as trust** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Why India's lowest-income shoppers stay loyal to one store — and it isn't trust"
- **Abstract says:** "The findings indicate that bounded rationality and lock-in significantly contribute to CRS, which, in turn, directly predicts CBS. Opportunism, although present, does not substantially influence CRS or CBS."
- **Problem:** The study measures constrained retail selection (shopping at one store because of limited options), not loyalty in the attitudinal sense; and "perceived opportunism" (suspicion of exploitation) is a narrower construct than "trust".
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to e.g. "Why India's lowest-income shoppers are locked into one store — and suspicion of the retailer isn't the reason"."

**Issue B. Unlisted detail about what lock-in comprises** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "lock-in — credit ties, proximity, dependence on a single shopkeeper — significantly drove constrained store selection"
- **Abstract says:** "consumer vulnerability activates bounded rationality, perceived opportunism, and lock-in effects, which together shape constrained retail selection (CRS) and constrained brand selection (CBS)"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not specify that lock-in consists of credit ties, proximity and dependence on one shopkeeper. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the specifics to the paper's measures or drop the parenthetical gloss."

**Issue C. Causal verb for cross-sectional survey data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Bounded rationality (limited information and literacy) and lock-in ... significantly drove constrained store selection, which in turn constrained which brands people could buy."
- **Abstract says:** "bounded rationality and lock-in significantly contribute to CRS, which, in turn, directly predicts CBS"
- **Problem:** "Drove" and "constrained which brands people could buy" imply causation from a cross-sectional PLS-SEM model, which the digest's own caveat says cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "were significantly associated with" and "which in turn predicted"."

### Study 14: Giving your shopping assistant a face: when human-looking AI avatars actually lift engagement
**Work ID:** W7215066966 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Modest" applied to the wrong effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this work suggests the lift is real but modest"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 shows that higher visual-form anthropomorphism increases user engagement intention, with emotional connection accounting for a meaningful but partial share of the effect. Study 2 provides associative support for the core relationships, although the association between anthropomorphism and emotional connection is comparatively modest."
- **Problem:** The abstract calls the anthropomorphism–emotional connection association in Study 2 comparatively modest; it does not characterize the overall engagement-intention lift as modest. The digest's caveat gets this right, but the body sentence does not.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "the engagement lift is real, but emotional connection explains only part of it, and the anthropomorphism–emotion link was comparatively modest in the survey study"."
