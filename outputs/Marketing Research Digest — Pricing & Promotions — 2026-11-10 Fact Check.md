### Study 4: What 6,602 auctioned paintings reveal about who really sets the price in a chaotic market
**Work ID:** W7224345056 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Absence of evidence stated as evidence of absence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The pandemic did not permanently inflate art prices the way headlines suggested; the premium showed up during the disruption but did not clearly persist afterward."
- **Abstract says:** "Pandemic premiums are supported in three models, whereas a persistent post-pandemic premium is not consistently supported."
- **Problem:** "Did not permanently inflate" asserts a null result, whereas the abstract reports only that a persistent premium was not consistently supported across model specifications. The second half of the sentence hedges correctly, but the opening clause overstates.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "The evidence for a lasting post-pandemic price premium was inconsistent across models; the premium showed up clearly only during the disruption.""

### Study 6: The Groupon customers everyone warned you about? They paid off within a year
**Work ID:** W7225402212 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Relative value overstated as outperformance** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and more valuable than organics the more heavily a firm discounts future income"
- **Abstract says:** "their relative value increased with a firm's discount rate of future incomes"
- **Problem:** The abstract says only that the relative value of Groupon customers rises as the discount rate rises; it does not state that they overtake organically acquired customers at any point. The digest asserts a crossover the abstract does not report.

### Study 7: Half of German consumers say they'd buy biodiversity-friendly food — and pay more for it
**Work ID:** W7225468467 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb for a survey association** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Biodiversity attributes raised stated willingness to pay across all segments, including the less receptive ones."
- **Abstract says:** "Across all target groups biodiversity-friendly product attributes are associated with higher stated willingness to pay"
- **Problem:** This is a cross-sectional survey with latent profile analysis; "raised" implies an experimentally demonstrated effect rather than the association the abstract reports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "raised" to "were associated with higher" stated willingness to pay."

**Issue B. Speculation beyond the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A new eco-label is coming to supermarket shelves, and it is not about carbon."
- **Abstract says:** "the market potential for these products and the characteristics of relevant consumer target groups remain unclear ... making communication, wording, and credible labelling decisive for market success"
- **Problem:** The abstract studies consumer acceptance and segment potential; it makes no claim that a biodiversity label is arriving in stores. The forecast is the digest's own.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Biodiversity could become the next sustainability claim on supermarket shelves — this study suggests the audience for it exists.""

### Study 9: When cigarettes get pricier, smokers switch — but only if they haven't already vaped
**Work ID:** W7225156193 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dual-user null stated too absolutely** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "people who had already added vaping to their routine kept buying cigarettes no matter what they cost"
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals who smoke cigarettes and use NVP purchased more NVP and less HTP with no significant effects of cigarette price ... for individuals who smoke cigarettes and use NVP, cigarette purchasing was not affected by the availability of other products"
- **Problem:** The abstract's conclusion about dual users concerns cigarette purchasing being unaffected by the availability of other products; "no matter what they cost" turns a non-significant result in a 28-person sample into a definitive statement about price insensitivity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "cigarette purchasing among dual users showed no significant response to the other products on offer, and price effects were not detected in this small sample.""

**Issue B. Dropped dual-user finding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "For dual users, cigarette purchases barely budged regardless of price or what else was on the shelf."
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals who smoke cigarettes and use NVP purchased more NVP and less HTP with no significant effects of cigarette price."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that dual users did shift between alternatives (more vaping products, less heated tobacco); the digest presents them as entirely unresponsive, omitting that within-alternative movement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that dual users still shifted between alternatives — buying more vaping products and less heated tobacco — even though cigarette purchases did not move."

### Study 10: Nearly 730,000 days of Airbnb data show deep learning can forecast demand — and explain itself
**Work ID:** W7167636549 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Best-performing model omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pitted traditional forecasting methods (regression, SARIMAX) against neural network architectures including LSTM, Transformer and a hybrid"
- **Abstract says:** "we compare traditional models (regression, SARIMAX) with advanced architectures (FCNN, LSTM, Transformer, and a Transformer–LSTM hybrid) ... The best-performing model (FCNN) was further used to simulate a 3x3 pricing game"
- **Problem:** By naming only LSTM, Transformer and the hybrid, the digest implies these were the winning models, when the abstract identifies the simpler FCNN as best-performing and as the model used for the pricing game — a relevant detail for anyone drawing a vendor-evaluation lesson.
- **Suggested fix:** "Name FCNN in the model list and note that it was the best performer and the model used for the 3x3 pricing simulation."

### Study 11: When explaining your algorithm backfires: short beats thorough on AI disclosure
**Work ID:** W7225591358 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. AI-washing skepticism overstated in consumer angle** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The study found shoppers instinctively discount heavy AI talk as "AI-washing" — doubt about whether the AI claims are real at all."
- **Abstract says:** "The trust advantage of a brief explanation over no explanation was accompanied by lower AI-washing skepticism, reflecting doubts about AI claims, whereas Study 1 provided no clear evidence that skepticism accounted for the lower trust under elaborated disclosure. In Study 2 (N = 220; 2 × 2 design), identical high-disclosure content lowered perceived authenticity and trust, through heightened skepticism, when framed as image-oriented rather than consumer-serving."
- **Problem:** Skepticism explained the trust drop only for image-oriented framing in Study 2; Study 1 found no clear evidence it drove the elaborated-disclosure effect. Presenting it as a general, instinctive consumer reaction goes beyond the abstract and sits awkwardly against the digest's own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as: "In one study, when detailed AI talk was framed as a brand credential rather than a service to the shopper, it raised 'AI-washing' skepticism and lowered trust — though the evidence for that mechanism was not consistent across both studies.""

**Issue B. Illustrative quote not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "when it was framed as image-oriented ("we're an AI-first company") rather than as serving the shopper"
- **Abstract says:** "identical high-disclosure content lowered perceived authenticity and trust, through heightened skepticism, when framed as image-oriented rather than consumer-serving"
- **Problem:** The quoted wording is an invented example presented as if it were stimulus material; it cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the quotation marks or mark it as the digest's own paraphrase, e.g. "framed as image-oriented (brand-credential language) rather than as serving the shopper"."

### Study 12: Nudges can buy down the price of a subsidy: confrontational apps cut what farmers demand to change practice
**Work ID:** W7225388749 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Undefined" results described as a null effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Peer pressure is the nudge everyone reaches for, but in this sample of farmers it did essentially nothing"
- **Abstract says:** "Confrontational nudges are most effective; facilitation nudges show heterogeneous response and social influence and signalling nudges generate undefined responses."
- **Problem:** "Undefined responses" means the direction/effect was not clearly identified, which is not the same as a demonstrated null. The body text ("gave no clear effect") is accurate, but the consumer angle hardens it into "did essentially nothing" and "underperformed".
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "produced no clearly identifiable effect in this sample" in both the consumer angle and the marketer angle."

### Study 13: Feeling powerful makes people pay less when they get to name their own price
**Work ID:** W7213671471 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Design details (priming, multiple studies) not in abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Across their studies, people primed into a high-power state paid less than those in a low-power state"
- **Abstract says:** "The results show that individuals with a high sense of power pay less than individuals with a low sense of power in pay-what-you-want contexts and that the agentic–communal orientation mediates the effect of power states on the payment."
- **Problem:** The abstract never says power was manipulated by priming or that there were multiple studies; the digest elsewhere concedes that no design details are reported, so the body contradicts its own caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "people with a high sense of power paid less than those with a low sense of power" and note that the abstract does not specify how power states were induced or how many studies were run; drop "primed" and "Across their studies" or mark them as unverifiable."

**Issue B. "Gap closed" overstates a narrowing** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Crucially, the gap closed when social motivation was made salient — under those conditions high-power consumers actually paid more."
- **Abstract says:** "when social motivation is salient, individuals with a high sense of power pay more and the payment difference between low-power and high-power individuals decreases"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the difference decreasing, not disappearing; "the gap closed" implies elimination.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the gap narrowed when social motivation was made salient — high-power consumers paid more under those conditions"."
