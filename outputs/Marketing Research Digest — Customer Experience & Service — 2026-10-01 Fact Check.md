### Study 1: When the AI is wrong, frontline staff stop thinking like customers
**Work ID:** W7214671269 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Scale of spillover overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "determines whether an AI error costs you one interaction or contaminates the next dozen"
- **Abstract says:** "exposure to incorrect AI recommendations significantly reduces subsequent customer-centric decisions, suggesting that algorithmic fallibility destabilizes trust and disrupts frontline employee decision calibration"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a reduction in subsequent decision quality but gives no magnitude or duration; "the next dozen" invents a quantity the data as described do not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Replace with "or degrades the decisions that follow it"."

**Issue B. Buffering condition omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "simply being exposed to a wrong AI recommendation lowered the quality of employees' subsequent decisions, and prescriptive guidance made that spillover worse"
- **Abstract says:** "Empowerment partially buffers this post-error decline, whereas prescriptive guidance exacerbates it."
- **Problem:** The digest keeps the aggravating factor but drops the matching protective one, making the post-error effect look less conditional than reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "empowerment partly cushioned the drop, while prescriptive guidance made it worse"."

### Study 3: The self-checkout efficiency paradox: the design choices that save money also break service quality
**Work ID:** W7197051994 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Surveillance claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "If self-checkout frequently makes you feel incompetent or surveilled, that experience is a documented feature of how these systems are designed"
- **Abstract says:** "design choices intended to maximize scalability and operational efficiency simultaneously generate socio-technical frictions that destabilize service quality"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes socio-technical frictions and consumer capability misalignment but says nothing about feelings of surveillance or incompetence; unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit to friction and capability mismatch, e.g. "If self-checkout regularly trips you up, that friction is built into the design.""

**Issue B. Usage frequency extrapolated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the costs show up as misalignment with less-capable or less-frequent shoppers"
- **Abstract says:** "alignment between service configurations and heterogeneous consumer capabilities, expectations, and perceptions"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to heterogeneous capabilities and repeated interactions but does not segment consumers by how often they use the systems.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "misalignment with consumers whose capabilities and expectations differ from the design assumptions"."

### Study 4: In an online firestorm, it's the obsessives who spread the damage — not the influencers
**Work ID:** W7213665568 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal phrasing for observational data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "that support measurably makes you post more"
- **Abstract says:** "Peer social support amplifies NWOM only among highly involved users, with no significant effect on influential users."
- **Problem:** This is correlational social-media data; "makes you post more" asserts causation, which the digest's own caveat says is limited.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "peer sympathy went hand in hand with more negative posting among the most involved users"."

**Issue B. "Obsessives" mischaracterizes the construct** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "it's the obsessives who spread the damage"
- **Abstract says:** "user behavioral involvement ... highly involved users spread more NWOM"
- **Problem:** "Behavioral involvement" means posting activity and personal stake in the issue, not obsessiveness; the label adds a pejorative reading not in the construct.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "the highly involved" or "the personally aggrieved" in the headline."

### Study 5: ChatGPT and HR managers read the same guest complaints — and prescribed different fixes

### Study 9: AI delivery tracking reassures shoppers — just not the impatient ones
**Work ID:** W7212038717 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "AI delivery tracking reassures shoppers — just not the impatient ones"
- **Abstract says:** "AEDT significantly increases RI ... The cross-sectional design does not permit definitive causal inference"
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly rules out causal inference; "reassures" reads as an established causal effect, while the body and caveats are correctly hedged.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "AI delivery tracking goes with more repeat buying — just not among the impatient"."

**Issue B. Mediation structure simplified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "mediated by a sense of control and then trust"
- **Abstract says:** "PC and CT each mediate this relationship, and the chained pathway through PC and trust is also significant."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports control and trust each as independent mediators plus a serial path; the digest reports only the serial chain.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "mediated by perceived control and by trust separately, and by the two in sequence"."

### Study 14: Make your service chatbot more capable, not more cuddly
**Work ID:** W7203477409 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Replication count** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In a counterintuitive interaction replicated twice, piling on emotional warmth actually weakened the satisfaction benefit of autonomy by diluting perceptions of competence."
- **Abstract says:** "Study 3 reveals a counterintuitive interaction... Study 4 replicates the interaction found in Study 3, and confirms that the attenuation happens in the translation of autonomous cues into competence perceptions."
- **Problem:** The interaction was found in Study 3 and replicated once, in Study 4; "replicated twice" implies two independent replications.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "an interaction found in one study and replicated in a second"."

**Issue B. Boundary condition dropped from findings sentence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Only autonomous human-likeness raised satisfaction, working through perceived competence; emotional cues raised perceived warmth but not satisfaction, and physical cues did nothing."
- **Abstract says:** "emotional anthropomorphism enhances perceived warmth, which does not translate into higher satisfaction in utilitarian service contexts"
- **Problem:** The abstract scopes the warmth-does-not-help result to utilitarian service contexts; the body sentence states it unconditionally (the limit appears only in Caveats).
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "in utilitarian service settings" to the findings sentence."

### Study 15: When AI fails customers, apologize -- and don't blame the machine
**Work ID:** W7172201743 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Qualifier dropped on apology result** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Apology clearly outperformed excuse-making and denial-style responses across conditions."
- **Abstract says:** "Study 2 (N = 560) demonstrates that the apology strategy most effectively mitigates corporate crisis damage, whereas diminish (e.g. excuse) and denial (e.g. mirror) strategies are suboptimal – though nuanced differences emerge across conditions."
- **Problem:** The abstract specifically notes that nuanced differences emerge across conditions; the digest asserts a uniform advantage "across conditions," which reverses that caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Apology was the most effective overall, though the authors note nuanced differences across conditions.""

**Issue B. Response prescription not tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "integrity-type failures (bias, deception, misuse of data) need a fundamentally harder response than ordinary performance glitches"
- **Abstract says:** "human-agency (vs. machine-agency) AI failures and AI integrity-violations (vs. competence-violations) inflict greater harm to the corporation"
- **Problem:** The abstract shows integrity violations cause more harm, but does not test whether they require a different or stronger response strategy; the examples (bias, deception, data misuse) are also not in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "integrity-type failures caused more damage in this study, which may mean they warrant more than a standard performance-glitch response.""
