### Study 1: Pickup lockers don't cannibalize home delivery — they keep customers from leaving
**Work ID:** W7214672185 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped moderators** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and the benefit shrank when stations were placed close to major offline retail alternatives"
- **Abstract says:** "these positive effects are moderated by: station accessibility, level of nearby offline competition, and product attributes"
- **Problem:** The digest reports only the offline-competition moderator; station accessibility and product attributes (which the abstract ties to "product suitability" implications for managers) are omitted, so readers get a narrower boundary condition than the paper states.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting the effect also depended on station accessibility and on product attributes, per the abstract."

### Study 2: Putting EV chargers in the parking lot lifted German grocery revenue 3.4%
**Work ID:** W7213254140 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unstated direction of moderators** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the data suggest rollout should be prioritized where parking is ample, competition is close, and shoppers live in apartments without home charging"
- **Abstract says:** "provide insights into the role of various store (surface-to-parking ratio, management type) and market (e.g., local competition, apartment-to-house ratio, neighborhood wealth) characteristics"
- **Problem:** The abstract names these characteristics but does not report the direction of their moderating effects, so the specific prioritization advice (ample parking, close competition, apartment-dwellers) is not verifiable from the abstract and reads as an established result.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the paper identifies these store and market characteristics as conditions that shape the effect, without asserting which direction each runs, or mark the prioritization as an inference to be checked in the full paper."

**Issue B. Moderator wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Effects were stronger in stores and neighborhoods with particular characteristics, including surface-to-parking ratio, local competition, and the mix of apartments versus houses."
- **Abstract says:** "They identify several attitudinal (perceived impulsiveness, storability) and behavioral (penetration, purchase frequency) category characteristics that explain where the revenue increase comes from and provide insights into the role of various store ... and market ... characteristics."
- **Problem:** "Effects were stronger in stores and neighborhoods with particular characteristics" implies a tested stronger/weaker split; the abstract only says the authors provide insight into the role of these characteristics.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "the paper examines how store and market characteristics — surface-to-parking ratio, local competition, apartment-to-house ratio — relate to the size of the effect.""

### Study 9: Chinese shoppers reward ESG only when they believe you mean it
**Work ID:** W7214549693 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation share overstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the mediating variable that carried most of the effect here"
- **Abstract says:** "with the indirect effect through identification accounting for 38.3% of the total effect, revealing a "dual-channel structure" in which a direct effect and an indirect, identification-mediated effect coexist"
- **Problem:** 38.3% is a minority of the total effect; the abstract explicitly describes a dual-channel structure in which the direct effect coexists. Saying identification "carried most of the effect" reverses the relative weighting.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "the mediating variable that carried about 38% of the total effect, alongside a direct effect.""

**Issue B. Skeptical consumers vs. skeptical environments** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ESG performance raised purchase intention even among skeptical consumers"
- **Abstract says:** "corporate ESG performance exerts a robust positive effect on purchase intention even in skeptical environments"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to skeptical environments (a market backdrop of greenwashing skepticism), not to a measured segment of skeptical consumers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "even against a backdrop of widespread greenwashing skepticism.""

### Study 10: Hand sanitizer stations still earn their keep: visible hygiene cues soften shoppers' dislike of crowded stores
**Work ID:** W7213558307 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Density vs. perceived crowding** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "crowding lowered shopping satisfaction, but that penalty shrank when visible hygiene cues were present"
- **Abstract says:** "Results showed that higher density reduced satisfaction, but this negative effect was attenuated when sanitary signals were present. Perceived crowding did not mediate the density–satisfaction relationship."
- **Problem:** The satisfaction effect came from manipulated store density; the abstract specifically reports that perceived crowding did not mediate it. Using "crowding" and dropping the null mediation blurs a finding the authors flag.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "higher store density lowered satisfaction — though perceived crowding did not explain the link — and the penalty shrank when hygiene cues were present.""

**Issue B. Country claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Single country (French-language sample)"
- **Abstract says:** "A two-study design was implemented within a single French-language survey (N = 425)."
- **Problem:** The abstract gives the survey language, not a country; a French-language sample could span several countries. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe it as "a single French-language survey pool (country not specified in the abstract).""

### Study 11: The borderless freelance marketplace isn't: four frictions that quietly kill creative work on global platforms
**Work ID:** W7213556593 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline overstates effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "four frictions that quietly kill creative work on global platforms"
- **Abstract says:** "four dualistic tensions, organizing, performing, belonging, and learning , that systematically constrain the creative processes of freelancers"
- **Problem:** "Kill" implies creative work is destroyed; the abstract reports tensions that constrain creative processes, with no measure of output loss.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "constrain" or "hold back" instead of "kill"."

**Issue B. Unverifiable mechanism detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "since platform metrics that reward speed and compliance can suppress the creative risk-taking you are paying for"
- **Abstract says:** "platform mechanisms shape creative realization in cross-border freelancer–client relationships, particularly under culturally heterogeneous service norms and Global North–South economic asymmetries"
- **Problem:** The specific claim about speed- and compliance-rewarding metrics is not stated in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute the specific metric claim to the full paper or soften to "platform mechanisms" as the abstract phrases it. Unverifiable from abstract only."

### Study 12: Blended burgers keep surprising people: review of 79 studies finds hybrid meat wins on taste more often than expected
**Work ID:** W7214004504 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Strongest predictor" overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "sensory appeal was the strongest predictor of liking"
- **Abstract says:** "Sensory appeal emerged as a strong predictor of liking and acceptance"
- **Problem:** The abstract says "a strong predictor," not that it ranked as the strongest among predictors.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "sensory appeal emerged as a strong predictor of liking"."

**Issue B. Unsupported subgroup claim** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Consumers who reject meat substitutes outright often like hybrid products when they actually taste them"
- **Abstract says:** "Across studies, hybrid meat products generally showed high consumer acceptance, liking, and preference, often exceeding expectations."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes no claim about consumers who reject plant-based substitutes; this invents a subgroup finding the review does not report in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "Acceptance of hybrid products was generally high and often exceeded people's expectations" without the rejecter subgroup."

**Issue C. Extrapolated replacement ceiling** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the evidence suggests the ceiling on how much meat you can replace is higher than most product teams assume"
- **Abstract says:** "a wide range of ingredients and non-meat ratios (1% to 80%) were generally well accepted"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the range studied was well accepted; it says nothing about industry assumptions or a ceiling.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "non-meat ratios as high as 80% were generally well accepted in the studies reviewed"."

### Study 14: Why the same live-shopping pitch lands differently on Amazon than on Facebook
**Work ID:** W7214279733 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Relative comparison stated as absolute levels** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Participants assigned to evaluate Amazon rated it high on shopping fit but low on social fit, while Facebook and YouTube showed the reverse pattern."
- **Abstract says:** "Participants evaluating Amazon reported higher TTF and lower STF than those evaluating Facebook or YouTube."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only a relative difference between platforms, not that Amazon scored absolutely "high"/"low" or that the other platforms mirrored the pattern.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "rated Amazon higher on shopping fit and lower on social fit than Facebook or YouTube"."

**Issue B. Causal verb for associational paths** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Both types of fit fed engagement and, through engagement, purchase intention"
- **Abstract says:** "Both forms of fit were positively associated with consumer engagement and, through engagement, with purchase intention."
- **Problem:** "Fed" implies a causal flow; the abstract describes positive associations in a survey-based model.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "were positively associated with engagement and, through engagement, with purchase intention"."

**Issue C. Added interpretation of offsetting effects** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but because Amazon's advantage on one dimension was offset by its deficit on the other, the total indirect effects washed out"
- **Abstract says:** "The two focal serial indirect effects ran in opposite directions and partially offset one another, while the total indirect effects were nonsignificant."
- **Problem:** The abstract states the two indirect effects ran in opposite directions and partially offset; the Amazon-specific explanation is the digest's own reading.
- **Suggested fix:** "State that the two indirect effects ran in opposite directions and partially cancelled, leaving total indirect effects nonsignificant."

### Study 15: Blind boxes inspire two kinds of shoppers — and one of them can't stop
**Work ID:** W7212380221 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Causal and outcome overstatement** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this evidence says the same design that delights most customers reliably tips a thrill-seeking subset toward compulsive buying"
- **Abstract says:** "an inspired-by state positively influences customers' purchase intention, with HP and OP mediating this relationship. Sensation-seeking positively moderates the relationship between the inspired-by state and OP"
- **Problem:** The study is a cross-sectional survey measuring obsessive passion and purchase intention, not compulsive buying behavior; "reliably tips" asserts causation the design cannot support and contradicts the digest's own caveat that causation cannot be established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "sensation-seeking buyers showed a stronger link between inspiration and obsessive passion, a pathway the authors associate with excessive purchasing"."

**Issue B. Unsupported market-growth claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Surprise-and-mystery retail formats — blind boxes, loot crates, mystery drops — are among the fastest-growing merchandising tactics"
- **Abstract says:** "Blind box retailing is becoming a prominent marketing strategy."
- **Problem:** The abstract only calls the format prominent and growing in popularity; the ranking claim about fastest-growing tactics is not supported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "blind box retailing is a fast-growing format, per the authors"."

### Study 16: eBay proved its own star ratings were hiding bad sellers — then rewired search to fix it
**Work ID:** W3121242579 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Direction of bias specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "visible star ratings are systematically too coarse and too flattering to separate good sellers from bad"
- **Abstract says:** "public reputation measures may be biased or too coarse to let buyers accurately infer seller quality"
- **Problem:** The abstract says public measures may be biased or too coarse without specifying that the bias is upward/flattering.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "too flattering" or say "biased and too coarse"."

**Issue B. "Long-run" gains overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a marketplace can raise long-run buyer activity simply by reranking search on a better internal quality signal"
- **Abstract says:** "The treatment shifted buyers toward higher-quality sellers and increased subsequent purchasing without reducing contemporaneous conversion."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports increased subsequent purchasing, not long-run activity; the body text also conflicts with the digest's caveat that persistence is not established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "long-run buyer activity" to "subsequent purchasing"."

**Issue C. Embellished frequency of silent transactions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "including the many transactions where buyers say nothing at all"
- **Abstract says:** "including transactions for which buyers leave no feedback"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not quantify how common no-feedback transactions are.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "including transactions where buyers leave no feedback"."

### Study 17: In livestream selling, more charisma is not better — smiles, looks and volume all peak in the middle
**Work ID:** W7172490860 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported mechanism for the inverted U** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "audiences appear to read excessive expressiveness as a sales tactic, while steady, informative delivery does the persuading"
- **Abstract says:** "nonverbal cues exhibit inverted U-shaped relationships − moderate levels are most effective, with both under- and over-expression reducing sales"
- **Problem:** The abstract documents the shape of the effect but does not test or report viewer inferences about sales tactics.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that over-expression reduced sales without asserting why, or label the interpretation as speculation."

### Study 18: Recommendations feel less manipulative when they come from several AIs instead of one
**Work ID:** W7202143753 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline outcome not measured** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Recommendations feel less manipulative when they come from several AIs instead of one"
- **Abstract says:** "structuring ethical recommendations through a plural AI architecture enhances consumers' self–brand connection. Study 2 demonstrates that plural recommendations increase perceived autonomy"
- **Problem:** Perceived manipulativeness was not among the measured outcomes named in the abstract; the measured constructs were autonomy, self–brand connection and purchase intention.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe headline around feeling more in control/autonomous rather than less manipulated."

**Issue B. Study mode assumed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Online experiments with hypothetical recommendation scenarios and purchase intentions rather than real purchases"
- **Abstract says:** "Across three experiments, this research examines the societal and psychological implications of perceived algorithmic plurality"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not state that the experiments were run online or that scenarios were hypothetical.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "three experiments measuring stated purchase intentions rather than real purchases; delivery mode not specified in the abstract." Unverifiable from abstract only."
