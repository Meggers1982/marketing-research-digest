### Study 1: Robots that show feelings win customers — unless the service involves something private
**Work ID:** W7215001822 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Overstated reversal under high privacy** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The effect reverses in contexts where privacy sensitivity is high: there, emotional expressiveness from the robot suppresses engagement rather than building it."
- **Abstract says:** "robot empathy facilitates consumer escapism and engagement in service environments characterized by low privacy sensitivity but may hinder these outcomes when privacy concerns are high"
- **Problem:** The abstract hedges ("may hinder"); the digest reports a definite reversal and active suppression of engagement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "where privacy sensitivity is high, the authors report that emotional expressiveness may hinder rather than help engagement.""

### Study 3: The hidden cost of heavy AI use at work: employees start cutting corners
**Work ID:** W7220733006 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline reverses the shape of the effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The hidden cost of heavy AI use at work: employees start cutting corners"
- **Abstract says:** "performance pressure mediates the nonlinear relationship between GenAI usage and CCB, such that this nonlinear indirect effect shifts from positive to negative as GenAI usage intensifies"
- **Problem:** The abstract's effect is inverted-U shaped: the indirect effect on corner-cutting turns negative as usage intensifies, so heavy use is not the risk zone — moderate use is. The headline tells skimmers the opposite of what the body (correctly) says.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as something like "The risky middle of AI adoption: moderate GenAI use is when employees start cutting corners.""

**Issue B. Workplace status read as seniority** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the employees most at risk are the senior, visible ones whose work carries the most weight"
- **Abstract says:** "workplace status moderates the inverted U-shaped relationship between GenAI usage and performance pressure. Specifically, high status sharpens this curve, whereas low status flattens it"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to workplace status, which is not defined as seniority or visibility; the digest adds an interpretation not supported by the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "the curve is sharpest among higher-status employees (as the authors measure workplace status)" and drop "senior, visible"."

### Study 5: What makes a voice assistant feel "smart": more things to control, fewer steps to do it

### Study 6: PR professionals describe generative AI as "parasitic": 29 interviews on what the tools are doing to their jobs
**Work ID:** W7220835940 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Quoted word attributed to participants** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "PR professionals describe generative AI as "parasitic""
- **Abstract says:** "Parasitic generative AI: how technologies feed on PR professionals' vulnerabilities [title]; Our findings reveal six prominent themes of vulnerability experienced by PR practitioners"
- **Problem:** "Parasitic" is the authors' own framing in the title; the abstract does not say practitioners used that word.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Researchers call generative AI 'parasitic' on PR professionals' vulnerabilities"."

**Issue B. Specifics beyond the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "practitioners described "strategic interruptions" — uncertainty about their own worth, eroded client relationships and ethical guidelines that lag the tools"
- **Abstract says:** "six prominent themes of vulnerability ... technological, professional value, emotional, relational, governance and developmental aspects ... prompting critical reflection on current ethical guidelines that may not keep pace"
- **Problem:** The abstract names theme labels only; "eroded client relationships" is a specific content claim it does not make, and "strategic interruptions" is the authors' summarizing phrase rather than practitioners' words.
- **Suggested fix:** "List the six themes as labelled and attribute "strategic interruptions" to the authors."

**Issue C. Ethics codes claim hardened** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "current industry ethics codes do not address it"
- **Abstract says:** "current ethical guidelines that may not keep pace with these challenges"
- **Problem:** The abstract hedges that guidelines may not keep pace; the digest asserts they do not address the issue at all.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "current ethics guidance may not keep pace with these challenges.""

**Issue D. Unsupported consumer-angle detail** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and they worry their clients will notice before their employers do"
- **Abstract says:** "[no equivalent statement]"
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only — no finding about practitioners fearing client detection is reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the clause or replace with the abstract-supported point about relational and professional-value vulnerability."

### Study 12: Giving Generative AI to Expert Creators Can Backfire — a Model Says Platforms Should Sometimes Hold Back
**Work ID:** W7214651072 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped qualifier on adoption conclusion** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Maximizing AI adoption is not optimal even when AI raises average productivity."
- **Abstract says:** "Maximizing AI adoption is therefore not always optimal, even when AI raises average productivity."
- **Problem:** Removing "always" turns a conditional result into a blanket rule, which is stronger than the model supports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "Maximizing AI adoption is not always optimal even when AI raises average productivity.""

**Issue B. Unstated claim that novices gain** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Novices gain, but experts can suffer an Einstellung or "expertise-fixation" effect"
- **Abstract says:** "This study examines when subscription-based content platforms should deploy generative AI, given the heterogeneous AI-induced Einstellung effects on novice and expert creators... robustness to heterogeneous expert adoption and positive novice baseline quality."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes heterogeneous Einstellung effects across novices and experts but never states that novices benefit; the digest asserts a directional gain that is unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the Einstellung effect falls unevenly on novice and expert creators, with the modeled friction concentrated on the expert side, rather than asserting novices gain."

### Study 14: Carbon labels on food move only the people who already care
**Work ID:** W7220360598 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Trust mechanism attached to the wrong gap** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "while the unconcerned barely budged — and the gap was largely explained by whether they trusted the information"
- **Abstract says:** "The association between climate attitudes and information internalization is substantially attenuated after accounting for reported trust."
- **Problem:** Trust attenuated the link between climate attitudes and information internalization (updating of self-assessed emissions), not the gap in projected emissions reductions. The digest generalizes a narrow attenuation result to the whole differential response.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the attitude–internalization association was substantially attenuated once reported trust was accounted for, rather than saying trust explained the overall response gap."

**Issue B. Comprehension framed as the rejected explanation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the labels are preaching to the converted, and trust, not comprehension, is the binding constraint"
- **Abstract says:** "underscoring the importance of clear, credible, and actionable carbon-emissions information and of accounting for heterogeneity in consumer receptiveness"
- **Problem:** The study measured trust and internalization; it did not test comprehension against trust, so contrasting the two as competing constraints goes beyond the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say reported trust was associated with how much people internalized the information, without claiming comprehension was ruled out."

**Issue C. Effect estimates stated as established reductions** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "All three formats cut projected emissions on average"
- **Abstract says:** "On average, all three formats produce negative estimated effects on projected emissions, with the largest point estimate observed for the ordinal ranking."
- **Problem:** The abstract hedges with "negative estimated effects" and "point estimate" without asserting statistical significance; "cut" reads as a confirmed reduction.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "All three formats produced negative estimated effects on projected emissions.""
