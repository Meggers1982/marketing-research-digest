### Study 3: A pop-up store on tour cut the band's own online merch sales in the same city
**Work ID:** W7212434558 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Magnitude overstated in reader angle** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Fans who can buy a shirt in person mostly stop buying it online — evidence that people aren't adding purchases across channels so much as choosing between them."
- **Abstract says:** "Results show that the establishment of pop-up stores significantly reduced online purchases by customers in host cities, demonstrating a reverse cannibalization effect in which offline experiential opportunities substitute for digital consumption."
- **Problem:** "Significantly reduced" is a statistically detectable decline of unreported size; "mostly stop buying it online" implies near-total substitution, and the generalization to how "people" behave across channels goes beyond one anonymized band's merchandise shop.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Fans who can buy a shirt in person buy noticeably less online — at least for this one band, suggesting some substitution rather than pure addition across channels.""

### Study 4: Ads designed to be screenshotted get saved — and liked — more
**Work ID:** W7213285496 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation stated more firmly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with perceived future utility acting as the mediating mechanism"
- **Abstract says:** "The results are consistent with perceived future utility as a mediating mechanism"
- **Problem:** The abstract only says results are "consistent with" this mechanism; the digest asserts it as established.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "with results consistent with perceived future utility as the mediating mechanism.""

**Issue B. Moderator path slightly misstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The effect was stronger ... when the ad carried dense, substantive information worth preserving."
- **Abstract says:** "screenshotable design more strongly increased perceived future utility when information density was high rather than low"
- **Problem:** Information density moderated the effect of screenshotable design on perceived future utility, not the overall saving/liking effect as implied.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that high information density strengthened the boost to perceived future utility."

### Study 5: Attacking your own side: why candidates run ads bashing their own party — and when it works
**Work ID:** W7203655820 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Frequency of effect overstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "voters will often set partisan loyalty aside when they judge the defiance makes the candidate more electable"
- **Abstract says:** "showing that consumers can occasionally set partisan loyalty aside in favor of strategic considerations like electability"
- **Problem:** "Often" replaces the abstract's "occasionally", turning a qualified, conditional finding into a routine one; the reader angle repeats this with "many quietly forgive it".
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "voters sometimes set partisan loyalty aside" in both the body and the reader angle."

**Issue B. Election analysis framed as an add-on** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across eight main and eight supplemental studies (N=11,473), plus an analysis of voting outcomes in recent US elections"
- **Abstract says:** "Across eight main studies and eight supplemental studies (N = 11,473), including an analysis of voting outcomes in recent elections"
- **Problem:** The abstract counts the voting-outcome analysis among the studies ("including"); "plus" implies it sits outside the reported set.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "plus" to "including"."

### Study 10: Do banking app savings features actually make people save? Users think so — if they trust the app
**Work ID:** W7202385295 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparison presented as finding** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Also note that trust in the app, not feature sophistication, was the gatekeeper — a brand-equity issue as much as a product one."
- **Abstract says:** "Such an outcome is influenced by the perceived usefulness of these features, users' trust in the app and other external factors."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists perceived usefulness, trust and other external factors as joint influences and never ranks trust above feature quality; the digest manufactures a head-to-head comparison and drops the "other external factors".
- **Suggested fix:** "State that perceived usefulness of the features, trust in the app and other external factors all shaped perceived savings improvement, without ranking them."

**Issue B. Causal claim from cross-sectional survey** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The savings nudges in your banking app work partly because you believe the bank is on your side; if you don't trust the institution, the same auto-save button does much less."
- **Abstract says:** "a survey completed by 215 users measured the effectiveness of mobile banking apps in perceived savings improvements ... influenced by the perceived usefulness of these features, users' trust in the app and other external factors"
- **Problem:** A 215-person survey of perceived improvement cannot establish that low trust causes the feature to do less; the outcome is perception, not observed saving.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as association and perception: "Users who trust their bank are more likely to feel the app's savings tools are helping them.""

**Issue C. Complementarity direction reversed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with financial literacy messaging across channels acting as a complement"
- **Abstract says:** "Financial literacy, promoted through various channels, is complemented by features in mobile banking apps that facilitate perceived improvements in savings."
- **Problem:** The abstract has app features complementing multi-channel financial literacy promotion; the digest flips which element is the complement.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that app features complemented financial literacy promoted across channels."

### Study 11: Money podcasts are doing what pension statements can't: getting young women to act
**Work ID:** W7172533758 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported comparison with institutional communication** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Money podcasts are doing what pension statements can't: getting young women to act"
- **Abstract says:** "personal finance podcasts and online communities provided relatable learning that prompted superannuation actions, with effects contingent on perceived credibility and context"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not compare podcasts against pension statements or any institutional channel, so the implied head-to-head advantage is not something the study reports.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Money podcasts and online communities prompted young Australian women to act on their super" and drop the comparison in the "Why it matters" line."

**Issue B. Contingency narrowed and illustrated with unsupported examples** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but only when the source was seen as credible and the timing fit a life transition"
- **Abstract says:** "with effects contingent on perceived credibility and context"
- **Problem:** The abstract names "credibility and context" as the boundary conditions; the digest converts "context" specifically into life-transition timing, and the story angle adds examples ("a job change or new baby") that are not in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say effects were contingent on perceived credibility and context, noting separately that life-course transitions shaped engagement; remove the invented transition examples or mark them as unverifiable from the abstract."

### Study 14: Solo women travelers get intrusive service — and most say nothing
**Work ID:** W7172243346 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Revenue-loss claim beyond the evidence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "meaning operators' complaint data badly understates the problem they are losing revenue to"
- **Abstract says:** "While SFTs are a lucrative market segment, their experience and by implication, their spending, at service encounters remains largely overlooked."
- **Problem:** The abstract only notes that SFT spending at service encounters is under-researched; it reports no link between intrusive encounters and lost revenue. The body states this as fact even though the digest's own caveat says there is "no quantification of prevalence or spending impact".
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "meaning operators' complaint data likely understates how common these experiences are; the revenue consequences were not measured"."

### Study 15: A single drought explains a fifth of Argentina's GDP swings
**Work ID:** W7203697215 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Variance share attributed to a single event** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A single drought explains a fifth of Argentina's GDP swings"
- **Abstract says:** "Drought shocks account for 21.2% of total GDP volatility, confirming their systemic nature."
- **Problem:** The 21.2% is the share of GDP volatility attributable to drought shocks collectively in a forecast error variance decomposition, not the contribution of any one drought.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Drought shocks explain a fifth of Argentina's GDP swings"."

**Issue B. Dropped qualifier on the inflation buffer** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "while consumer inflation stayed contemporaneously flat, buffered by collapsing incomes and central bank currency intervention"
- **Abstract says:** "consumer inflation remains contemporaneously unresponsive, buffered by severe income contraction in nontradables and active foreign exchange interventions"
- **Problem:** The abstract specifies income contraction in the nontradables sector; the digest generalizes this to "collapsing incomes".
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "buffered by a severe income contraction in nontradables and active foreign exchange intervention"."
