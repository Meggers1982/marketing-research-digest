### Study 4: Subsidize pickup, not delivery: airport e-commerce data shows where discounts actually pay off
**Work ID:** W7224921670 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline understates instant delivery** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Subsidize pickup, not delivery: airport e-commerce data shows where discounts actually pay off"
- **Abstract says:** "The estimated subsidy coefficient is largest for in-store pickup, followed by instant delivery and home delivery... Airport operators may prioritize subsidies for in-store pickup and instant delivery"
- **Problem:** The headline's "not delivery" implies delivery subsidies do not pay, but the authors recommend prioritising both pickup and instant delivery; only home delivery showed the weakest response.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Subsidize pickup and fast local delivery over home delivery"."

**Issue B. Imputed platform motive** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "platforms price-steer you toward the fulfillment method that is cheapest and fastest for them, which is often walking to the counter yourself"
- **Abstract says:** "This study examines how the relationship between subsidy intensity and store sales varies across fulfillment modes in airport e-commerce... The estimated subsidy coefficient is largest for in-store pickup"
- **Problem:** The study measured how sales responded to subsidies by fulfilment mode; it did not test platform intent or whether subsidies were deliberately set to steer shoppers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as an observation about where discounts generate the most sales, not about platforms' deliberate steering strategy."

### Study 5: Seven years of survey waves suggest shoppers who notice a retailer's inclusion efforts trust it more
**Work ID:** W7225631783 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "longitudinal" label for repeated cross-sections** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this gives a longitudinal counterpoint: the trust pathway held up across seven years of shifting political climate"
- **Abstract says:** "Using a multi-wave repeated cross-sectional survey fielded across seven annual waves from 2019 to 2025"
- **Problem:** Repeated cross-sections of different respondents are not a longitudinal design; the body text uses a label the caveats correctly disown.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "repeated annual snapshots" or "evidence across seven annual waves" in the body text."

**Issue B. Subgroup claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "but only among consumers who were actually aware of the initiatives"
- **Abstract says:** "results show that awareness of DEIA initiatives is positively associated with trust, and trust is strongly associated with loyalty"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports an association between awareness and trust; it does not report a conditional test showing the pathway holds only for aware consumers.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "the pathway runs through awareness of the initiatives" rather than implying a tested subgroup boundary."

### Study 10: How you announce your relationship online may shape what you buy next
**Work ID:** W7225714650 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Urgency moderator overstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The finding that an urgency cue wiped out the effect is the most actionable detail — scarcity messaging appears to override subtle-aesthetic preferences"
- **Abstract says:** "A marketplace urgency cue also moderates this process, such that the indirect effect holds when the cue is absent but diminishes when it is present."
- **Problem:** The abstract says the indirect effect diminishes with an urgency cue, not that it is eliminated; the digest builds its headline recommendation on the stronger claim.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the effect "was weakened when a marketplace urgency cue was present" and soften the accompanying recommendation."

**Issue B. Moderators attached to total rather than indirect effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The effect strengthened among people with higher relationship privacy orientation and disappeared when a marketplace urgency cue was present."
- **Abstract says:** "the indirect effect of soft launch on minimalist consumption via preference for subtle self-presentation strengthens as relation privacy increases... the indirect effect holds when the cue is absent but diminishes when it is present"
- **Problem:** Both moderators in the abstract apply specifically to the indirect (mediated) effect, not to the overall effect as the digest implies.
- **Suggested fix:** "Specify that the moderators qualify the indirect effect running through preference for subtle self-presentation."

### Study 11: China's ESG gap: firms follow the public on social issues and ignore regulators on the environment
**Work ID:** W7224526178 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal framing of a correlational index** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Public attention appears to function as a real governance force: the issues people search for are the issues companies move on."
- **Abstract says:** "The results show that Policy–Corporate Distance is consistently the largest... while firms align more closely with public attention in social issues."
- **Problem:** The study measures structural distance/alignment between policy, public search and firm ESG ratings; it does not establish that public search attention causes firms to act. The digest's own caveat says the work 'documents gaps rather than proving why firms behave this way', which this line partly contradicts.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to alignment language, e.g. 'Corporate behaviour lines up most closely with public attention on social issues — though the study cannot show that public concern is what moved firms.'"

### Study 13: Why people rent luxury handbags: status in Australia, sustainability in China
**Work ID:** W7223000149 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Category narrowed to handbags** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Why people rent luxury handbags: status in Australia, sustainability in China"
- **Abstract says:** "access‐based consumption such as luxury rental having gained momentum as an alternative retail channel... among current luxury renters"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to luxury rental generally, not handbags; the product category of the rentals studied is not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to 'Why people rent luxury goods' or 'luxury rental'."

**Issue B. Headline implies categorical motive split** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "status in Australia, sustainability in China"
- **Abstract says:** "both motivations act as antecedents of self‐congruity... sustainability motivation has stronger effects on brand experience among Chinese renters, while social recognition motivation has stronger effects on brand personality congruity and brand user imagery congruity among Australian renters"
- **Problem:** Both motivations operated in both markets; the difference is in the relative strength of specific paths, not an either/or split by country. The body text handles this correctly, but the headline reads as a clean dichotomy.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to e.g. 'status weighs more heavily in Australia, sustainability in China'."

### Study 14: Shoppers' mood changes how much augmented reality actually sells
**Work ID:** W7225315487 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Intention framed as sales in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Shoppers' mood changes how much augmented reality actually sells"
- **Abstract says:** "AR features leading to continuous usage intention (CUI) and online purchase intention (OPI)"
- **Problem:** The measured outcome is stated online purchase intention, not sales. The body and caveats make this clear, but the headline implies actual revenue effects.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to 'changes how much augmented reality moves buying intent'."

**Issue B. Specific moderation path not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the shopper's mood moderated the relationship between AR features and buying intent"
- **Abstract says:** "The results demonstrate the important role of CUI, the moderating role of mood, and the configurations of variables"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports 'the moderating role of mood' without specifying which relationship it moderates; the digest fills in the path. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say 'mood acted as a moderator in the model' without specifying the path, or attribute the detail to the full text."

**Issue C. Feature-richness comparison not tested in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "its commercial payoff depends less on feature richness than on whether users want to come back to the tool"
- **Abstract says:** "The results demonstrate the important role of CUI, the moderating role of mood..."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not report a comparison that ranks continued-usage intention above AR feature richness; this is the digest's inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as 'continued-usage intention emerged as an important link to purchase intention' without the implied ranking against features."

### Study 15: The wedding purchase retailers keep getting wrong: bridal lingerie in India
**Work ID:** W7225430644 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific retail fixes not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "pointing to fixes — private fitting, trained staff, discreet online fulfillment — that are operational rather than promotional"
- **Abstract says:** "Insights from this study can help lingerie brands to gradually break societal taboos surrounding this category, delivering a superior retail experience and adopt inclusive retail practices"
- **Problem:** The named fixes are the digest's own suggestions, not implications stated in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute them explicitly as the digest's suggestions, e.g. 'possible responses — private fitting, trained staff, discreet fulfilment — follow from, rather than being tested in, the study.'"

**Issue B. Overreach from 15 interviews** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "this qualitative work suggests retail design, not demand, is the binding constraint in some markets"
- **Abstract says:** "While the purchase evoked anticipation and confidence; cultural taboos, family conservatism and retail barriers also generated embarrassment."
- **Problem:** The abstract lists retail barriers as one of three sources of embarrassment alongside cultural taboos and family conservatism; it makes no claim about retail design being the limiting factor on demand, and an interpretive 15-person study cannot support that.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as 'the retail environment was one source of discomfort shoppers described, alongside cultural taboos and family involvement'."

**Issue C. Unsupported margin claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "an active source of shopper discomfort in a high-margin category"
- **Abstract says:** "(no information on category margins)"
- **Problem:** The abstract says nothing about bridal lingerie margins. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop 'high-margin' or source it separately."
