### Study 1: Giving food waste a face: talking carrots and smiling nuggets make upcycled food feel better-made
**Work ID:** W7226018155 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline product not in study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "talking carrots and smiling nuggets make upcycled food feel better-made"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 1 (N = 211) compared anthropomorphized and nonanthropomorphized advertisements for upcycled ice cream... Study 2 (N = 205) replicated this design using upcycled chicken nuggets"
- **Problem:** The stimuli were upcycled ice cream and chicken nuggets; no carrot product was tested, so the headline invents a category.
- **Suggested fix:** "Swap "talking carrots" for the actual stimuli, e.g. "chatty ice cream and smiling nuggets"."

**Issue B. Untested comparison in Why it matters** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a mascot or humanlike packaging voice is a cheap creative lever that appears to work through perceived quality rather than guilt or sustainability appeals"
- **Abstract says:** "Studies 1 and 2 revealed significant indirect effects of anthropomorphism on purchase intention, willingness to pay, and self-reported coupon usage through perceived warmth or competence and perceived quality."
- **Problem:** The studies compared anthropomorphized vs. non-anthropomorphized ads; they did not test guilt or sustainability appeals as an alternative route, so the contrast is unsupported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the effect ran through warmth/competence and perceived quality, without implying a comparison to guilt or sustainability appeals."

### Study 2: The raw carrot test: a 50-person study finds one simple food predicts how fast older adults eat everything else
**Work ID:** W7225292747 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal wording on eating rate** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Eating rate drives how much people eat and how full they feel"
- **Abstract says:** "Eating rate (ER) is a significant predictor of food intake and satiety"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes eating rate as a predictor, not an established cause of intake and satiety.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Eating rate predicts how much people eat and how full they feel"."

**Issue B. Strength of consistency overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Eating rate was highly consistent within individuals across foods"
- **Abstract says:** "ER showed good consistency across all foods."
- **Problem:** "Highly consistent" upgrades the abstract's "good consistency".
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "showed good consistency across foods"."

**Issue C. Method detail not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "annotated their oral processing behavior frame by frame"
- **Abstract says:** "while video-recorded for behavioural annotation of oral processing, including ER"
- **Problem:** Frame-by-frame annotation is not specified in the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "video-recorded for behavioural annotation of oral processing" or mark as unverifiable from the abstract."

### Study 3: Fusion products don't just get rated — they may make consumers feel better, and Chinese shoppers want to know you did it respectfully
**Work ID:** W7225801646 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Experiments conflated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "two experiments manipulated culture-mixing exposure across five product categories and manipulated concerns about cultural respect"
- **Abstract says:** "Study 3, an experiment manipulating culture-mixing exposure across five product categories... Study 4 additionally manipulates concerns about cultural respect"
- **Problem:** The five-category manipulation belongs to Study 3 only; Study 4 added the cultural-respect manipulation. The digest reads as if both experiments did both.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe Study 3 and Study 4 separately."

**Issue B. Moderator generalized in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Chinese shoppers want to know you did it respectfully"
- **Abstract says:** "reveals that concerns about cultural respect selectively amplify the self-expression pathway in China but not in the U.S."
- **Problem:** The finding is that respect concerns amplify one pathway among Chinese survey respondents, not that Chinese shoppers in general demand respectful sourcing.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "cultural-respect concerns amplified the self-expression route in China but not the US"."

### Study 4: "Natural" food beats "medicinal" shakes: why older adults resist nutrition supplements
**Work ID:** W7222525261 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Age comparison misstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "older participants were far less willing than younger ones to use either method preventively rather than as a treatment"
- **Abstract says:** "with older adults much less willing to consume either method to prevent malnutrition than to treat it"
- **Problem:** The abstract's comparison is within older adults (prevention vs. treatment), not older adults versus younger participants.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Older participants were much less willing to use either method to prevent malnutrition than to treat it.""

**Issue B. Sales claim unsupported** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The oral nutritional supplement category is losing sales to a positioning problem, not a taste or novelty problem"
- **Abstract says:** "Willingness to consume was assessed using visual analogue scales... Specific attitudes relating to "not seeing the point" or "valuing the benefits" of each method were the strongest predictors of willingness to consume"
- **Problem:** The study measured stated willingness in a hypothetical survey; no sales or market data were collected, so a claim about the category losing sales goes beyond the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** ""Stated willingness to use supplements is driven by framing and perceived benefit, not by taste or fear of novelty — a positioning hypothesis worth testing in market.""

**Issue C. Country not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Researchers surveyed 454 adults in the UK (mean age 47, 230 women)"
- **Abstract says:** "this study examined healthy adults' ( n = 454, M age = 47, female = 230) views"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not identify the country. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "in the UK" or attribute the location to the full text."

### Study 5: Consumers judge how "processed" a food is by counting the ingredients list
**Work ID:** W7162453624 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Correlation described as prediction plus added valence** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Shoppers appear to run a crude heuristic — short list equals natural equals good"
- **Abstract says:** "we observed strong correlations between the products' number of ingredients and their processing and naturalness assessments... the use of the number of ingredients can be viewed as a heuristic"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports correlations between ingredient count and naturalness/processing ratings; it does not measure whether shorter lists are judged "good" or healthier.
- **Suggested fix:** ""short list equals natural" — and note the correlational ranking-task basis."

### Study 7: Vapes that act like smartphones are linked to heavier use and stronger nicotine dependence in young people
**Work ID:** W7225230645 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dependence link overgeneralized** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this survey links each of those features to vaping more often and scoring higher on dependence"
- **Abstract says:** "Positive associations were similarly observed for the aforementioned features and NDS scores, with the exception of no significant relationship for e-liquid level indicator (β=0.03, p=0.481) and coil temperature controls (β=0.03, p=0.590)"
- **Problem:** E-liquid level indicators and coil temperature controls were not significantly associated with dependence scores, so "each of those features" overstates the dependence link.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "most of those features" or note the two exceptions."

**Issue B. Adjustment set incomplete** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "After adjusting for demographics, financial situation and other tobacco use"
- **Abstract says:** "adjusting for demographics, financial situation, other tobacco use and downloading a smart vape app"
- **Problem:** One covariate (downloading a smart vape app) is omitted.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "and whether they had downloaded a smart vape app"."

### Study 8: Thais who read food labels and seek out nutrition information ate more high-sodium food, not less
**Work ID:** W7225723597 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested ranking of constructs** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "intention and ingrained habit matter more than knowledge"
- **Abstract says:** "attitude towards intention to eat healthy food, were associated with lower odds of consumption, while attitude towards the benefits of healthy eating and behaviour related to food label use and information-seeking, were associated with higher odds"
- **Problem:** The abstract reports direction of associations for specific food-literacy dimensions; it does not compare the relative importance of intention, habit and knowledge.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say that different food-literacy dimensions pointed in opposite directions, without ranking intention/habit against knowledge."

**Issue B. Behavioural wording in headline** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "ate more high-sodium food, not less"
- **Abstract says:** "Sodium intake was assessed using the frequencies of self-reported eating instant food, packaged snack and adding high-sodium discretionary condiments"
- **Problem:** The outcome is self-reported frequency in a cross-sectional survey, so "ate more" reads as a measured behavioural difference.
- **Suggested fix:** ""report eating more high-sodium food, not less"."

### Study 9: Fear of new foods — not taste sensitivity — is the trait that best predicts what alcohol people like and drink
**Work ID:** W7223553671 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Prediction wording** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Food neophobia was the most consistent predictor of alcohol liking, familiarity and recent consumption"
- **Abstract says:** "Food neophobia emerged as the factor most consistently associated with alcohol liking, familiarity, and recent consumption"
- **Problem:** Cross-sectional associations are described as prediction, and the headline's "best predicts" goes further.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "most consistently associated with" in both headline and body."

**Issue B. Sex-variation caveat dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "emotional eating and reward sensitivity were tied to sweet and socially consumed drinks"
- **Abstract says:** "Emotional eating and sensitivity to reward were also associated with alcohol-related behaviors, especially for sweet and alcoholic beverages typically consumed in social settings, although the pattern of associations differed across the sex-stratified analyses"
- **Problem:** The abstract notes these patterns were not stable across the sex-stratified samples; the digest presents them as uniform.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that the emotional-eating and reward patterns differed between the sex-stratified analyses."

### Study 10: For 'adopt-a-farm' food services, upbeat framing beat warning framing — and trust mattered more than either
**Work ID:** W7225611263 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Trust effect stated more firmly than abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "though trust had a strong direct effect on perceived benefits"
- **Abstract says:** "context-specific trust has a strong positive main effect on perceived benefits, suggesting a general positive association with benefit appraisals rather than a framing-specific moderating effect"
- **Problem:** The authors explicitly hedge this as a contemporaneous association (trust was measured post-exposure in the same session), not a clean causal effect.
- **Suggested fix:** ""though trust was strongly associated with perceived benefits" — consistent with the caveat already noted."

### Study 12: Australia's black market now moves most of its tobacco — and modeling says crackdowns could cut smoking nearly in half
**Work ID:** W7154486028 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Illicit share stated as a clear majority** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Australia's black market now moves most of its tobacco"
- **Abstract says:** "50-60% of the tobacco market is now illicit"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives a range whose lower bound is exactly half, so "most" (and the consumer angle's "a market that is now bigger than the legal one") presents an estimate range as a settled majority.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use the abstract's range, e.g. "roughly half to 60% of Australia's tobacco market is now illicit"."

**Issue B. Uncertainty interval on deaths averted omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "an estimated 4,840 deaths averted over 20 years"
- **Abstract says:** "averting 4,840 deaths over 20 years (95% uncertainty interval 4,050–5,800)"
- **Problem:** A modelled point estimate is reported without its uncertainty interval, making the projection look more precise than it is.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "(95% uncertainty interval 4,050–5,800)"."

### Study 13: Why an unmet need makes a product feel suddenly, vividly desirable
**Work ID:** W7222560284 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Design and number of studies not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Across a series of experiments, researchers tested why consumers value objects more when those objects address a current need."
- **Abstract says:** "This research examines concrete construal level as one mechanism of need-based object valuation. We show that an unfulfilled need instantiates a concrete construal of need-relevant objects…"
- **Problem:** The abstract never states that the work comprised experiments or how many; the digest asserts a multi-experiment design. Unverifiable from abstract only (the caveat hedges this but the body text states it flatly).
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the researchers report evidence that…" or note explicitly that the abstract does not specify the number or type of studies."

### Study 14: Smart gym kit doesn't make members happier directly — it works by making them love the gym
**Work ID:** W7221082334 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediation presented as full mediation** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Smart gym kit doesn't make members happier directly — it works by making them love the gym"
- **Abstract says:** "each of the three attributes (intelligence, connectivity and aesthetics) was positively associated with perceived value, which in turn was linked to brand love and subjective well-being. Moreover, brand love served as a positive mediator between perceived value and subjective well-being."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports perceived value linked to subjective well-being as well as brand love acting as a mediator; it does not say the direct path is absent. The headline (and "not from the machines alone") implies full mediation that the abstract does not establish.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase to "Smart gym kit lifts well-being partly by making members love the gym" or "the well-being link runs largely through perceived value and brand love"."

**Issue B. Country context not stated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Single fitness center, single country context and one equipment brand (Technogym)"
- **Abstract says:** "The study included a three-wave panel design, where new members of a fitness center (n = 372) participated in a survey."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not name a country, so the single-country framing is unverifiable from abstract only (though it is listed as a caveat, not a finding).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the abstract does not report the country or setting beyond a single fitness center."
