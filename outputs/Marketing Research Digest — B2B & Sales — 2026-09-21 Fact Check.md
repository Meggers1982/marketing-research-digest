### Study 3: Hard-closing salespeople damage buyer trust — and the buyers say so themselves
**Work ID:** W7172448511 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal headline for cross-sectional survey** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Hard-closing salespeople damage buyer trust — and the buyers say so themselves"
- **Abstract says:** "transactional selling behaviour is negatively associated with both trust dimensions … The cross-sectional and regionally bounded sample limits generalizability."
- **Problem:** "Damage" asserts causation from a cross-sectional matched survey; the abstract states only a negative association, and the digest's own caveat concedes causal direction is unproven.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Hard-closing salespeople are linked to lower buyer trust — and the buyers say so themselves.""

### Study 4: Keeping the same account team in place improves the customer experience — but discounts can undercut it
**Work ID:** W7171153525 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Financial incentives" recast as "discounts"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Before defending margin with discounts in strategic accounts, consider whether that spend is diluting the advisory value you've built"
- **Abstract says:** "the use of financial incentives jointly shape CX and subsequent buyer behaviour … Financial incentives weaken the link between sagacious experience and behaviour but enhance the effect of social experience."
- **Problem:** The abstract measures financial incentives generally (which could include rebates, bonuses or volume terms), not discounting specifically; the headline and angle both substitute "discounts."
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "financial incentives" and note that the abstract does not specify which incentive types were studied."

**Issue B. Sagacious-experience effect understated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "though not on their "sagacious" (insight-based) experience"
- **Abstract says:** "while limiting the impact on sagacious experience"
- **Problem:** "Limiting the impact" implies team stability dampened the offering–sagacious experience link, which is a different result from simply having no effect.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "while limiting the effect on their 'sagacious' (insight-based) experience.""

**Issue C. Moderation described as a main effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Keeping the same account team in place improves the customer experience"
- **Abstract says:** "the stability of the supplier's interacting team strengthens the influence of supplier offerings on CX"
- **Problem:** Team stability is reported as a moderator that strengthens the offering–CX link, not as a direct driver of customer experience.
- **Suggested fix:** "Headline could read "Keeping the same account team in place amplifies what a supplier's offering does for customer experience.""

### Study 5: What Salespeople Say — and When They Say It — Decides Who Converts
**Work ID:** W7203901346 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Superlative claim not in the abstract** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the discomfort you feel when a rep pushes a concrete "so can we book Tuesday?" late in a conversation is measurable — and it is the single biggest documented killer of the sale"
- **Abstract says:** "For instance, more specific (compared to less specific) nudges increase lead receptivity by 11.82% earlier in the conversation but reduce it by 81.40% later in the conversation."
- **Problem:** The abstract presents the late-nudge effect as one illustrative example of a turn-level effect on lead receptivity, not on sales outcomes, and makes no claim that it is the largest documented factor in lost sales.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "single biggest documented killer of the sale" and say the late specific nudge was associated with a sharp drop in the lead's receptivity in that turn."

**Issue B. Paralinguistic label expanded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "paralinguistic ones (volume intensity, speech rate, pitch)"
- **Abstract says:** "paralinguistic acts (intensity, speech rate, pitch)"
- **Problem:** The abstract says "intensity" without specifying volume; the interpretation is plausible but not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "intensity, speech rate, pitch" as in the abstract."

### Study 6: Three-Quarters of a Japanese Luxury Resale Chain's Revenue Came From Visitors, Not Locals
**Work ID:** W7204571119 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline scope of the 74% figure** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Three-Quarters of a Japanese Luxury Resale Chain's Revenue Came From Visitors, Not Locals"
- **Abstract says:** "Non-resident buyers carry 74% of consumer revenue (47.9–65.5% under stricter procurement exclusions)."
- **Problem:** The 74% applies to consumer revenue only (the study separately analyzes a corporate wholesale channel), and "visitors" implies tourists when the abstract states the tourist-versus-daigou composition is bounded, not resolved.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "three-quarters of consumer revenue came from non-resident buyers" and keep the tourist/daigou ambiguity visible in the headline or standfirst."

### Study 7: Salespeople Proud of Their Employer Sell More — Unless They're Prouder of Their Profession
**Work ID:** W7202160517 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderation result restated as a main effect** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "though professional identification did not affect turnover intentions"
- **Abstract says:** "However, this moderating effect does not extend to turnover intentions."
- **Problem:** The abstract says professional identification did not moderate the identification–turnover link; it makes no claim about whether professional identification affects turnover intentions at all.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "though professional identification did not moderate the link to turnover intentions.""

**Issue B. Direction of the turnover effect assumed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Prestige improved sales performance and reduced turnover intentions indirectly, through salespeople identifying with the organization."
- **Abstract says:** "retailer organizational prestige indirectly affects sales performance and turnover intentions through organizational identification"
- **Problem:** The abstract states an indirect effect without specifying the direction for turnover intentions; "reduced" (and "improved") is an inference and also reads causally for a cross-sectional survey.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was indirectly associated with sales performance and turnover intentions" unless the direction can be confirmed from the full text."

### Study 8: An AI model beat seasoned B2B sales pros at calling which deals would close
**Work ID:** W7204437044 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Generalizing one firm's expert panel to expert judgment at large** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "expert gut feel in complex commercial negotiations is often near-random"
- **Abstract says:** "The model achieved balanced accuracies of 83.9% on the full dataset and 74.1% on a regional subset, substantially outperforming expert predictions, which reached 63.0 and 54.4%, respectively."
- **Problem:** The 54.4% figure comes from one regional subset at a single industrial partner; extending it to expert intuition "in complex commercial negotiations" generally goes beyond the evidence and sits awkwardly with the digest's own generalizability caveat.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the claim to this setting, e.g. "in this firm's regional cases, expert judgment was little better than chance.""

### Study 9: Meta-analysis: online product pages persuade along two separate tracks, and emotion doesn't work the way marketers assume
**Work ID:** W7204660594 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Mediator specified beyond the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "emotional responses only work indirectly, fully routed through attitude toward the product or brand"
- **Abstract says:** "the effect of emotion is fully mediated by consumer attitude"
- **Problem:** The abstract says "consumer attitude" without specifying product or brand attitude; the digest adds that distinction and then builds a measurement recommendation on it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "fully routed through consumer attitude" and drop the product/brand specification."

**Issue B. Presentation formats listed but not named in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "fragmented studies on online product presentation — images, video, zoom, interactive and immersive displays"
- **Abstract says:** "Online product presentation is the e-commerce equivalent of the salesperson … in an increasingly immersive, technology driven retail environment."
- **Problem:** The specific formats included in the meta-analysis are not listed in the abstract. Unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Either drop the list or attribute it as an example of the formats such research typically covers."

### Study 10: When suppliers play manipulative games, tighter contracts make distributors behave worse, not better
**Work ID:** W7208824278 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Untested inference about when contracts help** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the study implies that contract tightening works only when trust is intact, and that relationship investment is the better lever once a partner suspects manipulation"
- **Abstract says:** "contract completeness, market uncertainty and industry competitive intensity strengthen the positive relationship between manufacturer Machiavellianism and distributor opportunism … Relationship embeddedness mitigates this effect."
- **Problem:** The study tested contract completeness as a moderator of the Machiavellianism–opportunism link; it did not test whether contracts work when trust is intact, nor did it compare relational investment against contracting as interventions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit the angle to what was found: more complete contracts were associated with more, not less, opportunism where manipulation was perceived, while embedded relationships buffered it."
