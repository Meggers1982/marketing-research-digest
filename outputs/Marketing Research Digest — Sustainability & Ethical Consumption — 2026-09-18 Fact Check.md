### Study 4: How the community that helps you save can end up trapping you
**Work ID:** W7211969452 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped boundary condition on repair work** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Members work continuously to repair those misalignments, but that repair work itself reproduces the dependencies, deepening what the paper calls consumer entrapment."
- **Abstract says:** "Consumers continually work to realign these misalignments, yet under specific conditions, these efforts reproduce the contingencies that sustain participation while progressively deepening entrapment."
- **Problem:** The abstract limits the entrapping effect to "specific conditions"; the digest presents it as an automatic outcome of all repair work.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add the qualifier: "under certain conditions, that repair work itself reproduces the dependencies.""

### Study 5: Small firms that trust their partners most are the slowest to adopt AI
**Work ID:** W7213367223 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline states adoption behavior, study measured intention** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small firms that trust their partners most are the slowest to adopt AI"
- **Abstract says:** "high trust within cross-sector partnerships is significantly associated with lower AI adoption intentions"
- **Problem:** The headline describes observed adoption speed; the survey measured self-reported adoption intentions only, and the association is correlational.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to something like "Small firms that trust their partners most report the least intention to adopt AI.""

### Study 6: Consumers will pay more for "sustainable" beer — but Fair Trade labels actually cost you money
**Work ID:** W7204076930 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Baseline-group WTP described as an average** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A generic "Sustainable" claim earned a premium of €0.68 on average, rising to €2.18 among consumers who were both health-oriented and active in sports."
- **Abstract says:** "the "Sustainable" claim follows a lifestyle-driven value ladder: WTP increases from +€0.68 in the baseline group to +€2.18 among consumers with both high health orientation and sports participation"
- **Problem:** €0.68 is the value for the baseline (non-LOHAS) group at the bottom of a value ladder, not a sample-wide mean.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "on average" to "in the baseline group.""

**Issue B. Unsupported explanation for null recyclable-glass result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** ""recyclable" glass is so standard that shoppers place no value on it"
- **Abstract says:** "Packaging effects are asymmetric: reusable glass receives a clear premium (+€0.35), whereas recyclable glass shows no significant valuation."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports the null result but offers no explanation; the "so standard" reasoning is the digest's own speculation presented as study content.
- **Suggested fix:** "State the finding without the causal explanation, or mark it explicitly as a possible interpretation."

### Study 7: Does corporate social responsibility make people consume better — or just consume more guilt-free?

- **Abstract says:** "Green marketing positively influences brand loyalty, which further influences consumer sustainable action (CSA)."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not test or report a direct green-marketing-to-CSA path, so "mainly indirectly" implies a comparison the abstract does not make.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "the modelled route runs through brand loyalty, which in turn predicts consumer sustainable action.""

### Study 8: In 14,579 Vietnamese firms, stacking digital tools on top of each other starts to backfire
**Work ID:** W7204753244 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline mischaracterizes the interaction tested** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In 14,579 Vietnamese firms, stacking digital tools on top of each other starts to backfire"
- **Abstract says:** "Our findings show that DT and IC enhance MP, while their interaction reveals diminishing returns. Excessive integration between DT and structural capital efficiency and capital employed efficiency decreases MP"
- **Problem:** The diminishing returns concern the interaction between digital technology and intellectual-capital components, not the accumulation of multiple digital tools.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe the headline around digital investment combined with heavy structural and capital-employed intensity, not tool stacking."

**Issue B. "Outpaces the workforce" interpretation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this dataset suggests returns to digital tooling turn negative when it outpaces the workforce"
- **Abstract says:** "Excessive integration between DT and structural capital efficiency and capital employed efficiency decreases MP, while the interaction between DT and human capital efficiency enhances MP."
- **Problem:** The abstract shows negative interactions with structural and capital-employed efficiency, which is not the same as technology outrunning workforce capability; that is the digest's gloss.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe the actual moderators, or label the workforce reading as the digest's interpretation."

### Study 10: Tying executive bonuses to ESG goals appears to lower a company's reputational risk
**Work ID:** W7213035748 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Behavioral claim attributed to the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the data suggest firms that do it subsequently behave less badly, if only because they have raised the bar they will be judged against"
- **Abstract says:** "CSR contracting signals a firm's social commitment and raises stakeholder expectations that the firm will act responsibly. Firms tend to avoid violating stakeholder expectations by reducing their irresponsible behavior, thus lowering reputational risk. We further demonstrate that this relationship is partially mediated by reduced stakeholder pressure."
- **Problem:** Reduced irresponsible behavior is the authors' theoretical mechanism; the measured outcomes are reputational risk and stakeholder pressure, so "the data suggest firms subsequently behave less badly" overstates what was tested and implies a temporal causal sequence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Attribute it to the authors' interpretation: "the authors argue that raising the bar pushes firms to avoid irresponsible behavior, though the data measure reputational risk rather than conduct.""

### Study 11: Religious young shoppers buy secondhand clothes for the thrill and the status, not out of moral duty
**Work ID:** W7213254319 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Country named but not stated in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A survey of 316 religious young Muslims in Indonesia who had previously bought secondhand fashion online"
- **Abstract says:** "a survey was conducted among 316 religious young Muslims with prior online second-hand fashion purchase experience ... in the world's largest Muslim population that has significant growth of e-retailing activities"
- **Problem:** The abstract never names Indonesia; it only refers to "the world's largest Muslim population." The inference is reasonable but is not verifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "in the world's largest Muslim-majority market (described in the paper as such)" or note that the country is inferred."

**Issue B. Direction of the moderation not specified** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "though familiarity with the circular economy did strengthen the link between spirituality and that sense of responsibility"
- **Abstract says:** "Familiarity with CE moderates the relationship between spirituality and consumer felt responsibility for sustainability."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports only that familiarity moderates the path, not that it strengthens it; the direction is unverifiable from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "familiarity with the circular economy moderated the link between spirituality and that sense of responsibility.""

### Study 12: Say what you did or what you achieved — but don't do both in the same sustainability post
**Work ID:** W7170105320 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Hedge dropped on the combined-frame backfire** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "combining them in one message backfired because the two frames clash"
- **Abstract says:** "but using them jointly may backfire due to incongruence"
- **Problem:** The abstract hedges with "may backfire"; the digest reports the backfire as an established, unconditional result, and the headline turns it into a directive.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "combining them in one message may backfire because the two frames clash" and soften the headline (e.g., "mixing both in one post may backfire")."

**Issue B. Rule-of-thumb framing from one paper** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this gives a concrete rule of thumb: pick one frame per post and match the emotional tone to it"
- **Abstract says:** "Across seven studies ... we demonstrate that both frames increase engagement via distinct mechanisms, but using them jointly may backfire due to incongruence."
- **Problem:** Presents a hedged finding from a single multi-study paper as a ready-made communication rule, edging toward playbook language.
- **Suggested fix:** "Frame as a hypothesis to test ("suggests testing single-frame posts against combined ones") rather than a rule of thumb."

### Study 13: People who pay to upgrade their fitness app track more — for about a month, and still don't lose weight
**Work ID:** W4312971961 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Duration specified more precisely than the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "for about a month, and still don't lose weight"
- **Abstract says:** "these effects attenuate within several weeks and do not translate into sustained weight loss"
- **Problem:** "Several weeks" is converted into "about a month" and later into a specific "week four" intervention point, which the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "within a few weeks" and drop the specific week-four reference or mark it as illustrative."

**Issue B. Selection share overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this is a warning that most of that gap is selection plus a novelty window"
- **Abstract says:** "Sensitivity analysis and matched-sample designs confirm that the engagement responses are not solely driven by time-varying selection ... failing to account for endogeneity and selection in premium adoption leads to a substantial overstatement of its effects"
- **Problem:** The abstract explicitly reports that engagement effects are not solely a selection artifact; saying "most of that gap is selection" goes beyond "naive estimates substantially overstate."
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "a substantial part of that gap reflects who self-selects into paying, plus a short novelty window.""

**Issue C. Alternative explanation dropped** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a pattern the authors read as novelty wearing off rather than sunk-cost motivation"
- **Abstract says:** "more consistent with hedonic decline than with sunk-cost effects or motivational mean reversion"
- **Problem:** One of the two ruled-out explanations (motivational mean reversion) is omitted.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add "rather than sunk-cost effects or motivational mean reversion.""

### Study 18: Customers can tell when a company is still blaming the crisis -- and they call it opportunism
**Work ID:** W7203694398 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Propositional mechanism stated as established** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "four frontline mismatches (rule coherence, motive, capability versus promise, and access fairness) that drive attributions about a company's motives, competence and fairness"
- **Abstract says:** "we identify four corresponding frontline mismatches ... that help explain how crisis enactments may be interpreted through specific consumer attributions and emotional responses ... we advance new, mechanism-based propositions on how specific frontline missteps are associated with motive, competence, and fairness attributions"
- **Problem:** The abstract frames the mismatch-to-attribution link as untested propositions and associations; "drive" implies a demonstrated causal mechanism.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "mismatches the authors propose are associated with attributions about motives, competence and fairness.""

**Issue B. Hedge dropped in Why it matters** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "accumulate into trust and legitimacy damage once customers conclude the crisis is an excuse rather than a cause"
- **Abstract says:** "These micro-level mismatches may accumulate into macro-level risks to trust and legitimacy."
- **Problem:** The abstract's conditional "may accumulate into ... risks" becomes a stated outcome, which also sits at odds with the digest's own caveat that the mechanisms are propositions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "may accumulate into risks to trust and legitimacy.""
