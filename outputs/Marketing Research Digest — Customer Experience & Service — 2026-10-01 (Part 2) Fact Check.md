### Study 1: Making AI avatars look human matters less than making them useful
**Work ID:** W7204579696 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Dropped core condition in fsQCA result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "In the configurational analysis, capability and social influence emerged as core conditions for a strong shared experience, with lifelike appearance only a peripheral one."
- **Abstract says:** "Solo online customer experience, intelligent service capability and social influence all serve as core conditions, while sensory anthropomorphism functions merely as a peripheral condition."
- **Problem:** The abstract names three core conditions, including solo online customer experience; the digest lists only two, which slightly changes the picture of what drives shared experience.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add solo customer experience to the list of core conditions, e.g. "solo experience, capability and social influence emerged as core conditions.""

**Issue B. Unsupported definition of shared experience** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "shared (socially shared, co-viewed) customer experience"
- **Abstract says:** "shared online customer experience"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not define shared online customer experience as "co-viewed"; this operational gloss cannot be verified from the abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the parenthetical or label it as the digest's interpretation."

### Study 2: When an AI agent does your shopping, trust in the bot becomes trust in the product
**Work ID:** W7203638544 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderator re-labelled** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "moderated by how much the consumer generally trusts algorithms and by what kind of product was recommended"
- **Abstract says:** "with customers' tendencies towards algorithm use and recommendation target types acting as boundary conditions"
- **Problem:** The abstract describes a tendency towards algorithm use (propensity to rely on algorithms), not generalized trust in algorithms; these are related but distinct constructs.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rephrase as "how inclined the consumer is to use algorithms" to match the abstract."

### Study 5: Why the same in-store technology delights shoppers in one setting and flops in another
**Work ID:** W7213352682 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Overstated dismissal of technology** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "The upshot: context and surrounding actors, not the technology itself, drive whether smart retail lands."
- **Abstract says:** "This study investigates how customer experiences in smart retail are shaped not only by technological innovation but also by the contextual actors embedded in the service ecosystem."
- **Problem:** The abstract positions context as an addition to technological innovation, not a replacement for it; the digest reverses this into a claim that technology does not matter, which could misdirect investment decisions.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "context and surrounding actors, as well as the technology itself, shape whether smart retail lands.""

**Issue B. Sample size characterization** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Small-scale qualitative interview study in two retail settings only"
- **Abstract says:** "we conducted in-depth interviews in two smart retail settings"
- **Problem:** The abstract gives no number of interviews, so "small-scale" is unverifiable from abstract only (the two-setting point is correct).
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "qualitative interview study in two retail settings; number of interviews not reported in the abstract.""

**Issue C. Strength of mechanism claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "They isolated four underlying mechanisms"
- **Abstract says:** "uncovered four generative mechanisms – goal congruence, task fitness, brand synergy, and social consonance – that may explain variations in customer perceptions"
- **Problem:** The abstract hedges with "may explain"; "isolated" implies a firmer identification than claimed.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "proposed four generative mechanisms that may explain the variation.""

### Study 6: AI at the front line: the employees who shape the tools go above and beyond
**Work ID:** W7213983383 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Consumer angle contradicts main effect** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "the employees doing the best service work here weren't the heaviest AI users — they were the ones who had figured out where the tools help and where to deliberately switch them off"
- **Abstract says:** "The results show that both the depth and breadth of AI assimilation positively influence proactive service behavior."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that deeper and broader AI assimilation is associated with more proactive service behavior; the digest tells general readers the opposite, that heavy users were not the best performers. The balance result concerns crafting strategies, not level of use.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "employees who used AI more extensively were rated as more proactive — especially when they both expanded and deliberately bounded their use.""

**Issue B. Dropped mediation boundary** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the mechanism was "AI crafting" — actively reshaping how they used the tools"
- **Abstract says:** "Approach AI crafting mediates both relationships, while avoidance AI crafting only mediates the relationship between the breadth of AI assimilation and proactive service behavior."
- **Problem:** The digest collapses two distinct mediators and omits that avoidance crafting mediated only the breadth pathway.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that approach crafting mediated both depth and breadth effects, while avoidance crafting mediated only the breadth effect."

**Issue C. Who rated proactive behavior** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "how managers rated each employee's proactive service behavior"
- **Abstract says:** "paired-sample survey data from 234 employee-manager pairs in China's service industry"
- **Problem:** The abstract confirms paired employee-manager data but does not state that managers rated proactive service behavior; unverifiable from abstract only.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "paired employee-manager survey data" without specifying which party rated the outcome."

### Study 7: Virtual try-on is becoming a mirror — and over-flattering filters will cost you
**Work ID:** W7203598766 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts untested outcome** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "over-flattering filters will cost you"
- **Abstract says:** "the brand should avoid manipulation and over-glamorisation of the AR images to avoid customer dissatisfaction when they recreate the experience with actual products purchased after the virtual trials"
- **Problem:** This is the authors' practical recommendation, not a tested effect on returns or sales; the headline states it as an established cost.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "and the authors warn over-flattering filters may backfire.""

**Issue B. Moderation framed as dependency** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the self-extension experience depended on consumers believing the AR image was true to life"
- **Abstract says:** "Perceived realism moderates the relationship between perceived augmentation and self-extension experience."
- **Problem:** A moderation means the strength of the relationship varies with realism, not that the experience is conditional on belief in realism.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "the link to self-extension was stronger when consumers perceived the AR image as realistic.""

### Study 8: Custom work deals make salespeople better at rescuing unhappy customers
**Work ID:** W7203686382 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Omitted null moderation** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a manager's trust strengthened the link between polychronicity and deep acting"
- **Abstract says:** "manager trust strengthens the positive relationship between salesperson polychronicity and deep acting, but does not significantly moderate the relationship between polychronicity and surface acting"
- **Problem:** The non-significant moderation on surface acting is dropped, which could imply manager trust works on both emotional-labor routes.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add that manager trust did not significantly moderate the polychronicity–surface acting link."

**Issue B. Causal headline for survey data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Custom work deals make salespeople better at rescuing unhappy customers"
- **Abstract says:** "Using survey data collected from 348 salesperson–manager dyads and analyzed via SmartPLS 4."
- **Problem:** "Make" is causal language for a cross-sectional survey; the body and caveats correctly hedge, so the headline is out of step.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reword to "Custom work deals are linked to better service recovery by salespeople.""

### Study 9: Older guests judge hotel robots on trust, not on how good the tech is
**Work ID:** W7204434854 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Null result over-extended** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Older guests judge hotel robots on trust, not on how good the tech is"
- **Abstract says:** "Findings show that information quality and system quality do not directly influence senior consumers' perceived privacy protection. However, perceived trust and satisfaction positively impact hotel brand image."
- **Problem:** The reported null concerns the path from information/system quality to perceived privacy protection only. The abstract does not say technical quality fails to shape trust, satisfaction or brand image, so the headline and the "why it matters" claim that brand benefit flows through trust "rather than feature quality" go beyond the evidence.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Information and system quality did not directly drive seniors' sense of privacy protection, while trust and satisfaction were positively linked to brand image.""

**Issue B. Service quality omitted from model description** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "tested an Information Systems Success model using PLS-SEM"
- **Abstract says:** "this study examines the impact of information quality, system quality, and service quality on senior consumers' perceived privacy protection, trust, satisfaction, and hotel brand image"
- **Problem:** The digest names only information and system quality; service quality, a third antecedent in the tested model, is not mentioned, which makes the null look broader than it is.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that the model also included service quality, whose results are not reported in the abstract."

### Study 10: Shoppers don't reward green talk — and heavy discounts are where sustainability claims pile up
**Work ID:** W7203821753 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Deployment readiness overstated** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "automated detection tools capable of spotting claim–experience mismatches are now accurate enough for platforms or regulators to deploy at scale"
- **Abstract says:** "The proposed multimodal detection pipeline achieves an overall accuracy of 81.6 % on the Amazon hold-out set and 97 % on the BaSalam dataset, suggesting its potential usefulness for real-time consumer-facing and regulatory screening contexts."
- **Problem:** The abstract claims potential usefulness; the digest converts this into readiness for at-scale deployment by platforms and regulators.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "the authors suggest the pipeline could be useful for consumer-facing or regulatory screening.""

**Issue B. Generalization from two platforms** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "and this analysis suggests that's true of shoppers generally"
- **Abstract says:** "the study analyzes data from two large-scale e-commerce platforms: The Amazon Sales Dataset (1465 product listings) and the BaSalam Persian-language marketplace (182,275 product-review pairs)"
- **Problem:** The result comes from review text on two specific platforms (one of them a Persian-language marketplace) and is extended to shoppers in general; the digest's own caveat contradicts this line.
- **Suggested fix:** "Limit to "reviewers on these two platforms wrote mostly about performance, usability and value.""

### Study 11: Inside the Facebook groups where a drug reformulation turned patients into activists
**Work ID:** W7208731674 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Association reported as action** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "and those emotions translated into information sharing, collective mobilization, boycotts, formal complaints and heightened medical vigilance"
- **Abstract says:** "Emotions were associated with behavioral intentions such as information sharing, community mobilization, boycotts, complaints and medical vigilance."
- **Problem:** "Translated into" implies a causal sequence producing real-world acts, whereas the abstract reports an association with self-reported behavioral intentions expressed in posts. The digest's own caveat flags intentions-not-behavior, so the body text works against it.
- **Suggested fix:** "Rewrite as "and those emotions were associated with stated intentions to share information, mobilise collectively, boycott, complain and stay medically vigilant.""

**Issue B. Emotion targets attributed** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Patients voiced both gratitude, hope and joy toward each other and anger, anxiety and hatred toward institutions"
- **Abstract says:** "Patients expressed both positive emotions (gratitude, hope, joy) and negative emotions (anger, anxiety, sadness, hatred, disappointment), reflecting both community support and a loss of trust in the laboratory, health authorities, and healthcare professionals."
- **Problem:** The abstract does not say the positive emotions were directed at fellow patients and the negative ones at institutions; it says the emotions reflected community support and loss of trust. The targeting is an inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say the emotions "reflected both mutual support within the community and a loss of trust in the manufacturer, health authorities and healthcare professionals.""

### Study 12: Happy reviews get social credit; angry reviews get believed
**Work ID:** W7214683938 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Superlative beyond the data** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the warm, chatty five-star posts you enjoy most are precisely the ones other readers rate as least useful"
- **Abstract says:** "positive sentiment is associated with greater social value but lower informational value, whereas negative sentiment shows the opposite pattern"
- **Problem:** "Precisely the ones ... least useful" turns a directional association between sentiment and Useful votes into a categorical ranking of specific reviews; the abstract reports relative, average associations, not that such reviews are the least useful.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "the warm, chatty five-star posts tend on average to attract fewer 'Useful' votes, while complaints attract more.""

**Issue B. Predictive wording for correlational analysis** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Positive sentiment predicted more "Cool" votes (a marker of social value) but fewer "Useful" votes"
- **Abstract says:** "we examine how sentiment polarity and social language are associated with social engagement, measured through Cool votes"
- **Problem:** The abstract consistently uses "associated with"; "predicted" nudges towards a causal/predictive reading, though the caveats do note the analysis is correlational.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "was associated with" in place of "predicted" in the study summary."

### Study 13: People behave worse toward chatbots because they don't feel watched
**Work ID:** W7202155921 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Specific behaviors not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "increased unethical behavior such as inflated or dishonest claims"
- **Abstract says:** "interacting with AI (vs. human) service agents reduces perceived social presence and monitoring, lowering moral constraints and increasing unethical behavior"
- **Problem:** The abstract refers generically to "unethical behavior" in post-service contexts; it does not specify claim inflation or dishonest claims as the measured outcome.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "increased unethical behaviour in the post-service interaction" and note that the abstract does not specify the exact behaviours measured."

**Issue B. Unsupported mechanism claim** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the experiments suggest many people stretch the truth without quite noticing they are doing it"
- **Abstract says:** "reduces perceived social presence and monitoring, lowering moral constraints and increasing unethical behavior"
- **Problem:** Nothing in the abstract addresses awareness or non-awareness of the dishonesty; lowered moral constraint is not the same as acting unknowingly.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop "without quite noticing they are doing it" or replace with "when the sense of being observed drops away.""
