### Study 1: The promotion that offers no discount — and still moves product
**Work ID:** W7215933723 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Unsupported effect-size comparison** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "a well-framed appeal to "people like us" can be as motivating as 20% off"
- **Abstract says:** "Symbolic promotions ... create value by reinforcing consumers' social identity through shared group membership rather than by providing economic incentives ... generates a substantial increase in sales"
- **Problem:** The abstract never benchmarks the symbolic promotion against a monetary discount of any size; the "20% off" equivalence is invented and reads as a reported effect size.
- **Suggested fix:** "Drop the number: "a well-framed appeal to 'people like us' can motivate purchase without any price cut at all.""

**Issue B. "Brands" rendered as "authors and publishers"** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "with bigger gains for lesser-known authors and publishers than for established names"
- **Abstract says:** "with stronger effects for lesser-known brands than for well-known brands"
- **Problem:** The abstract says "brands"; whether the brand unit was author, publisher or both is not stated.
- **Suggested fix:** "Write "bigger gains for lesser-known brands than well-known ones (the abstract does not specify author vs. publisher).""

### Study 8: Half-leaving a sanctioned market may be worse for your reputation than staying put is good for it
**Work ID:** W4414312759 | **Verdict:** ❌ Significant issues

**Issue A. Headline asserts an unreported ranking** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Half-leaving a sanctioned market may be worse for your reputation than staying put is good for it"
- **Abstract says:** "home-country consumers react more positively to firms that pursue full exit from a sanctioned market than to those that adopt partial or no exit strategies"
- **Problem:** The abstract groups partial and no exit together as inferior to full exit; it does not say partial exit is judged worse than no exit. The headline invites the opposite operational conclusion (that staying beats hedging).
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe as "Full exit from a sanctioned market wins more consumer goodwill at home than half-measures — or staying.""

**Issue B. Mechanism not in the abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the research shows they read corporate ambiguity as evasion"
- **Abstract says:** "we find that home-country consumers react more positively to firms that pursue full exit ... Individual-level differences in susceptibility to normative influence, cognitive empathy, individualism, and ethical views on the use of sanctions shape these evaluations."
- **Problem:** No interpretive mechanism such as "ambiguity read as evasion" is reported in the abstract; it is the digest's inference.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "consumers reward a clean break more than a partial withdrawal" without asserting the underlying interpretation."

**Issue C. Strength of moderation overstated** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the research also shows judgments vary sharply by consumer values"
- **Abstract says:** "Individual-level differences in susceptibility to normative influence, cognitive empathy, individualism, and ethical views on the use of sanctions shape these evaluations."
- **Problem:** "Shape" is reported without magnitude; "vary sharply" adds an effect-size claim the abstract does not support.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "judgments vary with consumer values.""

### Study 9: Why crisis playbooks break down in practice: lawyers, nervous clients and the clock
**Work ID:** W7203759226 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

- **Abstract says:** "Legal advice, client risk aversion and media pressures frequently disrupt ideal sequencing, suggesting that deviations from SCCT prescriptions may result from structural constraints rather than practitioner neglect."

### Study 11: Not all opposition is persuadable: eight 'ethical personas' behind community resistance to offshore wind
**Work ID:** W7214777517 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Roundtables characterized as expert panels** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "persona framework is interpretive coding validated by expert roundtables rather than tested against behavioral outcomes"
- **Abstract says:** "Eight empirically grounded personas were developed and validated through two participatory roundtables... a replicable, multi-method persona development methodology that combines corpus appraisal analysis with participatory stakeholder validation."
- **Problem:** The abstract describes participatory stakeholder roundtables, which may include community members and practitioners, not an expert panel specifically.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "expert roundtables" to "two participatory stakeholder roundtables"."

**Issue B. Jurisdiction claim not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "single infrastructure category and jurisdiction"
- **Abstract says:** "The abstract names offshore wind as the context but does not identify a country or jurisdiction for the submissions and social media data."
- **Problem:** Unverifiable from abstract only; the abstract never states the jurisdiction studied.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say "single infrastructure category; jurisdiction not specified in the abstract" or drop the jurisdiction reference."

### Study 12: Wine and weed: German drinkers who use cannabis often use both at once — and motive predicts which
**Work ID:** W7214712049 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Complementarity generalized beyond cannabis users** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "this suggests the relationship is more complementary than substitutive, and that occasion motive — not category — is the useful segmentation variable"
- **Abstract says:** "Individuals consuming cannabis frequently tend to use both substances together, indicating complementarity for cannabis users. However, wine consumption frequency alone does not determine simultaneous or separate cannabis use."
- **Problem:** The abstract restricts the complementarity conclusion to frequent cannabis users within a sample of wine drinkers; stating the relationship is complementary in general goes slightly further than the data reported.
- **Suggested fix:** "Qualify as "among frequent cannabis users, the relationship looks complementary rather than substitutive"."

### Study 13: Why free pads don't get used: stigma, not access, is regulating the menstrual products market
**Work ID:** W7214428578 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. "Free pads" framing not in the study** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Why free pads don't get used: stigma, not access, is regulating the menstrual products market"
- **Abstract says:** "This study highlights why showing that access alone does not translate into adoption when cultural meanings and social expectations remain unaddressed."
- **Problem:** The abstract makes a general point that access alone is insufficient; it says nothing about free-distribution programs or unused free pads.
- **Suggested fix:** "Reframe to "Why access alone doesn't drive adoption: stigma as informal market regulation"."

**Issue B. Over-generalization of a 25-interview qualitative study** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "A reframing of trial barriers that generalizes beyond this category: when switching carries social or identity risk, awareness campaigns and price promotions won't move adoption."
- **Abstract says:** "An interpretive qualitative approach ... twenty-five in-depth interviews with women aged 18–45 ... in India ... At the upstream level, policy measures should address affordability through subsidies."
- **Problem:** The abstract offers an interpretive, India-specific account and still recommends affordability measures; claiming the pattern generalizes to other categories and that price promotions "won't move adoption" overstates what the study tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "a possible reframing of trial barriers worth testing elsewhere: where use is socially visible, awareness and price alone may not be enough — the authors still recommend affordability measures alongside normalisation"."
