### Study 1: Young adults will take a mortgage but won't borrow from mum: mapping what debt they refuse
**Work ID:** W7214655781 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Recruitment detail not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "self-selected online respondents."
- **Abstract says:** "a cross-sectional survey of 995 respondents spanning 14 debt products"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe the survey mode or recruitment, so "self-selected online respondents" cannot be verified from the abstract alone (it is an added, not an overstated, limitation).
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "survey respondents in two Anglosphere countries; recruitment details not reported in the abstract" or drop the mode descriptor."

### Study 3: Two different forces drive impulse buying in livestream auctions: love for the host, and envy of the winner
**Work ID:** W7214649704 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interviews described as confirming the quantitative model** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "The interviews confirmed that attachment to the host and comparison with winning rivals were experienced as distinct things, not two faces of the same feeling."
- **Abstract says:** "Interview accounts distinguished continuing host attachment from comparisons with successful viewers and documented improvement motives both with and without immediate purchase urge."
- **Problem:** The abstract says the interviews distinguished the two experiences and documented variation (including improvement motives without purchase urge); "confirmed" implies corroboration of the statistical model rather than descriptive differentiation.
- **Suggested fix:** "Use "Interview accounts distinguished ongoing attachment to the host from comparisons with successful bidders" and note that improvement motives appeared both with and without an immediate urge to buy."

### Study 4: When AI gives you financial advice, explaining yourself works on feelings, not logic
**Work ID:** W7204478707 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Moderators dropped** — Severity: Moderate

- **As written:** "Transparency was associated with lower cognitive load, which in turn raised both perceived usefulness and psychological comfort, but only psychological comfort predicted financial well-being; the "useful" route did not."
- **Abstract says:** "Moderation analyses indicate that FRT conditions the CL→PU and CL→PC links, whereas STHA conditions the AT→FWB, CL→PU, and CL→PC links."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports that financial risk tolerance and suspicion toward human advisors condition several of these paths; presenting the pathway without those boundary conditions makes it look more uniform across users than the paper claims.
- **Suggested fix:** "Add a clause noting the paths were conditioned by users' financial risk tolerance and their suspicion of human advisors."

**Issue B. Direct transparency effect omitted** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "the explainable-AI industry largely measures success by whether users understand the model; this suggests what actually links to well-being is whether disclosure makes users feel reassured"
- **Abstract says:** "AT enhances FWB both directly and indirectly by reducing CL, which in turn strengthens both PU and PC."
- **Problem:** The abstract reports a direct association between algorithmic transparency and financial well-being in addition to the comfort route; the digest implies the affective route is the only link.
- **Suggested fix:** "Note that transparency was also directly associated with financial well-being, with the comfort route the only significant mediated path."

### Study 5: When Tech in Stores Pays Off: It Depends on Whether the Experience "Feels Right"
**Work ID:** W7215084470 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Technology examples not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "digital layers such as augmented reality displays and immersive in-store technology grafted onto physical shops"
- **Abstract says:** "The integration of digital technologies into physical retail has given rise to phygital experiences … immersive continuity and interactive augmentation"
- **Problem:** The abstract names only the constructs "immersive continuity" and "interactive augmentation"; augmented reality displays are the digest's own example and cannot be verified from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe the constructs as the paper does, or label AR as an illustrative example rather than something the study tested."

**Issue B. Regulatory mode sub-types not specified in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "whether the experience matched a shopper's motivational mode (those who like to assess and compare versus those who like to move and act)"
- **Abstract says:** "their impact depends on alignment with consumers' regulatory mode, which determines the extent to which the experience is perceived as "feeling right.""
- **Problem:** The abstract refers to "regulatory mode" without naming assessment and locomotion orientations; the gloss is standard theory but is not verifiable from the abstract.
- **Suggested fix:** "Flag the assessment/locomotion split as the standard definition of regulatory mode rather than as reported study detail."

### Study 6: Luxury Brands in Thailand: Emotional Storytelling Beats Chasing Trends
**Work ID:** W7203581812 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Analytic method loosely described** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "analyzed the data with both conventional path modeling and configurational methods (necessary condition analysis and fsQCA)"
- **Abstract says:** "analysed using integrated generalised structured component analysis (IGSCA) for symmetrical paths and necessary condition analysis (NCA) and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) for asymmetrical logic"
- **Problem:** The symmetrical analysis was IGSCA, a specific component-based SEM, not generic "conventional path modeling"; NCA is also a necessity analysis rather than a configurational method in the fsQCA sense.
- **Suggested fix:** "Name IGSCA for the path estimates and describe NCA and fsQCA as necessity and configurational analyses respectively."

**Issue B. NCA conclusion reworded** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Interaction and trendiness showed only small-to-moderate effects and are better understood as baseline requirements than as loyalty drivers."
- **Abstract says:** "effect sizes are small to moderate (d = 0.090–0.234), so interaction and trendiness are better read as baseline enablers of loyalty than as strict necessary conditions"
- **Problem:** The abstract's contrast is "baseline enablers" versus "strict necessary conditions" in the NCA sense, not versus "loyalty drivers"; the rewording shifts what was tested.
- **Suggested fix:** "Say they are better read as baseline enablers than as strict necessary conditions for loyalty."

### Study 7: Which "Green" Cryptocurrencies Spread Panic, and Which Just Catch It
**Work ID:** W7203939686 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Strength of contemporaneous-vs-lagged contrast** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "Same-day connectedness far exceeded lagged connectedness, indicating that shocks propagate almost immediately rather than over days."
- **Abstract says:** "Contemporaneous connectedness is more pronounced than lagged connectedness, indicating rapid information diffusion within cryptocurrency platforms."
- **Problem:** "Far exceeded" quantifies a gap the abstract describes only as "more pronounced"; no magnitude is given.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change to "Same-day connectedness was more pronounced than lagged connectedness.""

### Study 8: Inside the Medicines Business: Experts Describe How Pharmacy Commerce Collides With Indigenous Health in Australia
**Work ID:** W7211924177 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Characterization of partnership views** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "participants distinguished sharply between genuine collaboration and relationships that extract legitimacy"
- **Abstract says:** "pharmaceutical industry efforts to partner with Aboriginal organizations; … and opportunities for genuine collaboration"

### Study 9: Italians stopped saving for their heirs — and started saving for themselves
**Work ID:** W7215076186 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Causal verb for a regression result** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "A multinomial logit model indicates that both structural economic conditions and individual financial-planning orientation drive the differences."
- **Abstract says:** "reveals that structural economic channels and individual financial planning orientations jointly explain saving motive heterogeneity"
- **Problem:** "Drive" implies causation from an observational multinomial logit on survey data; the abstract says these factors jointly explain heterogeneity.
- **Suggested fix:** "Change "drive the differences" to "jointly explain the differences.""

**Issue B. Survey characterization not in abstract** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "a nationally representative government survey"
- **Abstract says:** "six waves of the Italian Survey of Household Income and Wealth (SHIW) spanning 2012 to 2022"
- **Problem:** The abstract does not describe SHIW as nationally representative or as a government survey; unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Suggested fix:** "Describe it simply as the Italian Survey of Household Income and Wealth, six waves, 2012–2022."

### Study 10: Those "impulse" Shein hauls are actually carefully planned
**Work ID:** W7213342854 | **Verdict:** ⚠️ Minor issues

**Issue A. Interpretive leap in Why it matters** — Severity: Minor

- **As written:** "which means the saved cart and wish-list are the real conversion assets, not the surprise discovery. It also explains, concretely, why surveys showing high sustainability concern keep failing to predict sales."
- **Abstract says:** "Participants frequently described their purchases as spontaneous. However, their accounts also revealed evidence of planning … This pattern is conceptualised as strategic spontaneity … The findings also suggest that consumers manage sustainability concerns through cognitive buffering"
- **Problem:** An exploratory interview study with 20 participants describes a pattern; it does not establish which assets convert, nor does it "explain, concretely" the aggregate value-action gap in sales data. The abstract itself uses "suggest."
- **Suggested fix:** "Soften to "suggests that saved carts and wish-lists may matter more than surprise discovery, and offers one account of why stated sustainability concern does not track purchasing.""
