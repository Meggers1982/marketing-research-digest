# Topic Memory: Branding
_Last updated: 2026-10-10_

## Established findings
- Perceived motive governs persuasion: modest claims beat boastful ones via lower skepticism (W7203440056, 2026-08), commercially motivated testimonials depress repurchase (W7216479640, 2026-10), AI-authored activism reads as inauthentic (W7203997372, 2026-08), AI-designed products accepted only for passion-driven brands (W7202384182, 2026-08).
- Brand positioning (luxury vs. mass, premium vs. budget) flips the sign of the same creative lever: indulgent imagery (W7212008823), boastful copy (W7203440056), symbolic packaging design (W7220832664).
- Values-based crises outweigh competence failures for boycotts and brand hate, with spillover onto sponsors and endorsers (W7221118815, 2026-10); defectors from activism backlash act retaliatively (W7204024551, 2026-08).
- Cultural/individual-difference moderators (power distance, uncertainty avoidance, epistemic motives) repeatedly determine which brand signal works: W7220958293, W7221048994, W7203651996.

## Emerging threads
- AI as a brand-equity risk surface rather than an efficiency story: service-bot failures damaging consumer-brand relationships (W7219659105), human/AI task-risk workflow splits (W7214780511), disclosure-dependent acceptance (W7202384182, W7203997372).
- Consumer fatigue as a structural condition, not a mood: co-branding backlash in saturated markets (W7221096517), unresolvable social media ambivalence (W7220944415).
- Internal/production-side determinants of brand output — creatives' private brand attitudes (W7221130306), level-split internal rebranding buy-in (W7220855662).
- Methodological push to re-examine promotion ROI assumptions; dynamic models suggest discounts are more incremental and less switch-driven than standard models imply (W7220913319).
- Image advertising questioned as crisis armor; satisfaction and brand love carry protection (W7214968251).

## Feature ideas already pitched
- 2026-10-10: "The economics of sincerity" — consumers infer brand motive and discount messages accordingly, timely because of AI disclosure rules and endorsement/fake-review crackdowns (W7203440056, W7216479640, W7203997372, W7202384182, W7221130306). Suggested outlets: Marketing Week, Digiday, MIT SMR, The Atlantic.
- Not yet pitched, held in reserve: the "backlash economy" angle (co-branding fatigue, values-crisis boycotts, retaliatory defection, platform ambivalence).
