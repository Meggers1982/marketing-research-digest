# Topic Memory: AI & Marketing Technology
_Last updated: 2026-09-14_

## Established findings
- Anthropomorphism in AI interfaces has a ceiling and backfire points: name repetition (W7204492734, 2026-08), human-like bots after process failures (W7208749448, 2026-09), memory features read as "uncomfortably human" (W7212274308, 2026-09).
- Machine-ness is sometimes an advantage: bots explaining unfavorable recovery decisions (W7204096810), robot-delivered perks that disadvantage others (W7203703034), conversational AI negatively linked to compulsive buying (W7207605884).
- Virtual influencers trade novelty for credibility, and the novelty decays: meta-analysis of 210 studies (W7171554746, 2026-07) plus production- vs enjoyment-framing evidence (W7203949248, 2026-08).
- Generative personalization of copy underdelivers on behavior: LLM style-transfer added no behavioral gain over generic daily messages in a preregistered RCT (W7114890653, 2026-09).
- AI in judgment-heavy or premium contexts pays off only indirectly or conditionally: bank copilot cut approvals, satisfaction and autonomy (W7203942746); luxury AI had no direct brand-performance effect (W7212369437).

## Emerging threads
- Automation reshapes the top of the funnel, not just decisions: weak applicants self-select out of algorithmic evaluation (W7204140072) — watch for field replication.
- Selection, not production, as the new martech bottleneck: causal screening of AI-generated content against campaign history (W7172334002).
- Skills over seats in creative AI rollouts: only prompt-trained professionals produced more ideas, with no creativity gain (W7204938023).
- Language-as-signal analytics maturing: past-focused language predicting B2B defection (W7204117449), counterfactual phrasing in negative reviews (W7203674935), profanity penalties with a gender asymmetry (W7170627554).
- Personalization as memory technology with a dual moral valence (W7204874545); open-model likeness/NSFW ecosystems as an unmonitored brand-safety surface (W4416014100).
