# Topic Memory: Advertising
_Last updated: 2026-09-25_

## Established findings
- Assertive/commanding ad copy reliably triggers reactance and slightly reduces persuasion, worst in nonprofit contexts (W7214238772, 2026-09).
- Mobile ad value rests on perceived usefulness and absence of irritation; social norms barely matter; personalization, permission and in-app formats amplify (W7204247420, 2026-08).
- Green claims lift evaluations overall but yield less for already-green brands; conventional brands gain from rational, evidence-backed messaging (W7202096496, 2026-08).
- Disclosure of sponsorship is not an engagement penalty and may be an asset; covert sponsorship underperforms, especially for mega-influencers (W7213758328, 2026-09).
- Influencer effects are asymmetric: negative "de-influencing" moves brand attitudes more than endorsement, with no reputational payoff to the creator (W7202169140, 2026-08).

## Emerging threads
- Consumer response to AI-generated advertising hinges on plausibility and perceived corporate intent, not authorship; labeled AI ads can outperform for utilitarian products (W7204147563, W7214074450, W7203605245, W7213402036, W7213453650 — all 2026 Q3).
- Field-experiment evidence that branded search spend may be largely incremental-free, at least for small DTC advertisers (W7204491303, 2026-08) — watch for replications at scale.
- Platform choice as structural market force (search consolidates toward national chains, social disperses toward local) (W7214082546, 2026-09).
- Acquisition advertising producing downstream retention effects, blurring acquisition/re-engagement budget lines (W7212164169, 2026-09).
- Economic precarity reshaping luxury cues: wealth-coded messaging repels, craft-coded holds (W7213527998, 2026-09).
- Creator-economy segmentation: briefs should vary by influencer tier, species (pet accounts) and sensory category (W7203570653, W7214117679, W7213402036).
- Commercial capture of health information hashtags as a pre-regulatory signal (W7214105022, 2026-09).
