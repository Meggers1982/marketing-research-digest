# Topic Memory: Pricing & Promotions
_Last updated: 2026-09-15_

## Established findings
- Reference prices dominate paid prices: a remembered or displayed "was" price reshapes perceived value and demand even when unusable (W7207565403, W7172409631, 2026-08/09).
- Pain of paying survives digital wallets — cash hurts more than card across 13 preregistered replications, n=32,371, strongest among habitual card users (W7203715795, 2026-08).
- Under price pressure, brand switching (not basket cutting) is consumers' first response; core-food cuts concentrate in food-insecure households (W7204997399, 2026-09).
- Framing and choice architecture move purchase behavior at low cost: markdown date-vs-delay wording (W7204248241), pre-selected defaults (W7160239758), activation timing (W7170377972).

## Emerging threads
- Price tier as a moderator of messaging: positioning claims lose force at premium prices (W7203839485); framing advantage vanishes at deep discounts (W7204248241).
- Signals that backfire — voluntary guarantees read as weakness (W4375826978), staff discounts crowding out goodwill (W7170087457).
- Equity claims for behavioral interventions weakening: defaults help all income groups equally (W7160239758); financial literacy only moves comparison behavior (W7212374919).
- Voice/audio as a new inference layer in pricing and promotion targeting (W7210283217) — privacy angle likely to recur.
- Regional/platform pricing as a fairness story: some publishers price above platform guidance in lower-income markets (W7203929361).
- Green and health premiums are real but conditional on assortment placement and self-perceived expertise (W7203899003, W7170171784).
