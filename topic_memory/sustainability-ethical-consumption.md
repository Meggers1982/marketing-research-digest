# Topic Memory: Sustainability & Ethical Consumption
_Last updated: 2026-11-13_

## Established findings
- Sustainability claims act as amplifiers or tiebreakers, not drivers: they require pre-existing trust, familiarity or brand equity to pay off, and lose to sensory and functional attributes at the point of choice (W7224110232, W7225778945, W7225451567, Nov 2026; W7204076930, W7214091952, W7172534656, Aug–Sep 2026).
- Green attributes win stated approval but rarely a price premium; functional trust/safety attributes get paid for (W7204076930, W7172534656).
- Environmental motives are seldom the operative driver of "sustainable" purchases: status, identity signaling, taste, security and threshold hygiene factors dominate (W7225570405, W7225778945, Nov 2026; W7213254319, W7204951358, W7213931215, W7214159818, Sep 2026).
- Accurate climate knowledge does not produce acceptance in food; familiarity, satiety and sweetness govern (W7211939433, W7214091952, W7225778945).
- CSR's consumer payoff is indirect and contingent — on trust, governance and perceived sincerity — with no reliable direct effect on brand image (W7224110232, W7212036936, W7212064195, W7214208660).
- Audiences hold skepticism and engagement simultaneously, separating messenger from message and judging on content utility (W7224942359, Oct 2026; W7214725050, Sep 2026).
- Most evidence remains stated-preference, single-country and intention-based; WTP and willingness measures should be treated as hypothetical.

## Emerging threads
- Disclosure and its legibility as the financially rewarded asset: CSR reporting linked to firm value, stronger with a CMO present, while distinctive CSR prose signals symbolic rather than substantive commitment (W7225260260, W7225401845).
- Supply-chain sustainability as a marketing-adjacent topic: slack converting to resilience, and buyer green innovation lowering foreign suppliers' emissions where institutions align (W7223382174, W7225780096).
- Collective/industry-level sustainability failure modes — tensions blocking coalitions before a shared vision forms (W7223667129).
- Emotion-based de-consumption levers: awe routed through self-transcendence toward minimalism, as an alternative to guilt (W7225693773).
- Message–donor fit in repeat giving: scarcity framing works only on frugal identities; physiological state and other-benefit framing also moderate (W7225100539, W7215072413).
- Attribution split on the attitude–behavior gap now three-way: self-blame (W7211935268), system blame (W7213447462), and dissonance resolution that preserves loyalty (W7225451567).
- Identity-signaling versus authority appeals unresolved — expressive social signaling beat impact framing (W7225570405) while expert endorsement beat values-consistency (W7214115221).
- Service design as a producer of vulnerability for program beneficiaries; consumer labor and access equity (W7225785168, W7204439549).

## Feature ideas already pitched
- 2026-09-18: "The credit gap" — sustainability rewarded for legibility, not substance (W7212134127, W7204076930, W7212064195, W7170105320). Marketing Week, MIT SMR, The Grocer, Vox.
- 2026-10-02: "The last mile of the circular economy" — disposal, retention and resale as the failure point (W7214074641, W7214159818, W7213931215, W7172534656). Packaging Dive, Retail Dive, Grist, Marketing Brew.
- 2026-11-13: "Sustainability as multiplier, not motor" — green claims work only on top of trust, taste, identity or existing brand equity, and often as dissonance relief (W7224110232, W7225778945, W7223696417, W7225570405, W7225451567, W7225401845). The Drum, Vogue Business, MIT SMR, Vox.
