# Topic Memory: Sustainability & Ethical Consumption
_Last updated: 2026-09-18_

## Established findings
- Visible, voluntary, single-message sustainability signals earn consumer and stakeholder credit; invisible, mandated or mixed ones earn little or none (W7212134127, W7212064195, W7170105320; Sep 2026).
- Not all green claims pay: environmental/reuse claims command premiums while social-ethics claims (Fair Trade, community support) can carry negative willingness to pay in mainstream categories (W7204076930; Aug 2026).
- Sustainability framing is often not the actual purchase driver — status, brand access, curiosity and identity mediate instead (W7213254319, W7211939433, W7204951358; Sep 2026).
- CSR affects consumers indirectly via loyalty, perceived ethicality and authenticity signals rather than directly changing consumption (W7212036936; Sep 2026).
- Stated-preference choice experiments dominate this literature; nearly all WTP figures are hypothetical and single-country.

## Emerging threads
- Governance levers (ESG-linked executive pay) linked to lower reputational risk — an under-covered substance-versus-signal test (W7213035748).
- Dark side of community and commitment: ethical-consumption identity fatigue (W7211935268) and consumer entrapment in obligation-based communities (W7211969452).
- Cross-cultural measurement problems: standard food-motive instruments travel poorly, though convenience and sustainability replicate across seven countries (W7208710969).
- Trust as a brake, not a lubricant: high-trust B2B partnerships slow AI adoption for sustainable value creation (W7213367223).
- Crisis language as cover for permanent service reduction, read by customers as opportunism (W7203694398).
- Paywalls and digital tooling buying short-lived behavior change or negative returns when outpacing human capability (W4312971961, W7204753244).

## Feature ideas already pitched
- 2026-09-18: "The credit gap" — sustainability is rewarded for legibility, not substance; mandated disclosure and moralizing claims destroy value (W7212134127, W7204076930, W7212064195, W7170105320, W7213254319, W7204740815). Suggested Marketing Week, MIT SMR, The Grocer, Vox.
