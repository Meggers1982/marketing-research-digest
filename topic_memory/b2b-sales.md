# Topic Memory: B2B & Sales
_Last updated: 2026-09-21_

## Established findings
- Control-tightening in commercial relationships tends to backfire: activity-level sales control lowers meaningfulness and raises quit intent (W7211996947, 2026-09), detailed contracts amplify distributor opportunism when manipulation is perceived (W7208824278, 2026-09), hard-closing erodes buyer trust in matched dyads (W7172448511, 2026-08).
- Consultative/advisory selling builds trust and repurchase intent; competence-based (credibility) trust outweighs warmth-based (benevolence) trust for retention (W7172448511, 2026-08).
- Relationship continuity is a performance asset: stable account teams strengthen customer experience, while financial incentives/discounts dilute the value of supplier expertise (W7171153525, 2026-07).

## Emerging threads
- Models beat rep intuition on deal outcomes, with explainability driving adoption — expert judgment near coin-flip in one industrial case (W7204437044, 2026-08). Watch for replication beyond single-firm samples.
- Conversation analytics at the speaking-turn level: tactic effectiveness is phase-dependent, not fixed (W7203901346, 2026-08). Potential recurring "AI listens to sales calls" beat.
- Relational tolerance of dubious ESG claims inside procurement — greenwashing as a relationship-governance problem, not a disclosure problem (W7213538226, 2026-09).
- Employer prestige as a sales-performance lever, moderated by professional vs. organizational identification (W7202160517, 2026-08).
- Cross-border/traveler demand, not local sustainability-minded shoppers, driving Asia-Pacific luxury resale (W7204571119, 2026-08); circular-economy narrative may be overstated.
- E-commerce persuasion splits into cognitive (direct) and emotional (attitude-mediated) routes (W7204660594, 2026-08).
- Methodological caution: most B2B findings this run are cross-sectional self-report, single-country samples; the exceptions (matched dyads, time-lagged, transaction records, call recordings) are the stronger pitches.
